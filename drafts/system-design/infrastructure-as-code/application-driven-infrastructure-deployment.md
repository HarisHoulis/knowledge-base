# Application-Driven Infrastructure Deployment • Kief Morris • GOTO 2025

Kief Morris, a distinguished engineer at ThoughtWorks, frames the talk around infrastructure management for software delivery. He recalls the original promise of cloud and infrastructure as code: infrastructure on demand through APIs, without waiting on purchasing processes or data-center work, and code that would make environments consistent and reliable so applications would behave the same across environments (GOTO 2025).

In practice, Morris argues that this promise has only partly materialized. Deploying applications to development, test, staging, and production environments often takes too long and requires too much work; when something goes wrong, people must be brought in to investigate (GOTO 2025). He also describes a paradox: teams often lack enough environments for testing, while at the same time spending too much money on environments (GOTO 2025).

Infrastructure and platform people spend excessive time on routine tasks and firefighting, which is toil rather than value-adding work, leaving too little time to improve systems. They often have a list of improvements they would make if they had time, but are too busy with other work (GOTO 2025). Morris's focus is application-driven infrastructure deployment, with software teams including both developers and QAs as the beneficiaries (GOTO 2025).

- Cloud and infrastructure as code promised on-demand, consistent, and reliable infrastructure, but deployments across environments often remain slow and troublesome (GOTO 2025).
- Organizations face a paradox: not enough environments for testing, yet spending too much money on environments (GOTO 2025).
- Infrastructure/platform teams spend too much time on routine toil and firefighting, leaving little time for system improvements they know are needed (GOTO 2025).
- The talk advocates application-driven infrastructure deployment that serves software teams, including developers and QAs (GOTO 2025).
