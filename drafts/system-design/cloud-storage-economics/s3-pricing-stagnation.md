# S3 Is the Future, S3 Is the Past

A comment on the Hacker News discussion "S3 Is the Future, S3 Is the Past" observes that Amazon S3, which once saw relatively frequent price reductions, has not had a price drop in a full decade. The comment documents the historical price trajectory: from $0.150/GB-month at launch in March 2006, through successive cuts in 2010, 2012, 2014, and 2016, ending at $0.023/GB-month in December 2016. As of the time of writing, the price remains $0.023/GB-month, unchanged since that last reduction. The takeaway is that a service whose past was characterized by regular cost declines now appears frozen at its 2016 pricing, a notable shift in the economics of cloud storage.

- S3 launched at $0.150/GB-month in March 2006 and dropped in price several times over the following decade.
- The last recorded price cut was in December 2016, bringing the price to $0.023/GB-month.
- As of the comment, the price is still $0.023/GB-month, meaning no drop in a full decade.
- The stagnation contrasts with S3's earlier pattern of periodic price reductions.
