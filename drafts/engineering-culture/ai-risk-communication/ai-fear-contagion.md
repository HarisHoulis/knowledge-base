# The Contagion of Fear

In "The Contagion of Fear," Bryan Cantrill recounts youthful mistakes that caused unjustified panic among less technical peers and warns against repeating that pattern with AI. He criticizes ghoulish claims that leap into the mainstream through hand-wavy future extrapolation, citing Jacob Coxon's references to "hacking critical infrastructure" and "extinction-level bioweapons" without elaboration or relevant expertise (Willison, 2026).

Cantrill argues the public cannot be expected to understand LLMs, critical infrastructure, bioweapons, or extinction biology; that burden belongs to those making the claims. He says domain experts implicitly hold the public's trust through their expertise and must not abuse it, especially when raising alarms (Willison, 2026).

In a linked Oxide and Friends episode, Cantrill expands on his doubts about bioweapons concerns, urging that a biologist or someone with bioweapons experience weigh in. He says the bioweapon narrative "gets under my fingernails because it leaves so much to the imagination that we insert with fear" (Willison, 2026).

- AI doom claims often rely on speculative extrapolation rather than domain expertise.
- Experts must be circumspect and avoid abusing public trust, particularly when sounding alarms.
- The burden of proof for extraordinary AI harms lies with claimants, not the public.
- Bioweapon fears are especially susceptible to imagination-driven panic; actual domain experts should weigh in.
- Bryan Cantrill discusses these concerns with Simon Willison on Oxide and Friends.
