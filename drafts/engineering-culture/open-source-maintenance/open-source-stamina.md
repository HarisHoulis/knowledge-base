# Open Source Stamina

Kent C. Dodds argues that open source maintenance depends heavily on maintainer motivation and context. Contributors are often motivated by saving other developers time, seeing people use their work, enjoying API design, and using the libraries themselves; it is hard to stay motivated for something not used regularly, except when paid, as with his work at Alianza though he does not personally use Verizon phone service (source).

When maintainers stop using a tool often, they lose touch with common use cases and no longer know how, why, or in what way it is used. That can lead to building unwanted features or struggling to choose the right approach, which is demoralizing and drains stamina (source). Dodds says he maintains angular-formly because he uses it heavily at work and would likely seek someone else to take over if he stopped working with forms and/or Angular (source).

Handoffs do not mean maintainers stopped caring; TJ Holowaychuk still guides some old Node.js projects, and active involvement just becomes difficult (source). Dodds urges people to appreciate maintainers' donated time, help improve their stamina rather than deplete it, and not resent handoffs when motivating factors disappear (source).

- Open source motivation is tied to regular personal use, impact on other developers, enjoyment of API design, and paid support; without those, stamina fades.
- Maintainers who stop using a project lose context on real use cases and may spend time on features users do not care about.
- Handoffs, such as TJ Holowaychuk's Node.js projects and Karma, are often due to lost motivation or context, not lack of caring.
- Treat maintainers as humans donating free time: support their stamina and appreciate prior work when they hand off a project.
