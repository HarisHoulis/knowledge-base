# Giving Good Demos

Kent C. Dodds describes preparing a demo for paypal-scripts with Jamund Ferguson at PayPal, arguing that effective demos begin by establishing the problem the project solves. They explained that PayPal has thousands of configuration files for tools like ESLint, Babel, and Webpack, and estimated that reconfiguring tools costs over $1 million per year, making the need clear before showing the solution (Dodds, 2017).

He advises knowing the audience and speaking in terms they understand and care about. For their presentation, this meant framing the problem in terms of both developer productivity and money. He also recommends making things as quantitative as possible, using estimates to put the problem in perspective for people unfamiliar with the technical details (Dodds, 2017).

Dodds strongly recommends pre-recording demos. A failed live demo can ruin efforts to capture the audience, and recording lets you edit out fluff and respect the audience's time. His recorded paypal-scripts demo was 12 minutes, while the same demo live would have been more than twice as long. However, he notes you should still be able to deliver a live demo in case of questions or to verify the demo is not smoke and mirrors (Dodds, 2017).

Finally, he emphasizes practice. Running through the presentation beforehand helped them find technical difficulties and slide deficiencies, and because they had already done it, the actual presentation went well. He concludes that being a great developer is not enough if nobody knows the value you bring, so preparing well for demos maximizes the impact of your communication (Dodds, 2017).

- Start with "Why": establish the problem and its cost before demonstrating the solution.
- Know your audience and speak in their terms, including quantitative estimates such as money or productivity impact.
- Pre-record demos to avoid live failures, edit out rambling, and respect the audience's attention, while still being ready to demo live if needed.
- Practice the presentation beforehand to catch technical issues and improve slides and explanations.
