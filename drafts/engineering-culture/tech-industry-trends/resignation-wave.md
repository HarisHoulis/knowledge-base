# The Pulse: Meta’s self-inflicted resignation-wave

Meta's recent layoffs and forced reassignments have triggered a resignation wave among engineers who were not directly impacted. These employees are proactively seeking new opportunities due to the uncertainty and morale damage caused by the restructuring. In response, Meta is offering large equity retainers to retain talent, but this approach appears ineffective at stemming the outflow.

The situation highlights a broader cultural issue: even unaffected employees lose trust and motivation when management handles layoffs poorly. The equity packages fail to address the root causes of dissatisfaction, such as eroded psychological safety and perceived unfair treatment. This event serves as a case study in how restructuring decisions can backfire on company culture and retention.

- Meta's layoffs and forced reassignments have pushed unaffected engineers to look for new jobs.
- The company is offering large equity retainers, which are not working to stop the resignation wave.
- The wave demonstrates how restructuring can damage company culture and trigger voluntary attrition.
- Equity incentives are insufficient to retain talent when trust and psychological safety are eroded.
