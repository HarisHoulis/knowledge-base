# How Gratitude can make you a better developer

The post uses Thanksgiving as a prompt to argue that gratitude and attitude can improve both software engineering work and personal happiness [1]. It recounts a presentation by the author's brother showing that while circumstances are often outside our control, our thoughts about them are not: positive thoughts tend to lead to positive feelings, actions, and results, while negative thoughts tend to spiral downward [1].

A contrived developer example illustrates this: when a product manager reassigns you from GraphQL experimentation to a critical business requirement, resentment can produce cold behavior, shortcuts, and poor code, whereas a positive attitude can produce a well-tested solution and eventually a return to the original work [1]. The author cautions that positive results do not guarantee outcomes like a promotion, but attitude and thoughts remain within your control [1].

The post then cites a video claiming science shows gratitude makes people happier and shares a "Happiness Invitation": journal for one week, five minutes each night, listing things you are grateful for; reflect on who gave those things and express sincere gratitude to someone; then write down how you feel afterward [1]. The conclusion connects this practice to mindfulness, suggesting that with time and effort you can retrain your brain to think positively automatically [1].

- Circumstances are often uncontrollable, but your thoughts and attitude toward them influence your feelings, actions, and results [1].
- A negative attitude toward an unwanted work assignment can lead to resentment, shortcuts, and poor outcomes; a positive attitude can lead to better craft and better results [1].
- Gratitude is presented as a learnable practice that can increase happiness, supported by a cited video and a structured challenge [1].
- The "Happiness Invitation" involves nightly gratitude journaling, expressing gratitude to someone, and reflecting on how that feels [1].
- Mindfulness and repeated practice can help retrain automatic thoughts toward positivity over time [1].
