# Solidifying what you learn

The post argues that the best way to solidify understanding is to teach it to others. It opens with Ashley Williams’s quote: “Teaching is nature's way of letting you know how sloppy your understanding is” (source). The author says this has been true in his experience and outlines a learning process: consume information, build something with the technology, then teach what was learned in the process (source).

The author describes revamping his testing workshop for Frontend Masters and Workshop.me, splitting it into “Testing Practices and Principles” and “Testing React and Web Applications” (source). Iterating on workshop content improves delivery and deepens his own understanding because he must answer questions and simplify material for attendees (source).

He addresses common objections to teaching: “But, what could I teach? Everyone knows everything I know.” He responds that the moment you learn something new, you know something you could teach someone else (source). Using McDonald’s/Burger King and multiple Starbucks as analogies, he argues that unique approaches and accessibility let you reach people others cannot (source). He encourages taking every opportunity to teach because it increases the impact of time spent sharpening skills (source).

- Teaching is the best way to solidify what you learn and exposes sloppy understanding.
- A practical learning loop: consume information, build something, then teach what you learned.
- Iterating on and delivering workshops improves both teaching quality and personal mastery of the material.
- You can teach as soon as you learn something new; unique style and accessibility help you reach different people.
- Teaching increases the impact of the time you spend sharpening your skills.
