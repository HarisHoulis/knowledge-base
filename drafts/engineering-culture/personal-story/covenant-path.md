# The Dow's Start on the Covenant Path

In this personal essay, Kent C. Dodds recounts the journey of his friends Jeremiah and Beiyi Dow from meeting at a family camp to their baptism into The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. The story begins with a chance encounter at Aspen Grove, where the Dows' family was welcomed by Kent's family, leading to a lasting friendship [1]. Later, at Remix Jam, conversations about faith led to the Dows accepting a copy of the Book of Mormon and finding a local congregation in New Hampshire [2]. The Dows were drawn to the community's 'good fruit'—the welcoming people and the invitation to seek answers for themselves [3]. Kent was honored to speak at and perform the baptism on January 4th, describing the experience as deeply moving and spiritually powerful [4]. The essay emphasizes how small acts of kindness and intentional sharing of faith can lead to meaningful connections and sacred moments. Kent reflects on the unexpected privilege of being part of such a significant event, noting the Dows' preparedness and the warm reception they received from the ward [5].

- Small acts of kindness and genuine welcome can forge deep connections that lead to unexpected and meaningful experiences.
- Sharing one's faith can be done respectfully, inviting questions and allowing others to seek answers on their own terms.
- Community plays a crucial role in personal transformation, as exemplified by the Dows' positive reception in their new congregation.
- The baptism was not just a ritual but a covenant with Christ, representing the start of a spiritual journey for the Dows.
- Personal involvement in someone else's sacred moments can be humbling and profoundly rewarding.
