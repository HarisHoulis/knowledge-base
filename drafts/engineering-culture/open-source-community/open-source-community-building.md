# Building the Open Source Community We Want

Kent C. Dodds reflects on his first open source contribution in 2012—a typo fix in the Java Playframework—and how maintainer James Roper's simple "Thank you for your contribution" made him feel valued. He argues that small acts of gratitude can shape the community we want to live in (kentcdodds.com, 2019).

He parallels this with Larry and Sharon Adams, who revitalized their Wisconsin neighborhood rather than leaving it, helping reduce crime by 42% from 2007 to 2018. Dodds notes people should not be judged for leaving communities for their own health, but he admires intentionally improving a community instead of abandoning it, and says the same can apply to digital and open source communities (kentcdodds.com, 2019).

He calls for intentional community care: enforcing codes of conduct, helping newcomers through mistakes, and accepting that this takes extra effort compared with just doing the work yourself. He challenges readers to pause coding, identify rough spots in their corner of open source, and make plans to improve it so more people feel empowered to contribute (kentcdodds.com, 2019).

- Small courtesies like thanking contributors can have a lasting impact and help build the community you want.
- The Adams' neighborhood revitalization illustrates improving a community in place rather than leaving it.
- Open source communities need intentional maintenance of their social health, not just their code.
- Codes of conduct and hand-holding for newcomers create safety and sustainability, even if slower.
- Step back from code, identify rough spots, and improve your corner of the open source community.
