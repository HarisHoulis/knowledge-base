# Where Do AI Norms Come From? Listening First, Then Writing Them Down

Charity Majors answers a reader question about how companies should approach writing down norms and values around AI usage, following her LinkedIn post sharing Honeycomb's internal AI norms and values documents (Majors, 2026). Her advice: start by listening. She scheduled opt-in, open-door discussion hours capped at 10 people and asked wide-open questions like "What AI-related thing has annoyed you lately?", then followed emerging themes in later sessions to build a fuller picture of what was happening across the org (Majors, 2026). The sessions also served as a steam valve for pent-up anger and frustration, such that by the time the AI norms and values documents were released internally, the heat had simmered down considerably (Majors, 2026).

The post kicks off a planned blog dialogue between Majors and research psychologist Dr Cat Hicks on norms, ethics, and learning in the era of AI (Majors, 2026). Majors frames her interest via Hicks's book "The Psychology of Software Teams", which she read in a single sitting after months of delay while finishing the second edition of "Observability Engineering" (Majors, 2026). Despite stark differences in form — Hicks's slim 150-page book with fifty pages of reference notes versus Majors's sprawling 600-page O'Reilly volume — Majors argues both make the same case from different angles: what feeds greatness in people and teams, what our responsibilities to each other are, and how to engage with complex sociotechnical systems as builders and caretakers (Majors, 2026).

That framing extends to AI, where the stakes feel high and unsettled. Majors notes that teams may be delivering twice as much twice as fast yet falling far short of expectations if managers, execs, or investors expect 10x or 100x, and that the only honest answer to whether those expectations are unreasonable is that no one really knows yet (Majors, 2026). She describes the industry moment as ecstatic possibilities coexisting with existential dread, and announces that Hicks's response will appear at her newsletter, fightforthehuman.com (Majors, 2026).

- Start writing AI norms by listening: Majors used opt-in, open-door discussion hours capped at 10 people, opening with broad questions like "What AI-related thing has annoyed you lately?".
- Follow emerging themes across sessions to build a fuller picture of what is actually happening in the org rather than guessing at norms.
- The listening sessions acted as a steam valve for frustration, so the eventual internal release of the AI norms and values docs landed with much less heat.
- Majors is launching a blog dialogue with research psychologist Dr Cat Hicks on norms, ethics, and learning in the AI era.
- On AI output expectations, Majors argues no one honestly knows yet whether 2x-delivery teams falling short of 10x/100x expectations reflects unreasonable expectations or insufficient output.
