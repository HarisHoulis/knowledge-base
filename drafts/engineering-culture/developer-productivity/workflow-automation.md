# An Argument for Automation

In “An Argument for Automation” (Kent C. Dodds, 2020), Kent C. Dodds argues that automating repetitive developer workflows is worthwhile beyond simple time savings. Using his blog-post creation process as an example, he lists manual steps such as creating index.mdx, finding and downloading an Unsplash image, writing frontmatter, and committing/pushing changes. Even though the non-writing steps take only about 60 seconds, he says automating them still pays off because the main benefit is not just saved time.

One key benefit is preserving focus: automation reduces context switching, so creative work like writing or debugging is not interrupted by boilerplate setup. Dodds also highlights human error: computers can repeat mundane tasks accurately, while people perform poorly at repetitive manual work and are more likely to make mistakes as steps accumulate. Automation also enables “doing more” per task, such as generating YAML, resizing and optimizing banner images, retrieving image credits, and generating slugs and dates.

Beyond personal productivity, Dodds argues that sharing automation multiplies its value. If 100 other people use a tool, that is 100x the time saved, and successful open-source examples like semantic-release show the impact. He also frames automation as a learning opportunity, from building CLIs and integrating APIs to learning testing, CI, and releases. He concludes that not everything should be automated, but many workflows benefit from automation because it helps developers ship faster with fewer bugs (Dodds, 2020).

- Automating repetitive workflows saves more than time: it protects focus, reduces context switching, and keeps developers on creative, high-value work.
- Computers perform mundane repetition accurately, reducing the human error that grows with manual, repetitive tasks.
- Automation enables “doing more” per task—such as image optimization, metadata generation, and automatic credits—and can increase how often work like blogging or releases happens.
- Sharing automation, especially open source, multiplies time savings across users and can become a product or a valuable learning experience.
- Not everything needs to be automated, but automation often helps teams ship faster with fewer bugs.
