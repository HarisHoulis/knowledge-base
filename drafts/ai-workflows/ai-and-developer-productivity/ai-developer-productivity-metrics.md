# The State of AI in Software Development: Data from 400+ Orgs — Justin Reock, DX

Justin Reock, Deputy CTO at DX, presents findings from DX's quarterly State of AI and Impact Reports, which analyze raw data from 400+ organizations. DX's research platform was created by experts who developed the DORA metrics, SPACE framework, and DevEx framework, and it tracks developer experience and AI use in work processes. The session focuses on raw data from the latest report, especially organizational trends and changing work processes.

On development speed, Reock says DORA deployment frequency shows stable growth but is slowing down a bit. He notes the early surge may have been due to people filing PRs more often, and cautions that deployment frequency is a proxy metric that does not fully reflect value creation: it does not capture kickbacks, defect ratios, or failed changes. Regionally, North America shows an upward trend while Europe shows a slight decline over the last quarter, which Reock attributes to differences in how work is done, spending on technologies, token costs, and regulation.

Perceived delivery speed increased by about 4.5%, which Reock finds modest given a full year of AI investment and spending. He contrasts this with the flawed METR study, where among 16 engineers productivity dropped by 19% but perception increased by 20%, a 40% gap between perceived and actual productivity. In DX's broader data, he says the big picture remains quite stable.

For quality and delivery, Reock examines the DORA indicator for rate of failed changes. The observed quality changes are very unstable, with each line representing a separate company. Some top lines show 2% growth, which he says sounds unthreatening until considering that the industry standard is around 4%; that represents a potential 50% increase in defects compared to previous levels.

- DX's quarterly State of AI and Impact Reports analyze raw data from 400+ organizations to track AI's impact on developer experience and productivity.
- DORA deployment frequency shows stable growth but is slowing, with regional variation: North America trending upward and Europe slightly declining over the last quarter.
- Deployment frequency is a proxy metric and does not capture kickbacks, defect ratios, or failed changes.
- Engineers' perceived delivery speed rose about 4.5%, contrasted with METR's reported 40% gap between perceived and actual productivity.
- Failed-change data is unstable; a 2% increase could represent a potential 50% increase in defects relative to the roughly 4% industry standard.
