# Paul Ford on Why AI Still Needs Human Engineers

Paul Ford argues that the initial fear that AI would eliminate software developer roles has given way to a more nuanced industry realization: building truly cutting-edge software still requires humans to think and work together, maximize their skill sets, and practice their respective crafts. The quote is surfaced by Simon Willison on his blog (simonwillison.net, 2026-09-12), citing Ford's New York Times opinion piece "A.I. Was Supposed to Give Us New Killer Apps. What Happened?"

Ford concedes that AI can write very good software, but highlights a countervailing effect: it makes it easy to do someone else's job badly, which he cites as part of why many AI-driven projects fail. His conclusion is that now that everyone can code, it has become clearer why many shouldn't — a claim about judgment and craft rather than raw code generation ability.

The passage is framed as a reversal of earlier expectations that tireless robots would render human developers obsolete, and positions human collaboration and specialization as the remaining differentiator.

- AI can write very good software, but the industry is realizing cutting-edge software still requires humans working together.
- AI lowers the barrier to doing someone else's job badly, which Ford identifies as part of why many projects fail.
- Because everyone can now code, it's clearer why many people shouldn't — craft and judgment still matter.
