# Voice Agents Can Just Do Things

Charlie Guo argues that a common misconception about voice agents is that they must talk back. He proposes that speech is not the only way a voice model can respond, and identifies three emerging interaction modes: speech-to-speech, speech-to-action, and event-to-speech (Charlie Guo, 2026).

These modes are not new—Guo compares speech-to-speech to movie phone hotlines and event-to-speech to GPS navigation—but recent model capabilities make more interesting applications possible. They are remixable rather than mutually exclusive, and the best products combine them (Charlie Guo, 2026).

Speech-to-speech covers user speech and model speech, with examples like live practice and coaching for language learning, concierge-style customer support, and live translation. Guo expects support experiences to become more enjoyable than phone trees or the median human support agent, and suggests live streams could be dubbed in real time across languages (Charlie Guo, 2026).

Speech-to-action is described as underexplored: the user talks and the model uses tools. Examples include form filling—talking through government documents instead of typing every field—and creative tools. Event-to-speech is represented by systems like GPS navigation that respond to events rather than user speech (Charlie Guo, 2026).

- Voice agents do not have to respond with speech; three modes are speech-to-speech, speech-to-action, and event-to-speech.
- The modes are not new and are remixable, with the best products combining them.
- Speech-to-speech fits coaching, language learning, concierge support, and live translation.
- Speech-to-action is underexplored; form filling and creative tools are promising.
- Event-to-speech has longstanding examples such as GPS navigation.
