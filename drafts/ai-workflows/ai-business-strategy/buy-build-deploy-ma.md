# Buy, Build, Deploy: Using M&A to Drive AI Transformation

Prathik Naidu of Long Lake argues that selling AI into corporations from the outside is structurally hard, and that acquiring businesses and embedding AI into them from day one is a more effective path to real-world AI transformation. He walks through the typical vendor journey: finding a problem, building an agent that works for one narrow use case, then hitting the first wall at production deployment—security clearance, internal coordination, and integration with twenty-year-old systems. After that comes the adoption problem (whose job is it to drive usage?) and finally the monetization problem (tokens vs. subscriptions, price sensitivity, measuring ROI).

Naidu stresses that the model is not the bottleneck—models are becoming high quality and intelligence is growing exponentially. The difficulty is organizational: companies are complex networks of interactions among experienced people across sales and operations, so a third-party agent must integrate into that web. Vendors can connect to CRMs, ticketing systems, and document repositories full of SOPs and contracts, but the real operational reality lives in human judgment, exceptions, edge cases, and tacit knowledge held by the people doing the work.

Even a successful seven-figure contract leaves the vendor capturing little of the value created. If a client gains $5M/year in EBITDA impact, a conservative 10x multiple implies $50M in enterprise value—value that accrues to the business's P&L, not the supplier who did the deployment work. Hence the pitch: instead of implementing AI from the outside in, buy or build the business itself and embed AI into its foundation from day one, improving the entire workflow rather than a narrow slice.

- Selling AI into enterprises from outside hits successive walls: production integration, user adoption, and monetization/ROI.
- The model isn't the constraint—organizational complexity, tacit knowledge, and edge-case human judgment are.
- Vendors can integrate with CRMs and document repositories but still miss the operational reality of how work is actually done.
- Most economic value from AI deployment accrues to the client's P&L (e.g., $5M EBITDA at a 10x multiple = $50M enterprise value), not the supplier.
- Acquiring or building a business and embedding AI from day one lets you transform the whole workflow instead of a narrow use case.
