# Design at the Speed of Adjectives

Paul Bakaus argues that the roles of engineer and designer are blurring, with designers moving into code and engineers into design. The traditional waterfall handoff—PM defines the what, design refines, engineering builds—is collapsing in small and large companies, and tools like Impeccable provide a shared design language for cross-disciplinary communication (Bakaus, 2026).

Bakaus frames current design/agent interaction as two extremes: direct manipulation in pixel tools like Figma or Webflow, which operates at too low an elevation (padding, margins, spacing), and fully agentic prompting ("please design this"), which produces generic AI slop. He calls for a middle ground with the human in the loop at the right time (Bakaus, 2026).

His approach, "designing at the speed of adjectives" (and verbs), uses high-level descriptive controls rather than manual pixel tweaks or wholesale delegation. He demonstrates Impeccable, a design skill that works across harnesses including Claude Code, GitHub Copilot, Cursor, and Codex. A prompt like "make it bolder" yields a design that is bolder than the baseline, though not perfect—AI artifacts like section numbers still creep in (Bakaus, 2026). The talk notes AI slop is a moving target: purple gradients have given way to "Claude beige," Instrument Serif fonts, and italics (Bakaus, 2026).

- Direct manipulation is too low-level, while fully agentic design produces slop; the middle ground is human-in-the-loop at the right abstraction.
