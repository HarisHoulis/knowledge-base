# One Designer + AI: Scaling Conference Design Deliverables

Vincent Weng, a senior creative designer at AI Engineer, describes being the sole designer for a conference scaling to 7,000 attendees, 140+ sponsors, 300+ speakers, and 600+ sessions (AI Engineer talk). Every sponsor, speaker, sign, sticker, landing page, and announcement needs design, so missing a sponsor logo or publishing a wrong speaker schedule becomes a major issue. His design team is himself plus Devin, GPT, and Figma; the point is that tools are no longer the main problem, but applying them effectively is where the advantage lies.

He organizes the solution around five practices: foundation first, reusable designs, automated workflows, validated output, and removing frictions (AI Engineer talk). The foundation includes a design system with typography, colors, and components. He connects this to atomic design—creating the smallest parts and combining them like LEGO pieces into deliverables. For example, defining desktop and mobile typography tokens is necessary because LLMs otherwise introduce random font sizes and produce sloppy output.

He also references Simon Wilson’s 2025 test of asking LLMs to create a vector pelican riding a bicycle, noting it is still not usable for a designer (AI Engineer talk). Instead, he suggests asking ChatGPT for a still PNG and vectorizing it in Figma, then shipping it—an example of thinking outside the box regardless of current LLM capabilities.

- Vincent Weng was the only designer supporting AI Engineer conference scale: 7,000 attendees, 140+ sponsors, 300+ speakers, and 600+ sessions, requiring hundreds of deliverables.
- His design team included AI tools—Devin, GPT, and Figma—shifting the challenge from tool availability to effective use.
