# Meta's Muse: Cute Packaging, Underestimated Power

John Gruber argues that Meta's Muse is receiving deserved attention both for its technical foundation and its packaging. Technically, each user gets their own entire persistent Linux VM running in Meta's cloud, and the system is delivered in an easy-to-install, easy-to-use form — complete with a cute mascot (daringfireball.net/linked/2026/09/25/aten-muse). Gruber calls it "the first consumer-accessible agentic AI system" and credits Meta with executing it well.

The open question, per Gruber, is whether consumers understand what they are actually getting. He draws a power-saw analogy: someone buying a saw capable of severing fingers is almost certainly aware of that risk, but that awareness does not obviously transfer to agentic AI. "I don't think people realize how powerful — and thus dangerous — Muse is, especially if it's running on your Mac," he writes.

The underlying concern is the gap between approachable consumer packaging and the real capability of a persistent, agentic system with broad access to a user's machine. Simon Willison surfaces the quote as a notable framing of that tension.

- Meta's Muse is described as the first consumer-accessible agentic AI system, with each user getting a dedicated persistent Linux VM in Meta's cloud.
- Its easy-to-install, cute-mascot packaging may mask how powerful and dangerous the system is.
- Gruber's power-saw analogy: users understand physical tools that can injure them, but likely do not grasp equivalent risk in agentic AI.
- Particular concern is raised about Muse running on a user's Mac, where agent capabilities reach beyond a sandboxed cloud VM.
