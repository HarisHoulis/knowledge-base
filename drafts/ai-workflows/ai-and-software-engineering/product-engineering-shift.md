# Quoting Laurie Voss: We are all Product Engineers now

Laurie Voss argues that the cost of writing code has collapsed, and that the cost of reviewing, fixing, and operating it is following and will get there too. What is left of making software, then, is finding out what people actually want, defining it precisely, and making it pleasant to use.

That remaining cost is per piece of software and does not transfer between projects. Because there is no ceiling on demand, the amount of software will grow toward infinity, and that non-transferable cost becomes the whole job.

The implication is a shift toward product engineering: as code production becomes cheap, the differentiating work is understanding and specifying what should be built and ensuring it is pleasant to use.

- The cost of writing code has collapsed due to AI.
- The cost of reviewing, fixing, and operating code is following and expected to collapse as well.
- The remaining non-transferable costs—discovering user needs, defining them precisely, and making software pleasant to use—become the whole job.
- Demand for software has no ceiling, so the total amount of software will keep growing toward infinity.
