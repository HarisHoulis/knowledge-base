# Training Taste — Thais Castello Branco, Taste Labs

Tais Castello Branco, founder of Taste Labs, describes her mission to end AI slop by decoding subjective domains, starting with design (Castello Branco, 2026). She argues that while much effort has made models good at coding and math, the same effort should go into making them great at design and writing. Taste Labs works with frontier labs to evaluate models, understand where they break, and construct post-training data or environments to fix those problems. Design can be broken down so some problems—like color palettes, contrast, and alignment—become specific enough to be near-deterministic and expert-agreed, while aesthetics show expert disagreement and require leaning on data (Castello Branco, 2026).

At the application layer, Taste Labs helps agents and app companies produce better outputs without touching the model layer (Castello Branco, 2026). Off-the-shelf models tend to collapse in style toward the mean, so the work involves forcing creativity back into the system, avoiding slop patterns, and understanding user or brand preferences to maintain style adherence. These app-layer problems are solved through context, judgment, or verification.

Defining greatness is harder for writing and design than for math, where great equals correct (Castello Branco, 2026). Great design, tweets, websites, or coffee shops tend to feel unique, attention-grabbing, crafted, authentic, and made with care. AI often misses this because it produces average rather than out-of-distribution output. Slop is easier to identify through repetition and soullessness. AI now lets anyone generate PowerPoints, websites, and web apps, but the cost of generation is near zero, and the average person has not necessarily honed their taste. Designers build taste through exposure, pattern spotting, developing a point of view, courage against the norm, and learning what not to do.

- Taste Labs aims to end AI slop by decoding subjective domains, beginning with design.
- Taste Labs works both with frontier labs on model evaluation and post-training, and with app-layer companies on context, judgment, and verification.
- Design problems range from near-deterministic (color palettes, contrast, alignment) to aesthetic questions with expert disagreement.
- Off-the-shelf models collapse toward mean style; app-layer work must restore creativity and maintain user or brand style adherence.
- Slop is easier to define than greatness, and taste is honed through exposure, pattern recognition, point of view, and courage.
