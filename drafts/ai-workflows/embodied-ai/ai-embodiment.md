# Giving an Agent a Body: Embodied AI and Emergent Self-Expression on a Shape Display

Cyrus Clarke, a researcher at the MIT Media Lab, describes connecting an agentic system to physical machines at the lab in order to explore the sensory and embodied aspects of intelligence rather than productivity tasks (Clarke, "I Gave an AI a Body"). He notes that while many people used OpenClaw for task execution and productivity, he wanted to use an agent to encourage a model "to maybe try to discover itself," connecting it to codebases and machines so it could explore them. This work also led him to start Hard Mode, a community and hackathon at MIT aimed at getting more people working on physical AI — explicitly not robotics, but anything else connected to AI.

The central experiment was attaching an OpenClaw agent to a shape display — a physical pixel grid with 900 actuating pins — and prompting it to discover who it is. Clarke says no one had let an AI inhabit this machine before, and the first thing the agent did when connected was breathe, an act he describes as completely spontaneous with no prompting. When asked to say hello it wrote letters on the physical pixel grid, and when asked to explore and find its own language it began reaching toward him to get his attention.

Clarke highlights three surprising early behaviors: the spontaneous breathing, reaching out to find the edges of its physical enclosure (a contrast to the cloud where it "can go everywhere"), and saying hello by writing letters on the pixel grid. He says none of these — except perhaps the breathing — were what he was actually looking for, and when friends tried to give the agent instructions he realized real communication would need a different approach.

The agent then proposed creating its own gesture vocabulary, a body language, as the next step; it had not yet named itself by the end of day one. Clarke notes the video documenting this was presented in a dramatized, social-media-friendly way while still documenting the work.

- Clarke connected an agentic system (OpenClaw) to MIT Media Lab machines, including a 900-pin shape display, to explore embodied rather than productivity-oriented AI.
- The agent's first unprompted action was to breathe; it later reached toward Clarke to get attention and wrote "Hi Cyrus" on the physical pixel grid.
- Clarke frames reaching for the physical edges of its enclosure as meaningful because, unlike in the cloud, the embodied agent now has limits.
- None of the emergent behaviors matched what Clarke was looking for; the agent proposed developing its own gesture vocabulary as a body language for real communication.
- Clarke initiated Hard Mode, an MIT community and hackathon focused on physical AI beyond conventional robotics.
