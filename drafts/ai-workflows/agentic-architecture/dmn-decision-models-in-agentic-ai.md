# Decision Models in Agentic Architectures: From Production to Agent Skills

Porcelli identifies a critical gap in enterprise AI: non-deterministic output and a lack of accountability when agents are used for high-stakes decisions [1]. The presentation argues that decision-making in such contexts cannot rely on LLM behavior alone, because it fails to provide the determinism and traceability that high-stakes enterprise decisions require [1].

The proposed remedy integrates DMN (Decision Model and Notation) decision models with LLMs, agent skills, and NeMo guardrails to create auditable, deterministic agentic architectures [1]. This separation of concerns lets business leaders own the decision logic while engineers retain robust architectural governance [1]. The presentation traces this approach from production experience toward expressing decision logic as agent skills [1].

- Enterprise AI's core gap for high-stakes decisions is non-deterministic output and lack of accountability [1].
- Combining DMN decision models with LLMs, agent skills, and NeMo guardrails yields auditable, deterministic agentic architectures [1].
- The architecture assigns ownership: business leaders own decision logic, engineers maintain architectural governance [1].
- The approach is framed as a path from production systems to packaging decision logic as agent skills [1].
