# Mousepower: Agents That Can’t Be Measured, Can’t Be Managed — Maximillian Piras, Yutori

Maximillian Piras, founding designer at Yutori, argues that agents have a measurement problem: users and builders struggle to value agent work, especially token costs relative to outcomes. He describes his own workflow of backgrounding many agents in parallel to explore peripheral tasks, such as slide design, while his active attention is elsewhere; later, the bill prompts doubts about whether it was worth it and whether he was token maxing or sequencing agents inefficiently (AI Engineer, 2026).

At Yutori, Piras works on computer-use models: agents that learn to use a computer like a human when information cannot be retrieved via an API or MCP. This approach is less efficient than APIs or MCPs, but it serves as a last resort for extracting data and manipulating information that was previously inaccessible (AI Engineer, 2026).

Much of his design work involves customer conversations to understand how people intuitively value agent use cases. Customers are excited about agents but often feel they are only scratching the surface, and they ask what the best use cases are and how to think about trade-offs between token costs and value. Piras says this muscle is still being built, and his thesis is that agents have a measurement problem; he invokes a mandatory Upton Sinclair quote as part of that argument (AI Engineer, 2026).
