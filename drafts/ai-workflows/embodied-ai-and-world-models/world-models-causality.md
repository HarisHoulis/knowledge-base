# World Models Need Causality, Not Pretty Pixels — Christopher Manning, Moonlake AI

In this talk, Christopher Manning frames the North Star for AI — and for cognitive science — as understanding and developing embodied intelligence, and describes Moonlake's efforts toward a practical form of general-purpose embodied artificial intelligence (Manning, AI Engineer). The talk's stated thesis is that world models need causality, not pretty pixels. He structures the session as a long historical build-up to AI before turning to what Moonlake actually does.

That build-up starts before the term 'artificial intelligence' existed: 1940s and early-1950s work on cybernetics sought to combine communication, control, and feedback in living organisms and computers, and led to the first work on neural networks and perceptrons (Manning, AI Engineer). Manning shows Rosenblatt's perceptron, noting it predates the term artificial intelligence and that early neural networks were physically wired rather than implemented as modern matrix multiplication.

The term AI was coined by John McCarthy, who gathered the 1956 Dartmouth Summer Research Project; Manning singles out Claude Shannon among the attendees as his personal hero (Manning, AI Engineer). McCarthy later moved to Stanford and founded the Stanford Artificial Intelligence Laboratory in 1963, originally housed in a building near Arastradero, and that lab hosted the first video game tournament in 1972.

Natural language processing, Manning notes, began as machine translation and also predates the term AI: a 1954 public demonstration of Russian-to-English translation by machine was reported on the front page of the New York Times, which he observes resembles the 2026 front page in its political headlines (Manning, AI Engineer). The system was pitched as a Cold War tool for tracking what the Russians were doing, with claims that computers would replace most human translators.

- Manning states the long-term goal for AI, and for cognitive science, is to understand and develop embodied intelligence; Moonlake's aim is practical, general-purpose embodied AI.
