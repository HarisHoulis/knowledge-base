# Spotting AI-Written Scripts: Surface Tics vs. Absence of Voice

A TikTok creator quoted by Simon Willison argues that AI-written scripts for TikTok and YouTube are obvious to detect — but not primarily because of their surface stylistic tics. The recognizable AI-isms include the "it's not X, it's Y" construction, the rule of three, and a "really weird broken staccato-like way of writing" loaded with punctuation that "sounds really deep, but it's not" (source).

The deeper tell, per the quote, is not what the text contains but what it lacks: "It's the lack of *anything*. It's the lack of a definitive sort of spear of your voice." The speaker claims to be able to tell that the creator has no actual opinions about the subject they're discussing (source).

The passage is preserved as a quotation by Simon Willison, with the full video on TikTok. The emphasis is on absence of a distinct authorial voice and genuine point of view as the reliable signal, rather than any single formulaic phrase (source).

- Surface AI-isms cited: "it's not X, it's Y", the rule of three, and broken staccato writing with heavy punctuation that "sounds really deep, but it's not"
- The stronger signal is absence — the lack of "a definitive sort of spear of your voice"
- The quoted speaker says they can tell the writer has no opinions about the topic being discussed
- Applies specifically to AI-written scripts for TikTok and YouTube content
