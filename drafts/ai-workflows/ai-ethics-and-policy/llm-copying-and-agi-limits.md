# AI, Corporate Responsibility & Democratic Legitimacy: Extended Q&A with Joanna Bryson

In this extended GOTO 2025 Q&A, Joanna Bryson responds to an audience question arguing that today's "AGI" is essentially copy-pasting other people's work — code that already exists on GitHub, music that only sounds like Dolly Parton because the model previously ingested her catalogue and averaged it. Bryson treats this averaging at scale as the reason such systems appear intelligent without being so, and connects it to the mechanics of hallucinations: large language models must aggregate enough evidence for a claim to have a chance of being true, using weighted averages rather than emitting the single most likely sentence [GOTO 2025].

The consequence is a deliberate trade-off between creativity and reliability. Bryson cites Google's Gemini tuning, where the threshold of evidence required before asserting something was lowered to make the model "more creative and less reliable" and to push it toward the user's chosen direction [GOTO 2025].

She also pushes back on capability-progress graphs, arguing they are misleading and that they plateau at or not far above human capacity. What the field is actually doing, in her account, is getting very good at "uploading what we as a culture know" — the human superpower being shared knowledge, which is what enables buildings, microphones and other cultural artefacts [GOTO 2025].

Beyond the technical discussion, Bryson recalls a parliamentary meeting with a Bulgarian MEP whose view of AGI she characterizes as "digital anarchy," and describes telling ambassadors that current political conflict should be understood as an organized battle against the rule of law, particularly against money-laundering regulation [GOTO 2025].

- Current "AGI" largely recombines existing human output — code already on GitHub, music averaged from an artist's catalogue — which can look like intelligence at scale without being it [GOTO 2025].
- Hallucinations are tied to the evidence-aggregation trade-off: models seek weighted averages with enough supporting evidence rather than the single most probable sentence [GOTO 2025].
- Creativity versus reliability is an explicit tuning dial — e.g. Google lowering Gemini's evidence threshold to be more creative and less reliable [GOTO 2025].
- Progress graphs are misleading and plateau at or just above human capacity; the real achievement is uploading cultural knowledge, made possible by humans' capacity to share knowledge [GOTO 2025].
- Bryson frames current corporate-political conflict as an organized battle against the rule of law, particularly money-laundering regulation, and rejects the "digital anarchy" reading of AGI [GOTO 2025].
