# Green AI: Making Machine Learning Environmentally Sustainable

Charles Humble’s YOW! 2025 talk “Green AI” opens by questioning AI hype through an anecdote about a Microsoft spokesman visiting a school and a student defining AI as “a failed attempt to simulate human intelligence using a Turing machine” (GOTO Conferences, 2026). Humble notes that AI is currently “having a moment” and that there is pressure to adopt it in daily life and working practices, making it important to step back and consider the implications (GOTO Conferences, 2026).

The talk traces a broader pattern: software followed Moore’s law until relatively recently, and AI has similarly advanced as hardware became more capable and new or rediscovered techniques generated excitement, followed by periods of disappointment known as AI winters (GOTO Conferences, 2026). Humble argues there is something seductive about the idea of a machine that thinks like humans, and points to the rapid adoption of ChatGPT and large language models alongside ethical concerns such as anthropomorphization (GOTO Conferences, 2026).

For recent large transformer models, Humble says the architecture itself is not new; what is new is scale. The process takes a huge corpus—such as the internet—and trains on it, with reinforcement learning from human feedback at the end, producing a system that imitates what it ingested (GOTO Conferences, 2026).
