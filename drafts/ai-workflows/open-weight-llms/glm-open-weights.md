# GLM-5.2: Open Weights, Near-Frontier Intelligence

In this talk, Zixuan Li of Z.ai introduces GLM-5.2 to the public and situates it within the company's history and naming. The corporate entity is Zhipu, the models are branded GLM, and GLM originally stood for "General Language Model Pretraining with Autoregressive Gap Filling," a paper published in 2021. Li claims the lab was one of the first to explore large language models, alongside OpenAI, Anthropic, and DeepMind. GLM is no longer used as an architecture, but the name persists as the brand behind GLM 5.1 and 5.2 (Zixuan Li, Z.ai).

Li frames the goal as pushing the "upper limit of intelligence" rather than only IQ-style benchmarks such as AIME or math and physics problems. Between GLM 4.5 and 4.7, the team explored reasoning, programming, and agentic capabilities (transcribed as "genetic" in the talk). For GLM 5.2, Li says the model sits somewhere between Opus 4.7 and 4.8 on complex long-horizon tasks such as Terminal Bench 2.1, and that it shows significant improvement over the prior version. A new "high" thinking level was added as a thinking budget for complex tasks, and Li states that the model without thinking is still better than the 4.1 model with thinking — which he calls a huge step forward for open-weight models (Zixuan Li, Z.ai).

Li also argues GLM is more than a coding model. Although it is used in Claude Code, Codex, and OpenCode, he says the team trained it on many other things: improved results on GDPval-style evaluations and math problems, plus work on role-playing and general communication, with the aim of improving every aspect of the model. He notes the model is significantly ahead of other open-scale models on an artificial intelligence index, though still close to (not at) advanced proprietary models, and urges people to try it for general chats and everyday work tasks, not just coding (Zixuan Li, Z.ai).

The talk ends as Li begins addressing the question he says he is often asked: why Z.ai opens its weights — whether the company is worried about its business or afraid of losing something. The transcript cuts off before he answers (Zixuan Li, Z.ai).

- GLM stands for "General Language Model Pretraining with Autoregressive Gap Filling"; the 2021 paper predates the model's use of the name as a brand, and the company behind it is Zhipu.
- GLM 5.2 is positioned between Opus 4.7 and 4.8 on complex long-horizon benchmarks such as Terminal Bench 2.1, and adds a new "high" thinking budget level.
- Li claims GLM 5.2 without thinking outperforms GLM 4.1 with thinking — a notable step for open-weight models.
- The model is trained well beyond coding, with claimed gains in math, GDPval-style evaluations, role-playing, and general chat.
