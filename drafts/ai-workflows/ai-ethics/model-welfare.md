# Mustafa Suleyman: A Warning About 'Model Welfare'

In a quote relayed by Simon Willison, Microsoft AI CEO Mustafa Suleyman argues against granting AI models any form of moral standing: "We should not treat models as though they have feelings, preferences, rights, or any entitlement to our welfare" ([Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/16/mustafa-suleyman/)).

Suleyman grounds the argument in the role of consciousness: it is "the foundation of our ethical, legal, and political systems," so extending rights to entities that lack it would be unjustified by the available evidence ([Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/16/mustafa-suleyman/)).

He adds a practical concern: inviting another entity to share any flavor of those rights "will make the AI containment and alignment challenge even harder" — framing model-welfare advocacy as an obstacle to technical safety work, not just a philosophical misstep ([Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/16/mustafa-suleyman/)). The post is tagged ai-ethics, generative-ai, ai, microsoft, and llms.

- Suleyman rejects treating models as having feelings, preferences, rights, or entitlement to human welfare.
- His stated basis: consciousness underpins ethical, legal, and political systems, and granting models rights is not justified by the evidence.
- He argues model-welfare framing makes AI containment and alignment harder.
