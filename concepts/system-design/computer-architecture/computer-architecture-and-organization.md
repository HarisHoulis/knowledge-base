# Modern Computer Architecture & Organization: Jim Ledin on GPUs, LLMs, and Low-Level Fundamentals

In this GOTO Book Club interview, Jim Ledin discusses the third edition of his book 'Modern Computer Architecture & Organization'. Ledin, an embedded systems engineer with nearly 30 years of experience, explains that his interest began with slow graphics on a Commodore 64, leading him to learn 6502 assembly language and the interface between hardware and software. He argues that understanding low-level processor capabilities—whether ARM, Intel, or RISC-V—helps developers solve high-level tasks more effectively (GOTO Conferences, 2026).

- Low-level hardware and assembly knowledge improves high-level software development.
- GPU and LLM chapters center on stable principles like parallelism and memory bandwidth, not transient products.
- Modern GPU generations evolve incrementally, making core architecture understanding more valuable than chasing yearly releases.
- Scaling compute from a single chip to data-center-wide systems depends on interconnect and communication bandwidth.
