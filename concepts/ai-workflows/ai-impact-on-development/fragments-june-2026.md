# Fragments: June 2

Martin Fowler's June 2026 fragments collect observations on AI's effect on software development and knowledge work. He highlights Greg Wilson's critique of flawed AI productivity metrics—such as lines of code generated or developer self-reports—while acknowledging that qualitative self-assessments, though imperfect, can serve as a 'dim light' when better measures are absent. Benedict Evans's historical analysis shows that extensive automation of accounting did not reduce the number of accountants, illustrating how forecasting AI's impact on jobs is nearly impossible due to the Jevons paradox and the changing nature of work itself.

- Common AI productivity metrics are flawed; qualitative signals may still provide limited value.
- Historical automation trends suggest AI job impact forecasts are unreliable.
- Open-source models are catching up to closed models faster on benchmarks (2-7 months for GPT-4o vs 13-18 for GPT-4).
- AI-generated hallucinated citations poison the web's knowledge pool, as seen in Ernst & Young's report.
- LLMs amplify existing codebase quality: 'generative debt' emerges when models imitate technical debt as precedent.
