# Fable & The End of the Free Lunch

Drew Breunig observes that before Fable, investing heavily in coding harnesses or context strategies seemed like a waste because new models would arrive at the same or lower price and 'paper over most of your problems.' However, Fable's arrival changed this calculus: while it is 'incredible,' its high cost paired with cheaper models being 'good enough' for most coding needs forced a more deliberate approach. Teams now must explicitly decide which tasks warrant the expensive model and which can be handled by cheaper alternatives. This marks the 'end of the free lunch' where you could rely on model improvements rather than optimizing your own workflow (Drew Breunig, 'Fable & The End of the Free Lunch', 2026).

- Pre-Fable, improving coding harnesses felt low-value because model upgrades would soon solve the same problems at equal or lower cost.
- Fable is powerful but expensive, while other models like Opus, 5.6, K3, and GLM are 'good enough' for most code.
- The cost gap forces teams to think about which tasks get the premium model and which can use cheaper alternatives.
- This marks a shift from relying on model generation improvements to active cost-aware engineering of workflows.
