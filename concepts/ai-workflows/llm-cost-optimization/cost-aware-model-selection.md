# Fable & The End of the Free Lunch

Drew Breunig observes that prior to Fable, it felt wasteful to invest heavily in coding harnesses or context strategies, because each new model would arrive at the same or lower price and solve most problems (https://simonwillison.net/2026/Aug/23/drew-breunig/). Fable changed that calculation: it was "incredible" but costly, while previous models like Opus, 5.6, K3, and GLM were "good enough" for most code needs. This forced a strategic decision about "what work went where" — allocating tasks to models based on value and cost. The "end of the free lunch" implies that as model improvements plateau, teams must optimize their own workflows and make deliberate trade-offs between premium and existing models.

- Before premium models, waiting for the next cheaper model was a better strategy than optimizing workflows.
- Fable's high cost relative to 'good enough' alternatives makes task-to-model allocation essential.
- Even excellent frontier models may not be cost-effective for all coding work.
- The era of free lunch from model improvements is ending, shifting focus to engineering efficiency.
