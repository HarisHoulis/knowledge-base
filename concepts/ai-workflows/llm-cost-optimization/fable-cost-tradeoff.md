# Fable & The End of the Free Lunch

Drew Breunig reflects on how the release of Anthropic's Fable model changed his team's approach to coding workflows. Before Fable, investing heavily in coding harnesses or context strategies felt pointless because newer models would quickly arrive at similar or lower prices and solve existing problems. However, Fable's exceptional capabilities came with a high cost, while other models like Opus, 5.6, K3, and GLM were 'good enough' for most coding tasks. This prompted the team to deliberately decide which tasks warranted the premium model and which could be handled by cheaper alternatives, marking an end to the assumption that model improvements would always provide a free lunch.

- Prior to Fable, rapid model improvements made optimizing coding harnesses seem wasteful.
- Fable is incredibly capable but priced far higher than existing models.
- Existing models (Opus, 5.6, K3, GLM) remained sufficient for the majority of coding work.
- The cost disparity forced a more deliberate allocation of tasks across different models.
