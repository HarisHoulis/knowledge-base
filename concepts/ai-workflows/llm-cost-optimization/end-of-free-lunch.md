# Fable & The End of the Free Lunch

Drew Breunig reflects on a shift in AI coding workflows triggered by the release of Fable, a high-cost, high-performance model. Previously, developers could rely on regular model improvements to automatically solve problems, making investments in coding harnesses or context strategies feel low-value. Fable changed that calculus: its exceptional quality came at a steep price, while other models like Opus, 5.6, K3, and GLM were 'good enough' for most coding tasks, making Fable impractical for routine use.

- Before Fable, frequent model upgrades made optimizing workflows feel wasteful.
- Fable's high cost forced a reconsideration of resource allocation.
- Models like Opus, 5.6, K3, and GLM were sufficient for most needs, so Fable wasn't universally necessary.
- The new challenge is deciding which tasks justify using premium models versus cheaper alternatives.
