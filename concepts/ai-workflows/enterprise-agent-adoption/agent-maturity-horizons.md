# Redesigning How Software Gets Built With AI Agents — Sonar & McKinsey Panel

A McKinsey-led panel with the CEOs of Sonar and GitLab discusses why most companies have adopted AI agents but few see scaled business results. The moderator reports that 80–90% of companies say they have implemented AI or agents in some form, yet fewer than a third have scaled it or are seeing business results, often because efforts stay confined to pilots or small environments rather than spanning the whole organization.

The panel frames maturity in horizons: most companies sit at "Horizon 2," where agents perform tasks under human supervision and verification, while very few reach "Horizon 3," where the workflow is fully automated start to finish under human supervision. Three factors are identified as enabling scale: rethinking the entire SDLC cycle rather than the traditional workflow, having reliable agents, frameworks, and context, and—most often ignored—the human factor.

On the human side, the boundaries of traditional roles such as software engineer, product manager, and designer blur, and organizations that rebuild their operating model around this shift are the ones seeing dramatic productivity gains. Skeptics who initially assumed agents would simply do the job gradually became convinced that organizations must be restructured.

Sonar's CEO describes his own company's transformation: an 18-year-old open-source project adapting its engineering team to new tools, with the explicit goal of creating real value rather than maximizing tokens. The company organized around a "manage, verify, decide" model, built a toolkit, and worked with McKinsey to analyze its actions—discovering the team had already created agents of its own, including PRD agents, and needed to learn how to map PRD content into scores used to verify results.

- 80–90% of companies claim some AI/agent implementation, but fewer than a third have scaled it or see business results, often due to staying in pilots rather than organization-wide deployment.
- Most organizations are at "Horizon 2" (agents under human supervision and verification); few reach "Horizon 3" (fully automated end-to-end workflows under human supervision).
- Three scaling factors: rethink the whole SDLC rather than the traditional workflow, provide reliable agents/frameworks/context, and address the often-ignored human factor.
- Blurring role boundaries (engineer, PM, designer) and rebuilding the operating model around that shift correlate with the biggest productivity gains.
- Sonar's own journey centered on creating real value rather than maximizing tokens, using a "manage, verify, decide" model and mapping PRD content into verification scores.
