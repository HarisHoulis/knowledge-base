# Fragments: July 6

Reflecting on the second Future of Software Development Retreat, Martin Fowler notes a significant shift in the AI-software engineering discourse. Unlike the earlier hesitant discussions, participants now exhibit confidence that agentic tools deliver real value, with many already shipping production code (Fowler, https://martinfowler.com/fragments/2026-07-06.html; Edwards-Alexander, https://overwatering.org/blog/2026/07/notes-from-fose-europe/). The debate over whether AI changes software engineering is over, replaced by practical questions of how to implement these tools effectively.

- The second retreat showed a transition from aspirational talk to real-world adoption, with attendees shipping production software and evolving patterns for agentic engineering.
- Architecture and good design remain critical—partly because AI models benefit from the same clarity humans need, and partly as a hedge against potential AI dependency risks (e.g., rising costs or restricted access).
- Ethical engagement with AI, as argued by Charity Majors, is preferable to renunciation; building within the system is a more effective way to drive change than withdrawing.
- Token costs are spiraling out of control, prompting companies like Citi and Amazon to throttle AI usage, downgrade models, and even resort to 'caveman' prompts to reduce expenditure.
