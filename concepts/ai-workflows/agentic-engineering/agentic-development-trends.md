# Fragments: July 6

The article also addresses the ethics of AI through Charity Majors' post. Majors acknowledges AI's harms—training on stolen data, slop, lack of accountability, skill atrophy—but argues that renouncing AI provides no ethical gain and that engagement, rather than purity, drives change. Additionally, Fowler shares reports from 404 Media about companies throttling AI usage due to skyrocketing token costs, including one company's bill rising from $5M to $15M monthly, and efforts to reduce costs by using less powerful models or even prompting AI to 'speak like cavemen'.

- Agentic development has moved from speculation to production; the value is now evident in real deployments.
- Good software design remains critical because AI agents interpret codebases using human-like constructs; the Developer Experience and Agent Experience overlap.
- Token costs are escalating sharply, leading companies to throttle AI usage, cap models, and seek creative cost-cutting measures.
- Ethical engagement with AI—accepting complicity and working to improve systems—is more constructive than boycotting AI entirely.
- The retreat also saw the rise of new terms like 'harness engineering' and continued exploration of architecture's role.
