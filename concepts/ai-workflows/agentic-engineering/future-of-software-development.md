# Fragments: July 6

Martin Fowler reports from the second Future of Software Development Retreat in Europe, highlighting a shift in attitude among practitioners. Where the first retreat was hesitant and aspirational, this one exuded confidence: agentic development is now being shipped in production, not just discussed. He notes the rapid emergence of new concepts like "harness engineering" and a growing concern about token costs, which have replaced earlier enthusiasm for encouraging AI use [source](https://martinfowler.com/fragments/2026-07-06.html).

A central question remains whether architecture and design still matter in an agentic age. Fowler presents two contrasting hypotheses: one that The Genie's advanced capabilities make good design unnecessary, and another, captured by Laura Tacho's phrase "the Venn Diagram of Developer Experience and Agent Experience is a circle," suggesting that AI agents benefit from the same modularity and naming that help humans. He cites Adam Tornhill's work as supporting the latter view, and adds Mathias Verraes' argument that good design also serves as a hedge against dependence on AI, given uncertainties around availability and costs [source](https://martinfowler.com/fragments/2026-07-06.html).

The article also addresses the ethics of AI through Charity Majors' post, which argues that engagement and building with AI is more ethical than purity and renunciation. Fowler agrees, acknowledging complicity while advocating for action. Additionally, he reports on token cost escalation, citing 404 Media's findings that companies are throttling AI usage and even making models speak like cavemen to reduce expenses. Finally, he highlights a case of fraudulent DMCA takedowns, illustrating the vulnerability of online content [source](https://martinfowler.com/fragments/2026-07-06.html).

- Agentic development has moved from theory to production, with confidence replacing hesitancy at the retreat.
- Architecture and design still matter in AI-assisted coding, as agents benefit from the same clarity that helps humans.
- Token costs are becoming a major operational concern, prompting companies to limit AI usage and seek cost-control measures.
- Engaging with AI is ethically complex; showing up and building is seen as more responsible than renunciation.
- Abuse of DMCA claims can suppress legitimate content, as seen in Gergely Orosz's case.
