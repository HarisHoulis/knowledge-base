# Buying GPUs Should Work Like Buying Stocks — Compute Exchange

Carmen Li, CEO of Compute Exchange, argues that GPU procurement should resemble modern stock markets rather than calling hundreds of individual sellers. She notes the compute ecosystem has exploded to over 300 private cloud providers, token factories, and API routers, which raises the question of how to transact efficiently. Her answer is a neutral exchange where buyers can access proven resources for near-term needs, analogous to going to Nasdaq to buy stocks, with known liquidity and deal certainty.

Compute Exchange positions itself as a neutral marketplace that by design owns no GPUs, since owning inventory would create a conflict of interest with both buyers and suppliers—likened to Nasdaq owning stocks. Each deal is evaluated individually to find the most suitable compute for the client, focusing on reserved compute forward contracts and bundled tokens. The platform reports over 100 verified providers and 80,000+ GPUs in stock, covering all regions except China, with a commitment to respond to requests within 24 hours.

Unlike so-called brokers, the exchange claims to actually test GPUs: for existing reserved capacity it checks UID, inventory, and performance using NVIDIA DGX, Inference X, and MLPerf to verify buyers get what was promised. For forward capacity (e.g., GPUs starting January 2027), it reviews orders and commitments to support due diligence, and it conducts KYB/KYC on customers so providers can trust counterparties' ability to pay.

Li frames the forward markets as a precursor to physically delivered futures, drawing on her background as an energy options market maker at DRW Trading. She compares WTI (physically delivered) and Brent (cash settled) to the products Compute Exchange is building, offering reserve contracts and forward contracts on both GPUs and tokens—the latter akin to a dedicated local deployment of reserved bare-metal GPU clusters.

- Compute Exchange is a neutral GPU marketplace that owns no GPUs, avoiding conflicts of interest with buyers and suppliers.
- It claims 100+ verified providers and 80,000+ GPUs in stock across all regions except China, with 24-hour response commitments.
- Existing capacity is validated via UID, inventory, and performance testing (NVIDIA DGX, Inference X, MLPerf); forward capacity is reviewed for due diligence.
- KYB/KYC checks on customers help providers assess counterparty ability to pay.
- Products include reserve and forward contracts on GPUs and tokens, modeled on energy futures like WTI (physical delivery) and Brent (cash settlement).
