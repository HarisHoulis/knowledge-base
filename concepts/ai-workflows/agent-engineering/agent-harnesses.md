# Fragments: July 13 - Harness Engineering and Self-Hosted Models

The article is a collection of notes from the Thoughtworks Future of Software Development Retreat. It highlights the growing importance of harness engineering for LLM agents, focusing on two sides: guides (like context management, keeping agent files small) and sensors (like computational testing and validation). The author also discusses the motivations for self-hosting open-weight models, including cost, sovereignty, security, and the desire to avoid dependence on frontier model firms. A recurring theme is how much autonomy to give agents, framed by Kief Morris as the "unit of work" handed to an agent, and the need for human acceptance criteria. Additionally, the article touches on managing agents by objectives rather than methods, the financial impact on online course creators due to AI, and the potential end of cross-platform UI frameworks in an agentic programming world.

- Harness engineering for LLMs involves managing context (guides) and validation (sensors), with an emphasis on keeping context small and using stronger testing methods.
- Self-hosting open-weight models is increasingly attractive due to lower token costs, data sovereignty, security, and independence from frontier model companies.
- The central debate is how much to trust agents: the key is defining the unit of work you hand over and how you verify it.
- Managers using LLMs directly is not about permission but about managing by objective, not method, as Drucker described.
- Course creators are seeing revenue drops due to AI, raising concerns about incentives for high-quality free content.
