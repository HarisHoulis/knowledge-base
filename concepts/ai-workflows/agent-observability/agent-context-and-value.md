# Your AI Agent Has No Nervous System

Matt Gibiec of Dynatrace argues that AI agents, despite being fast, lack the "nervous system" — the contextual awareness and feedback that humans use to adapt when an approach isn't working. He notes that agents commonly get stuck in loops, repeating the same actions and wasting tokens without producing the desired result, because they are not yet smart enough to change their approach the way a human researcher would [source].

Gibiec frames the core problem as a data and context gap. Citing Dynatrace CTO Bernd, he says agents "work quickly, but without vision": their effectiveness is limited by the quality of data available for analysis and decision-making. When an agent lacks context, detail, or the proper permissions to extract information from tools in the environment, it hallucinates and automates processes that add no value [source].

He also raises operational and security concerns, observing that audience members widely reported both operational issues and security issues with agents. Beyond reliability, he challenges teams to measure the value agents bring to users, noting that most organizations have no instructions for measuring agent value and have rushed into the AI era without pausing to define success [source].

The takeaway is that agents don't need a literal nervous system or vision to be effective — they need real context from your environment. Gibiec urges teams to slow down, understand what they are trying to achieve, and define metrics for agent success before spending engineering time, effort, and money on building and running agents [source].

- AI agents are fast but lack the contextual "nervous system" that lets humans adapt when an approach fails, leading to repetitive loops and wasted tokens.
- Agent effectiveness depends on the quality of data available: missing context, detail, or tool permissions causes hallucinations and valueless automation.
- Operational and security issues with agents are widespread among practitioners.
- Most organizations lack defined metrics for measuring the value their AI agents deliver to users.
- Agents don't need vision or a nervous system — they need real context from your environment.
