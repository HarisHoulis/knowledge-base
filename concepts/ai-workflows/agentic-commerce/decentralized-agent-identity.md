# Beyond the Lethal Trifecta: Agentic Commerce on the Open Internet

Levine's answer is identity: agents carry JWT tokens that resolve to a registered organization, much like DNS resolves a name, with an audit trail underneath. Two hours before his talk, he registered a DUNA (decentralized unincorporated nonprofit association) under new West Virginia law, granting an agent organization legal standing to own property, enter agreements, open bank accounts, and be held responsible in court, provided it does not distribute profits to members (which would turn memberships into securities). Governance is handled not by voting but by decision markets, where members trade pass and fail tokens on proposed policies. He posits that blockchain finally found its problem in providing the registry and market infrastructure for this system.

- The lethal trifecta of private data, untrusted content, and the ability to act prevents agents from operating openly; enterprises confine agents to walled gardens and pay a context cost.
- Agent identity via JWT tokens resolving to registered organizations, analogous to DNS, creates an audit trail and enables legal accountability.
- A DUNA (decentralized unincorporated nonprofit association) grants agent organizations legal standing to own property, contract, and open bank accounts, without profit distribution to members to avoid securities status.
- Governance runs on decision markets rather than votes, with members trading pass/fail tokens on policies.
- LambdaMOO serves as a historical precedent for composable online communities, which platforms later eroded.
