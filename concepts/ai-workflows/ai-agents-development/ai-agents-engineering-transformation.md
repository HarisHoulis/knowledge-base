# Slow Down to Speed Up: So Much Has Changed in 6 Months

Gergely Orosz's keynote at Craft Conference examines how AI agents have fundamentally changed software engineering in just six months, using Meta's massive outage as a cautionary tale. The outage, where Meta AI could change any account's email via a simple prompt, resulted from AI-generated and AI-reviewed code combined with heavy layoffs and forced reassignments of security and integrity teams. Orosz argues that Meta's obsessive focus on AI models over core product health led to this embarrassing incident, highlighting the risks of neglecting quality and reliability.

The shift began around November 2025 with more capable models like Opus 4.5 and GPT-5.4, making AI agents genuinely useful. Data from Linear, Cursor, and others shows developers now ship 5x more pull requests, produce 2.5x more code, and create 3x larger PRs compared to 18 months ago, with a significant rise in changes accepted without human review. Companies like Anthropic and OpenAI have deeply integrated AI agents: at Anthropic, 70-90% of code is generated by Claude; OpenAI's Codex team rarely writes code by hand. Google and Uber are also adopting AI widely, with Uber building custom internal infrastructure like Code Inbox and uReview to manage AI-assisted development effectively.

Trends include increased individual productivity but flat team productivity, the rise of tokenmaxxing and AI tooling, disappearance of middle management, and CEOs/CTOs returning to coding. Software quality is declining, as seen in GitHub reliability issues and the proliferation of AI slop. Orosz advises engineers to adapt by focusing on code review, developing 'taste', and embracing AI workflows to future-proof their careers. Feedback from attendees suggests these changes are widespread, affecting many organizations beyond tech giants.

- Meta's most embarrassing outage was caused by AI-generated, AI-reviewed code and gutted security/integrity teams, serving as a warning about prioritizing AI over product reliability.
- The November 2025 release of more capable AI agents (e.g., Opus 4.5, GPT-5.4) marked a turning point, making agents genuinely useful and transforming development workflows.
- Developers now ship significantly more code: 5x more PRs, 2.5x more code, and 3x larger PRs, with many changes merged without human review, increasing the risk of quality issues.
- Top tech companies like Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, and Uber are all-in on AI agents, but they often need to rebuild internal developer infrastructure to integrate agents effectively, as Uber did.
- Engineers should adapt by focusing on code review, taste, and learning to work alongside AI agents, as the landscape has changed irreversibly in just six months.
