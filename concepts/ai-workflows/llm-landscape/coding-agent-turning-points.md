# 2026 in LLMs: Three Turning Points for Coding Agents

Simon Willison reviews the last 12 months of LLM development through three turning points. The first came in November 2025, when Claude Opus 4.5 and GPT 5.1 made coding agents reliable enough for everyday use, shifting them from a curiosity that made weird mistakes to a dependable daily tool. He credits the timing with letting developers spend December holidays testing the new models, so that by January many realized everything had changed and this would be the main theme of 2026.

- November 2025 was a watershed for coding agents: Claude Opus 4.5 and GPT 5.1 crossed the threshold from curiosity to everyday reliability
- Willison's January 2026 prediction was that it would become undeniable that LLMs write quality code, countering the belief that every line must be checked manually
- He uses SVG drawings of pelicans on bicycles as a model evaluation benchmark, noting early models produced bikes that didn't work
- He coined 'deep sadness' for the existential crisis developers feel when models can do what makes them unique, faster and sometimes better
- He predicted a major prompt-injection incident would hit front pages in 2026, after predicting it for roughly four years running
