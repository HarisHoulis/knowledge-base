# Why Compute Still Trades in Group Chats

Affaan Mustafa and Alejandro Soumah Fernandez of Jump Trading describe how computing resources are traded today as an unlit, over-the-counter market. Most concluded contracts for fixed capacity over a term are negotiated privately through Slack, X, and Telegram group chats rather than on any widely adopted public exchange, with agreements typically combining an MSA, an SLA, and a cluster-specific order form [source]. They call these 'dark pools of computing resources' by analogy to finance, noting the parallel to commodity chats like ICE and Bloomberg chat.

On the macro side, they point to falling costs of a given capacity, while noting cost still depends on model usage, capacity, and speed. Cheaper tokens unlock latent demand: teams consume far more tokens for the same budget, and previously too-expensive programs become viable. But the underlying capacity still must be created and paid for, so they distinguish the price of intelligence and the number of tasks people want to perform from the amount of equipment needed to serve them [source].

They invoke Jevons' paradox to explain an observed decoupling: token prices dropped further while computing prices stayed stable, because cheaper tasks encourage more consumption. The macro structure is described as bare bones — chips (H100, H200, B300), capacity, buildings, grid connections, and capital all must come together before compute can be delivered [source].

- Most compute capacity trades are private, bilateral, long-term contracts concluded in Slack, X, and Telegram chats rather than on public exchanges.
- Contracts typically combine an MSA, an SLA, and a cluster-specific order form with hardware-specific wording.
- Falling token prices expand usage (Jevons' paradox), yet underlying compute capacity must still be built and paid for.
- Token prices and compute prices have decoupled, with compute prices remaining stable as token prices fell.
- The compute market's macro structure spans chips, capacity, buildings, grid connections, and capital.
