# Why AI Startups Need Compute Markets, Not Just Clouds

Evan Conrad of San Francisco Compute argues that building a frontier AI lab is an economic nightmare rooted in how compute is bought and financed. His company runs a physically settled forward order market for compute: it acts as both equipment operator (like a cloud) and marketplace, letting capacity be reserved and purchased now or in the future, akin to a grid for calculations. The claim is that problems start at the design stage of a supercomputer—the bill of materials, bare-metal orchestration, and services on top—so data-center owners who try to become GPU clouds without doing the full stack efficiently will ultimately fail their customers.

- San Francisco Compute combines equipment operation with a forward order book for reserving compute capacity now or in the future.
- Running a supercomputer requires efficiency across the whole stack—design, bill of materials, bare-metal orchestration, and services—or customers suffer.
- The company pivoted from a media-generation startup (Junlark) after being forced to sublease its cluster, becoming effectively the first GPU broker to avoid bankruptcy.
- Creating a compute market requires solving two hard problems: the cold start of a market and building a cloud without becoming the cloud.
- Startups face compute scarcity with long lead times and must pay large deposits upfront because lenders cannot underwrite AGI outcomes.
