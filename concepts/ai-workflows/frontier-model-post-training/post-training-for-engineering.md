# Post-Training Gemini 4 for Real Engineering Work

Melvin Johnson, who leads the Gemini post-training program at Google DeepMind, describes how Gemini 4 was retrained for real engineering tasks. He frames the release cadence as a turnaround: after 236 days without a leading model, Google shipped two advanced models in 28 days, a roughly tenfold improvement in delivery speed, and he notes that external criticism (citing Ethan Mollick's remark about a long break between releases) helped push the team to act. The Gemini 4 series spans Carbon, a cutting-edge model for deep intelligence, and Hydrogen, a fast model for everyday tasks that runs across platforms including the Gemini API, the Gemini app, and Search.

- Gemini 4 series splits into Carbon (deep intelligence) and Hydrogen (fast, broadly available)
- Carbon reportedly ranks first on Cursor Bench, Perplexity's Wander, and Zapier's Automation Bench while being substantially cheaper than the next-best model
- AI writes about 75% of Google's new code, up from under 25% a few years ago, with 100,000 engineers using anti-gravity daily
- Post-training for engineering relies on hard tasks, experienced-engineer-style evaluation, and large-scale RL cycled through internal testing where failures seed the next iteration
