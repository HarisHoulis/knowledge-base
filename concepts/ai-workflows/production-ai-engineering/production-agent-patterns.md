# Production Patterns for Agents, Evals, and LLM Costs: QCon AI New York 2026

QCon AI New York 2026 (December 15-16) has confirmed sessions from engineers sharing how they handle the constraints that emerge once AI reaches production. The article frames four recurring pressures: code arriving faster than it can be reviewed, rising token costs, evals that must be good enough to act on, and agents that need permissions and guardrails.

Rather than treating these as separate concerns, the piece presents them as the practical realities of operating AI systems in production. The conference sessions are positioned as first-hand accounts from engineers dealing with these constraints, rather than abstract theory.

The announcement is a preview of the event's session lineup, with the central message that production AI work is defined by review throughput, cost management, evaluation quality, and agent safety.

- Once AI is in production, code arrives faster than it can be reviewed.
- Token costs rise and must be actively managed.
- Evals have to be good enough to act on.
- Agents require permissions and guardrails.
