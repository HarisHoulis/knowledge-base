# How a Remote Company Builds AI Fluency — Em Shreve, Automattic

Em Shreve of Automattic describes how the fully distributed company (over 1,400 people in 82 countries) builds AI fluency rather than simply handing out tools. The core belief is that quality training and results require giving people real time to learn and experiment, so Automattic runs two-week in-person immersion programs split 50/50 between guided learning and real project work. The guided half is delivered by the Learning and Development team and AI Implementation team, bringing in internal experts for seminars and hands-on training, while the project half has employees bring real tasks, tools, dashboards, or new skills and MCPs they want to build.

The engineering curriculum starts with basics, skills and how to build them, MCP and spec-based development, and responsible/conscious AI use, then moves to more complex topics: creating MCPs for new functionality, the Codex and Claude hook systems, autonomous agents, and background procedures. It wraps with applied topics such as applied AI in WordPress and building a personal assistant. Shreve stresses that the curriculum cannot be written once because AI changes week to week and the best model shifts with each release, so it must be constantly updated to avoid teaching something six months out of date. Automattic also runs its own internal MCP server, Context Automattic, to quickly add toolkits and layers of governance and observability.

The program aims for multipliers, not just attendees: every person sent should spread knowledge to those the program cannot yet reach. To date this year Automattic has run seven cohorts of roughly 45 people each, targeting about 600 people by year end — around two-thirds of engineers plus other focus areas. Reach is expanded through a guide/champion program, local AI meetings held by program graduates, and cross-functional and non-technical formats including workshops for finance, management, and other departments.
