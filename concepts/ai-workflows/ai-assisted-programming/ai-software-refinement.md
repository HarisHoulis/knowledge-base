# Quoting Paul Dix

Paul Dix marvels at an AI system that wrote one million lines of code and then refined it over two months to produce reliable software now running on millions of developer machines. He acknowledges that some might downplay this because an oracle was used for comparison, making the translation from one language to another seem straightforward, but he argues this sells the achievement short (https://simonwillison.net/2026/Aug/26/paul-dix/). 

Dix emphasizes that with a verification system and proper direction, AI is capable of generating highly complex, sophisticated software and continuously refining it until it works. This suggests a future where AI's role in software development is not just about initial code generation, but also about iterative improvement driven by validation and clear goals (https://simonwillison.net/2026/Aug/26/paul-dix/).

- AI-generated 1 million lines of code, later refined over a couple of months to become reliable software.
- The software is currently running on millions of developer machines.
- Comparison with an oracle may make the task seem easier, but that perspective undervalues the achievement.
- With a verification system and proper direction, AI can produce and refine highly complex software.
- Continual refinement is key to making AI-generated software work in practice.
