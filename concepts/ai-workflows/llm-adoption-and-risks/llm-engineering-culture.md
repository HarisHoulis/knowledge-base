# Fragments: July 21

Martin Fowler's fragment covers key findings from the Future of Software Development Retreat, including the shift from code generation to verification as the bottleneck, the emergence of harness engineering, an apprenticeship crisis, the executive/engineer expectation gap, and legacy modernization as a near-term value pool. He highlights the disconnect between boards pushing LLM adoption and engineers wary of security risks, illustrated by a cautionary tale where an ML model optimized for desert equipment caused a $100 billion fire loss when applied in the arctic due to decaying mosquitoes. Fowler emphasizes the need for sensors and feedback loops, and discusses the challenges of citizen developers 'vibe coding', suggesting controls and separate infrastructure to mitigate shadow IT and the 'lethal trifecta' of security risks.

The fragment also examines the bubble mentality surrounding AI, noting that unlike the dotcom era, there is less visible innovation and more wariness, with cost-cutting as the primary driver. For operations, LLMs are seen as helpful for analyzing event streams and assisting human teams, but auto-remediation requires careful documentation and feedback to development teams. Fowler cautions against overestimating agent capabilities for incident resolution, which is often non-linear and requires human adaptability.

Finally, Fowler discusses the growing negative reaction to 'LLM-speak' in writing, citing a law professors' study where LLM answers were preferred over human peer answers, and pointing to DSLs as a promising way to make LLMs more reliable and token-efficient. He advises writers to 'Say Your Writing' to combat the homogenizing effect of AI on prose and to preserve a distinctive human voice.

- Verification, not code generation, is the current bottleneck in AI-assisted software development.
- The executive/engineer expectation gap on LLM risks is a serious organizational risk, illustrated by a costly context-mismatch failure.
- Citizen developers using vibe coding create shadow IT, requiring controls, separate infrastructure, and careful governance.
- LLMs are useful in operations for anomaly detection and incident understanding, but auto-remediation needs guardrails and human oversight.
- A distinct human writing voice is becoming more valuable as AI-generated prose triggers reader 'LLM-antibodies'; reading aloud helps.
