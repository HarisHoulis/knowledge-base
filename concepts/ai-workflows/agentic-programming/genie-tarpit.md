# Fragments: May 5, 2026

Martin Fowler's May 2026 fragments survey the state of AI-assisted programming. Rahul Garg's Lattice framework operationalizes patterns to reduce AI friction by embedding composable skills—atoms, molecules, refiners—and a living context layer that accumulates project standards and decisions, making AI coding assistants apply a team's own rules over time. Fowler also highlights a double feedback loop observed by Jessica Kerr: developers use AI to change the software and, in parallel, adjust the AI tooling itself—a return to the 'Internal Reprogrammability' joy found in Smalltalk and Lisp cultures.

- Lattice provides structured, composable skills (atoms, molecules, refiners) plus a project context layer to embed engineering disciplines into AI coding workflows.
- AI-assisted development involves a double feedback loop: changing the product and changing the tooling, with frustration as a signal to improve the environment.
- Google's AI Overview defamation lawsuit underscores that companies must take responsibility for AI-generated content that causes real harm.
- Big tech AI infrastructure spending is staggering—over 50% of revenue at Amazon/Alphabet/Microsoft, 75%+ at Meta/Oracle—while Apple's ~10% spend suggests a bet on local, on-device AI.
- Kent Beck's 'Genie Tarpit' questions whether AI coding tools can maintain internal software quality or will sink under accumulating complexity.
