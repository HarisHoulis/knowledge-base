# The Sweet Side of the Bitter Lesson

In this talk, Thariq Shihipar reflects on his time at Anthropic and revisits predictions he made about AI-assisted software development, arguing that the feared "bitter lesson"—where alien, uncontrollable model behavior takes over—did not play out as expected. He admits several things he got wrong: the belief that "Bash is all you need" (he now prefers MCP, code modes, skills, and gradual feature disclosure over pure CLI interaction), the belief that "React is all you need" (models now write competently in HTML, Swift, Figma JSON, and custom languages), and the fear that diffusion models would "eat" art (LLMs now create art through code by controlling tools like Blender, Photoshop, and Figma).

His central question is how to develop AI while remaining human-centric. He frames developers as the first line of defense and the forefront of AI implementation, since how programmers interact with AI will shape its effect on other intellectual labor. He describes AI as simultaneously an industrial revolution—software factories turning development from an unreliable craft into a non-limiting factor—and a Renaissance, where creativity, utility, and soul put into software remain economically valuable.

He introduces the idea of a "toothed edge" of capability, the jagged frontier visible in benchmarks that has grown from writing a function to building a Slack clone or a Mario Kart port, alongside a "smooth interior space" representing what models can reliably do as they improve.

- The feared "bitter lesson" of alien, uncontrollable models did not materialize; models instead work through human tools and formats.
- Bash-only and React-only assumptions were wrong—agents now use MCP, skills, code modes, and many languages and formats.
- AI is both an industrial revolution (software factories) and a Renaissance where human creativity remains economically valuable.
- The "toothed edge" of model capability keeps expanding, from simple functions to full applications and ports.
- Developers are the first line of defense and their AI interaction patterns will shape other sectors.
