# Climbing the Ladder of Trust with Coding Agents

In a conversation with Matt Pocock, Poteto (creator of pstack) describes how his approach to AI coding agents evolved from micromanaging a single agent to scaling to thousands of merge requests per month. His journey began before joining Cursor (now SpaceX AI), after leaving Meta's React team. While building a side project, he realized he was spending all his time micromanaging one agent, creating skills whose impact he could not assess, and iterating blindly. This project later became the basis of pstack, and the techniques he learned—particularly around verification—remained relevant when he later worked on the agent window and Grokbot.

The core insight is what he calls the "ladder of trust": as you trust agents more, you can assign them more complex tasks or scale up using more agents. At Cursor, he again found himself acting as a "meat proxy"—the intermediary between his agent and Chrome DevTools—which frustrated him. He emphasizes that agents, even the most advanced ones, tend to take the path of least resistance and choose the easy way out. Much of his skill development focused on making the easy way the right way, through meticulous verification and teaching agents to execute workflows like he would.

His talk, "How I sent 2,500 merge requests (PR) to production last month," went viral with about 3 million views, resonating with the growing interest in software factories, improving work quality, increasing speed, and climbing the ladder of trust with agents so teams can release more products.
