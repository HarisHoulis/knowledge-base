# Am I Holding This Right? A Solo Developer's Skeptical Take on LLM-Assisted Coding

Daniel Terhorst-North opens by situating himself as a long-time software practitioner ("thirty-something" years) who began using Claude in early 2025 and still partly subscribes to Emily Bender's "stochastic parrot" critique—that LLMs produce plausible-sounding output without understanding. He stresses that this critique doesn't negate the tools' value, comparing it to driving a modern car without understanding its internals: you can still use it effectively.

- The speaker remains partly convinced by the "stochastic parrot" framing of LLMs but argues they can still deliver real benefits
- He contrasts his own experience with claims that LLMs make developers write code faster than ever
- Coding is framed as not the bottleneck of software development; business impact is what matters
- He cites data suggesting heavy generative-AI spend often yields only marginal gains in shipped product
- He cares about code's impact and future changeability, not code itself
