# My AI Employees Won't Let Me Sleep

Kent C. Dodds describes how removing every reason for coding agents to wait on him turned into a personal productivity trap. By moving to cloud agents (Cursor, Devon, and similar) that run simultaneously without his laptop, can be launched repeatedly without interfering with each other, and can be checked from his phone, his laptop stopped being a bottleneck—but his time was not freed up.

The root cause is that his agents became extremely capable and highly autonomous, wired through Cody so they can connect to everything, fetch the data they need, and perform secure actions he has permitted. The result is that he is left with only decisions to make: he has become the boss, but unlike a human boss he feels free to message his workers at any hour, because the agents don't care if he texts them at 2 a.m. with a new idea.

Previously a middle-of-the-night idea could be written down and evaluated in the morning; now it can be handed to an agent that does the job immediately and comes back with feedback and follow-up questions, so the process never stops. With no restrictions on the agents, the only remaining limitation is him—and that difference is the real problem. He frames the cost in personal terms, including a moment at an amusement park with his kids where he got separated from them because he was sending something to his agents, and he says he is building a solution on camera.

- Cloud agents removed the laptop bottleneck by running in parallel and being reachable from a phone, but did not free up the author's time.
- Highly autonomous, well-connected agents reduce the human's role to answering a few questions—which then never end.
- Because agents don't need rest, the author treats them like employees he can message at 2 a.m., eroding boundaries.
- The only remaining limitation in the loop is the human, which the author identifies as the core problem.
- The cost is personal: he got separated from his kids at an amusement park while dispatching work to agents.
