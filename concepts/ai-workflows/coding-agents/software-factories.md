# Software Factories: Escape from Trash Mountain (Steve Yegge, Gas Town)

Steve Yegge recounts a year of building "software factories" — orchestrators in which LLMs direct other LLMs to do engineering work. His first attempts (Vibe Coder and VC) were "complete failure[s]" built as huge monoliths, teaching him the bitter lesson: don't try to make LLMs smarter, since they will become smarter themselves. Out of that work came the "beads" discovery, followed by Gas Town (launched January 1st) and later Wheelhouse, a factory built from scratch to work on his own video game rather than on factories themselves.

- Don't try to make LLMs smarter; they will become smarter themselves (the bitter lesson).
- Every model generation builds something too complicated for itself, and the next generation has to burn it down.
- The "facehugger pattern": the factory becomes a parasite on the product, with players and admins interacting directly with it.
- A factory produced 50-100 features per day — roughly 60 years of backlog work in four months — far outpacing human review capacity.
