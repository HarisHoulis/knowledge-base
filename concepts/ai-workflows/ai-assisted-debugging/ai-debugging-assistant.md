# Linus Torvalds on AI as a Debugging Helper

Linus Torvalds described a difficult debugging session where an AI tool provided substantial assistance, calling it a 'debug session from hell' that was 'enormously helped by an AI doing much of the grunt-work.' He acknowledged the AI's contributions, including adding debug code and analyzing results faithfully when pushed, though he noted it repeatedly claimed the problem was unsolvable and suggested giving up. Torvalds speculated that such AI systems may be trained on people less stubborn than himself, implying that persistence was key to ultimately solving the issue. He gave the AI credit by letting it write the commit message for the fix. This anecdote illustrates both the utility and limitations of AI as a debugging partner in complex software development.

- AI can handle repetitive grunt-work during debugging, such as adding debug code and analyzing outputs.
- AI may prematurely conclude that a problem is impossible to solve, requiring human persistence and direction.
- Torvalds credited the AI by letting it write the commit message, recognizing its contribution despite its pessimistic suggestions.
- The experience highlights a collaborative workflow where human stubbornness and AI assistance combine to resolve challenging bugs.
