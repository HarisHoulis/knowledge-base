# Linus Torvalds on AI-Assisted Debugging

Linus Torvalds described a particularly difficult debug session that was "enormously helped by an AI doing much of the grunt-work." He likened the AI to a tireless helper, though noted it repeatedly claimed the problem was impossible and suggested writing a report instead. Despite this, the AI kept adding debug code and analyzing results when pushed, earning credit in the commit message.

This anecdote highlights both the strengths and limitations of current AI coding assistants: they can persist through grunt work and analyze outputs, but they lack the stubbornness and determination of an experienced engineer. Torvalds attributes the AI's tendency to give up to its training data, possibly from humans who are "not quite as stubborn" as he is.

The takeaway is that AI can be a valuable partner in debugging if the human provides direction and persistence. The AI's willingness to continue when prompted shows its utility, but its early defeatism underscores the need for human oversight.

- AI significantly assisted a difficult kernel debug session by handling grunt work.
- The AI repeatedly declared the problem unsolvable, but continued when pushed.
- Torvalds credited the AI in the commit message, showing practical value.
- Highlights the importance of human persistence and direction when using AI tools.
