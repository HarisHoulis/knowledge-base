# /wayfinder: Nothing is too big to plan anymore

Matt Pocock introduces Wayfinder, a skill for planning large-scale work with AI agents. He argues that existing planning tools, including his earlier 'grill me' or 'grill with doc' skills, are too tied to a single session and limited by the agent's context window. This forces developers to constrain their ambitions to fit AI rather than planning the full scope of work. Wayfinder removes that limitation by orchestrating planning across multiple sessions, enabling ambitious projects to be tackled without being bounded by a single conversation (Pocock, 2026).

Wayfinder conceptualizes planning as map-making: you have a start point and a vague destination, with fog in between. It creates tickets—each requiring its own session—such as prototyping, grilling, or research. It tracks a 'frontier' of known decisions and keeps everything still in fog unresolved until enough information is gathered. It can also manage non-research tasks like configuration or errands, and it supports dependent decisions and parallel planning. The approach is based on pre-AI software planning fundamentals and works with any coding agent. Early users report strong results, including one-shotted prototypes and even a custom harness built around the approach (Pocock, 2026).

- Wayfinder enables planning large chunks of work that exceed a single agent context window.
- It orchestrates multiple session types—grilling, prototyping, research—into a coherent map.
- The map tracks a frontier of known decisions and leaves unresolved items in 'fog of war' until they can be decided.
- It supports dependent decisions and parallel planning, and works with any coding agent.
- Based on pre-AI software planning fundamentals, not a AI-specific constraint.
