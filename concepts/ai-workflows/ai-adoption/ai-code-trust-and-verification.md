# Stop Being Skeptical About AI for Development with Charity Majors

In this interview, Charity Majors, CTO of Honeycomb, explains why she shifted from skepticism to believing AI is a generational change for software engineering. She marks November 2025 as her turning point, driven by Opus 4.5 and especially the Claude Code harness, which turned coding harnesses from shell scripts into serious infrastructure. She compares AI's 2025 impact to that of cloud computing in 2010, noting that while early skepticism was justified by past overhyped technologies like COBOL and no-code tools, the current trajectory is unmistakable (Orosz, 2026).

Majors argues that professional engineers will soon ship code they have never read, and the industry's job is to build systems that validate such code. She draws a parallel to the 'pets to cattle' shift in infrastructure: instead of fixing flawed code, teams will generate new code that is verifyably correct. She also offers contrarian views, calling code review overrated and asserting that 20 years of DevOps failed because engineers still lack a true feedback loop from production. With AI-generated code, she says, engineering discipline must increase, especially around validation via tests, evals, and conformance testing.

Her career advice for engineering directors is to 'run towards the waves' and get AI experience immediately, warning that future job filters will exclude those without it. She suggests that anxious leaders consider returning to individual contributor roles for more agency. On AI fatigue, she recommends small acts of control, like Honeycomb's no-AI Wednesdays. Finally, she urges both AI enthusiasts and skeptics to tell the whole story, acknowledging the wins and the costs, so that everyone can navigate the change together (Orosz, 2026).

- AI's impact in 2025 is comparable to cloud computing in 2010, marking a generational shift in software engineering.
- Skepticism was historically justified, but the rise of coding harnesses like Claude Code has made AI indispensable.
- Engineers will increasingly ship code they haven't read; the bottleneck becomes verification and reliability engineering.
- Code review is overrated; humans add more value in deciding what to build than in reading code for correctness.
- DevOps has failed, and AI-generated code demands more engineering discipline, especially in validation and observability.
