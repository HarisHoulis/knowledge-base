# 2025 was for AI what 2010 was for cloud

Charity Majors draws a direct parallel between the adoption of AI in 2025 and the adoption of cloud computing around 2010. She recalls how at Linden Lab in 2006, the cloud was initially an experimental sideline used for overflow storage and testing, but by 2010 it had become the mainstream foundation of engineering. Similarly, she argues that AI has shifted from being a satellite, hype-driven technology to the core of developer tools and infrastructure, even though a speculative bubble remains (Majors, 2025).

Majors acknowledges the widespread cynicism toward AI, comparing it to past hype cycles like dotcom, blockchain, and metaverse. However, she insists that the existence of froth does not disprove the existence of value. She and Fred Hebert previously urged SREs to engage with AI seriously rather than dismiss it, and eight months later her own perspective has evolved to see AI as already foundational. The essay is a call for engineers to embrace the shift and contribute their realism and risk-awareness to shape AI's integration (Majors, 2025).

- AI in 2025 has become as central to mainstream engineering as cloud computing was in 2010, not just an experimental add-on.
- Hype bubbles are common with transformative tech; AI's bubble doesn't negate its underlying value.
- Engineers, especially SREs, should engage with AI from an informed, risk-aware perspective rather than dismiss it.
- The shift is particularly evident in developer tools, where AI is now foundational infrastructure.
