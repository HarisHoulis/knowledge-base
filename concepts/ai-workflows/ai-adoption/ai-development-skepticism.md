# Stop being skeptical about AI for development with Charity Majors

In this episode of The Pragmatic Engineer, Charity Majors, CTO of Honeycomb, explains how her perspective on AI for software development evolved from skepticism in early 2025 to seeing it as a generational change by November 2025. The turning point was Opus 4.5, but she emphasizes that the coding harness, specifically Claude Code, made the bigger difference by turning harnesses from shell scripts into serious infrastructure (Orosz, 2026). She compares AI's impact on the software industry to the cloud in 2010: a shift that was initially met with skepticism but is now clearly foundational. Majors acknowledges that skeptics had good reason to doubt AI, citing past technologies like COBOL, neural nets, and no-code tools that promised to transform software development but fell short. However, she argues that AI is different because it changes the economics of code generation, making it possible to generate hundreds of variants of a function faster than hand-writing one. This could lead to a 'pets to cattle' transition in code, where problems are solved by generating and verifying new code rather than fixing existing code manually. The critical question becomes: what would it take for engineers to be comfortable shipping code they have not read? Majors believes it is a 'when,' not an 'if,' and that the engineering profession must build systems to validate and verify AI-generated code. She offers contrarian takes: code review is overrated and the least valuable part of human contribution; DevOps failed to connect software engineers to production; and non-deterministic AI-generated code requires more engineering discipline at the validation stage, not less. For engineering directors, she advises running toward AI and getting AI experience on resumes immediately, even suggesting that those feeling anxious should consider returning to individual contributor roles for more agency. On AI fatigue, she recommends small acts of control, such as the 'no AI on Wednesdays' practice at Honeycomb. Finally, she calls on both AI enthusiasts and skeptics to tell the whole story, including costs and wins, and shares a personal rule: never send a message to a human that you have not read in full (Orosz, 2026).

- Charity Majors' turning point was in November 2025, crediting Opus 4.5 and the Claude Code harness for making AI a generational change in software engineering.
- AI's impact on software is compared to the cloud in 2010; code may shift from 'pets' to 'cattle' as generation and verification replace manual fixes.
- Engineers will eventually ship code they haven't read; the bottleneck becomes validation and reliability, not code generation.
- Code review is overrated and DevOps failed; AI-generated non-deterministic code demands more engineering discipline, not less.
- Advice: gain AI experience immediately, take small acts of control against AI fatigue (e.g., no-AI Wednesdays), and both AI camps should share costs and wins honestly.
