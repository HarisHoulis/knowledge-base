# Stop being skeptical about AI for development with Charity Majors

In a wide-ranging conversation, Charity Majors explains why skepticism about AI in software development was rational up to 2025, but became increasingly untenable after November 2025. Her turning point came from using Opus 4.5 combined with Claude Code, which transformed AI coding harnesses from shell scripts into serious infrastructure. She compares AI's industry impact in 2025 to cloud computing in 2010: a clear inflection point after which mainstream adoption and structural change became inevitable (Orosz, 2026). She also notes that past promises like COBOL, neural nets, and no-code tools legitimately justified skepticism, but the current wave is different because of the capability leap in coding harnesses.

- AI moved from a productivity aid to a generational shift in late 2025, driven by improved models like Opus 4.5 and mature harnesses like Claude Code.
- Engineers need to confront the question of shipping code they haven't read; the bottleneck is shifting from writing code to validating and verifying it.
- Code review is overrated; humans add the most value in conversations and deciding what to build, not in manually checking syntax and bugs.
- Non-deterministic AI-generated code requires more engineering discipline, especially in testing, evals, and conformance checks.
- Engineering leaders should gain AI experience now, consider returning to IC work for more agency, and manage AI fatigue with small acts of control like no-AI Wednesdays.
