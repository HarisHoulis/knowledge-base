# Stop Being Skeptical About AI for Development with Charity Majors

Charity Majors, CTO of Honeycomb, argues that skepticism about AI in software development was rational in 2025, but by 2026 it is clear AI is transforming the industry. Her turning point came in November 2025 with Opus 4.5 and, more importantly, Claude Code, which turned coding harnesses from shell scripts into serious infrastructure. She compares AI's impact in 2025 to cloud computing's impact in 2010, suggesting that AI will fundamentally change the infrastructure of building software (Orosz, 2026).

Majors predicts that software engineers will eventually ship code they have not read, making verification the key bottleneck. She draws a parallel to the 'pets to cattle' transition in infrastructure: instead of debugging individual servers, teams recreate them via Terraform and Kubernetes. Similarly, code may move from being carefully edited to being regenerated and verifiably correct. She also shares contrarian views: code review is overrated, and 20 years of DevOps have failed to connect engineers to production (Orosz, 2026).

AI-written, non-deterministic systems demand more engineering discipline, especially in validation through tests, evals, and conformance testing. Majors advises engineering directors to 'run towards the waves' and gain AI experience, noting that future job interviews will filter out those without it. She also suggests taking back control from AI with small acts, like Honeycomb's no-AI Wednesdays, and asks both AI enthusiasts and skeptics to tell the full story, including costs (Orosz, 2026).

- AI skepticism was justified until 2025, but by 2026 AI is a generational change comparable to cloud computing in 2010.
- The critical question is when, not if, engineers will ship code they haven't read; validation systems must be built to handle this.
- Code is becoming 'cattle'—generating new, verifiably correct code will replace hand-fixing bugs, following the infrastructure 'pets to cattle' model.
- Code review is overrated, and DevOps has failed to create a true feedback loop between engineers and production.
- To stay relevant, professionals should gain AI experience, consider IC roles for agency, and combat AI fatigue with small acts like no-AI Wednesdays.
