# Why AI Agents Still Won't Pay for Anything

Jan Curn of Apify frames agentic payments as a hot but still-unresolved space: despite a proliferation of payment protocols, agents largely don't pay yet. Apify runs a marketplace of roughly 80,000 AI tools ('actors') built by a global creator community, and paid almost $2 million to creators last month, so enabling agents to buy tools is directly valuable to the business. Curn notes he was the only speaker on agent payments at the prior year's AI Engineer World Fair, whereas this year the conference had a dedicated track, reflecting a year and a half of rapid progress.

The core argument is that agents will eventually need budgets to execute increasingly long-term autonomous tasks, since otherwise they must stop and ask for approval. Curn cites a prediction (attributed to 'Guard') that by 2028 the agent economy will reach $15 trillion, with 80% of B2B activity between companies done autonomously by agents — a figure he treats skeptically but as indicative of the opportunity drawing fintech and financial players in.

Much of the talk catalogs the resulting protocol landmine: L42 from Lightning Labs (mid-2020), Mastercard's agent payments (April), Coinbase's X42 (May 2025, described as the most popular current standard), KYA Pay and Skyfire from Visa (June), Google's AP2, OpenAI and Stripe's ACP (September), Visa's TAP (October), Google and Shopify's UCP (January), Alipay's ACTP and Stripe/Tempo's MPP, Alipay's MMP, UnionPay's APOP, and OKX's agent payment protocol. Curn argues this has become a battleground for the future of agent e-commerce, with each player trying to leverage its existing network and infrastructure.

Curn suggests cryptocurrency has a strong chance of winning the agent fee war because, done right, it has no single owner controlling the ecosystem, unlike credit cards, PayPal, or bank debit. He argues these traditional methods are far cheaper to beat for small agent payments, though he notes he owns no cryptocurrency himself.

- Agents will need budgets to act autonomously on long-running tasks rather than pausing for human approval.
- A crowded field of competing standards (X42, AP2, ACP, TAP, UCP, MPP, and others) has emerged from card networks, crypto firms, and big tech in roughly 18 months.
- Apify's marketplace of ~80,000 creator-built actors paid nearly $2 million to creators last month, making agent purchasing a direct business interest.
- Curn argues crypto has an edge for agent payments because no single company owns the ecosystem and fees undercut cards, PayPal, or bank debit for small transactions.
