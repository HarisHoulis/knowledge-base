# What It's Like Inside a Company Staffed Almost Entirely by AI

Journalist Evan Ratliff describes his immersive-journalism experiments with AI agents, framing them with the provocation that "it's okay to laugh at AI agents" — the technology is powerful and potentially dangerous, but also absurd, and we adapt to it so quickly that we stop noticing how strange it is that we can talk to these creatures and let them act on their own in the world. He traces this back to his earlier work covering technology's dark side and his preference for putting himself in extreme situations to understand how technology unfolds.

His first experiment, the Shell Game, involved creating a professional voice clone of himself, connecting it to a voice agent built by a law graduate, and wiring it to his own cell phone line. The clone then called and took calls from scammers, spammers, telemarketers, friends, family, his therapist, and colleagues — even conducting interviews on his behalf — at a time when most people didn't realize chatbots could be used that way. He illustrates the effect with a recording of his clone calling his friend Warren about a football match; Warren only realized mid-conversation that he was talking to a clone.

By 2025 Ratliff moved to a new experiment, motivated by the growing talk of AI agents and the sense that 2025 was "the year of the agent" — the basis for the talk's premise of a company staffed almost entirely by AI.

- Ratliff argues it's fine to laugh at AI agents: they are serious, potentially dangerous technology, but also absurd — and we adapt to them too quickly to notice.
