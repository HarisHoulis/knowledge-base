# Stop being skeptical about AI for development with Charity Majors

Charity Majors, CTO of Honeycomb, has evolved from a cautious AI skeptic in early 2025 to a believer that AI is a generational change for software engineering, comparable to the cloud's impact in 2010. Her turning point came in November 2025 with Opus 4.5 and the maturation of coding harnesses like Claude Code, which shifted AI from a simple script to serious infrastructure. She acknowledges that skeptics had valid reasons based on past overhyped technologies like COBOL and low-code tools, but argues that the industry is now heading toward a future where engineers will ship code they have never read, making verification systems the new bottleneck (Orosz, 2026).

- AI's impact on software engineering in 2025 is similar to cloud computing's impact in 2010, marking a generational shift.
- The economics of code generation are changing: generating many variants of code is becoming faster than hand-writing once, potentially leading to a 'pets to cattle' transition for code.
- Code review is overrated; humans add more value in conversations and deciding what to build than in reading code for correctness.
- Non-deterministic AI-generated systems require more engineering discipline, especially around testing, evals, and conformance verification.
- Engineering leaders should urgently gain AI experience and consider returning to IC work for more agency, as future job interviews will filter out those without AI expertise.
