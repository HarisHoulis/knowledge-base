# Why AI Won't Replace Stock Pickers

Jack Woodruff, founder and former CIO of Candlestick Capital Management (previously a portfolio manager at Citadel's Surveyor Capital), argues that AI will reshape but not replace fundamental stock picking. Long-term stock selection, he says, has always relied on assembling a mosaic of data to form a non-standard view, and that search for alpha will not change; what changes is how information is synthesized now that basic knowledge is universally accessible. He expects the traditional portfolio manager and analyst roles to be "completely turned on their head," though he considers it too early to say exactly how.

Woodruff frames investment management as an exercise in time management: attention is the bottleneck, and returns diminish as time is spread across more stocks. AI can compress preparatory work—turning six to eight hours into five minutes—freeing time for more research, channel checks, and industry analysis in pursuit of divergent views. But he rejects the idea that this leads to simply cutting headcount: in a highly competitive industry, someone else will still work sixteen-hour days hunting for an edge, so freed time gets reinvested rather than banked.

He also pushes back on the notion that junior analysts will stop reading 10-Ks and transcripts. Deep work on a topic still requires personally processing information; AI summaries reduce time but don't remove the need to learn. Routine data gathering and cleaning can be outsourced to AI, and agents can guide analysts toward what is worth working on—so the winners will be those who use these tools to allocate time toward finding alternative views.

- Long-term stock selection and the search for a non-standard, mosaic-based view won't change; only how information is synthesized will.
- Investment management is time management—AI compresses prep work so analysts can spend more time on research and divergent views.
- Efficiency gains won't mean fewer analysts, since competitors will reinvest freed time into finding an edge.
- Junior analysts will still need to read filings and transcripts; AI summaries reduce time but don't replace deep learning.
- Data cleaning and validation can be outsourced to AI, with agents guiding analysts toward the right work.
