# The Missing Layer: Design Taste in AI Agents

Hassan El Mghari, a developer experience lead at Together AI, argues that design and UX are the number one reason his AI apps have reached millions of users. He builds roughly 10 apps a year despite not being a professional designer, emphasizing that polished UI sets successful AI apps apart from the growing sea of generic 'vibe coded' applications. He notes that adding just 10 to 20% extra effort focused on UI is a significant competitive advantage in the current AI landscape (El Mghari, 2026).

The talk identifies common visual 'tells' of AI-generated apps, which he collectively calls 'AI slop.' These include purple gradient backgrounds, italicized headers, 'scroll to explore' cues, all-caps pills with wide letter spacing, gradient logos, and excessive emoji use. He suggests these patterns are so ubiquitous that they make AI apps look identical and low-effort, deterring users (El Mghari, 2026).

To address this, El Mghari created a design skill called 'Hallmark.' Hallmark codifies these AI slop patterns and instructs AI models to avoid them, effectively teaching models what not to do. In addition, Hallmark provides a collection of distinct design themes to help generate more original and visually appealing interfaces, steering AI output toward more distinctive and user-friendly designs (El Mghari, 2026).

- Design and UX are critical differentiators: a small 10-20% extra UI effort gives AI apps a major competitive edge.
- Common 'AI slop' patterns include purple gradients, italic headers, scroll-to-explore, all-caps pills, gradient logos, and emojis.
- Hallmark is a design skill that codifies anti-patterns (slop gates) and offers alternative themes to prevent generic AI-generated UIs.
- Vibe-coded apps are recognizable by repetitive visual tells, making them feel low-effort and indistinct.
