# Making Machine Learning Environmentally Sustainable

Charles Humble's GOTO Copenhagen 2026 talk examines the largely hidden environmental cost of AI's rapid growth, noting that Microsoft and Google have both reported carbon emissions surging by 30-50% in recent years, driven primarily by AI infrastructure. He frames the problem across the AI lifecycle, from development and cost through data collection, training, deployment, and maintenance.

The talk offers practical strategies for reducing impact at each stage. These include demand shifting and shaping (choosing where and when workloads run), model compression techniques such as pruning, distillation, and quantisation, federated learning, and speculative decoding. Humble argues that choosing where and when to run workloads can reduce software carbon emissions by up to 99%.

Beyond technical tactics, he addresses business-level planning, including making AI green during project planning and training on renewable energy, and closes with four steps for making all computing greener.

- Microsoft and Google reported carbon emissions rising 30-50% in recent years, driven primarily by AI infrastructure
- Model compression via pruning, distillation, and quantisation reduces the environmental cost of LLMs
- Demand shifting and shaping—choosing where and when workloads run—can cut software carbon emissions by up to 99%
- Other levers include federated learning, speculative decoding, and training on renewable energy
- Sustainability should be considered at every lifecycle stage, from project planning through deployment and maintenance
