# The Signal Layer: What to Build When Anything Can Be Built — Lena Hall, Akamai

The rest of the talk traces how signal survives the trip to a customer. Hall warns against founders who compress past legibility and org charts that round toward the mean. She describes source distortion—deleted customer pain—and organization distortion as an investment problem. A narrow eval can become a promise, so you must weld the limit to the claim. Trust is the thing with no grader, and it is ultimately what makes signal durable (12:38–18:04).

- Because everyone has the same AI leverage, average work is worthless; asking a model what to build gives the same answer as competitors.
- Taste is trainable (preference under feedback), so it is not a durable moat; judgment about unprecedented or unobservable situations is scarce.
- Hamming's criterion flips: agents give you an attack on everything, so choosing which problem deserves an attack is the critical skill.
- Signal is lost through source distortion, org-chart averaging, and converting narrow evals into overpromises; limits must be welded to claims.
- Trust has no grader, making it the highest-value thing to build in an age of machine-generated answers.
