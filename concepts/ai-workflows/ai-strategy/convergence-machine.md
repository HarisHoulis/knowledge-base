# The Signal Layer: What to Build When Anything Can Be Built

In this talk, Lena Hall argues that the abundance of AI agents has collapsed the cost of average work to zero, because the same leverage reached competitors on the same morning. She describes AI models as 'convergence machines' that produce competent, confident, and identical answers based on common knowledge, making it impossible for them to decide what to build next or what users truly want. The single decision they cannot make is where to point them (Hall, 2026, 1:37-5:47).

Hall rejects the consolation that taste will save us, noting that taste is just preference under feedback, and feedback is exactly what these systems learn. Instead, what survives is judgment: judgment about things that have not happened yet (since no data exists for them) and judgment inside relationships the model cannot observe, because it has read everything written about your customer but never met them (Hall, 2026, 7:13-9:56). She invokes Hamming's idea that a problem is important only when you have an attack on it; agents handed everyone an attack on everything, so the scarce skill became choosing which problem deserves one (Hall, 2026, 8:34-9:55).

The rest of the talk focuses on how signal survives the trip to a customer. Hall describes source distortion—the deleted customer pain in what models are trained on—and organization distortion, where org charts round toward the mean and reduce signal. She also warns that a narrow eval can become a promise if you weld the limit to the claim, and that trust is the thing with no grader (Hall, 2026, 12:38-18:04).

- AI abundance makes average work valueless; differentiating requires unique judgment, not taste.
- Models are convergence machines producing common knowledge; they cannot choose where to point.
- Judgment is scarce because it deals with unprecedented events and unobservable relationships.
- Signal degrades through source distortion and organizational rounding, so preserving it is critical.
- Trust has no benchmark or grader, making it a uniquely human and valuable asset.
