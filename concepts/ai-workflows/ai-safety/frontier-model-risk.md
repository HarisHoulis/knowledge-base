# Fragments: August 18

Martin Fowler shares his takes on recent AI developments, including Noah Smith's argument that intelligence is not an unbounded scalar but a conversion ratio with an optimality bound. Smith suggests future AI may not be vastly 'smarter' in the human sense, but can become more replicable, responsive, and capable of understanding 'cloud laws'—causal regularities too diffuse and complex for an individual human to intuit or communicate. Fowler adds that human value will come from artfully combining our human nature with these new AI capabilities.

- AI intelligence has an optimality bound; gains come from replication, speed, and understanding new types of regularities rather than unbounded IQ.
- AI may grasp 'cloud laws' and tacit distributed human knowledge, opening new directions without necessarily outsmarting humans in traditional terms.
- Alex Stamos warns that government takedowns of frontier models inject political risk and push users toward open-weight alternatives like GLM 5.2.
- Security efforts should shift from merely finding AI bugs to fixing them and integrating analysis into continuous delivery pipelines.
- Effective visualization, such as dot-density maps instead of choropleths, better communicates election forecasts and population density.
