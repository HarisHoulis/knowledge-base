# Tokens Are the Cheap Part: AI Cost vs Developer Salary in Engineering Economics

Steve Jamison, Head of AI Research and Development at Jellyfish, argues that token costs are the smallest part of AI's true cost, based on data from the Jellyfish platform — described as the industry's largest engineering workflow data set combined with AI telemetry. AI spending has increased 60 times year-over-year, while consolidated PRs per engineer rose 43% over the same annual period, a productivity gain Jamison calls phenomenal and unprecedented in such a short time frame. AI spending increased in early 2026, when productivity gains began to increase steadily.

Jamison frames the tension between these numbers: costs up 60x versus productivity up 43%. Comparing raw spend across companies is insufficient — some report spending $100,000, $500,000, or millions on AI, and budgets are repeatedly revised as reality sets in. Crucially, neither figure accounts for the largest expense item: developer salaries. Because AI makes developers dramatically more productive, its value should be assessed not as overhead but as part of the cost of creating each PR — the "intelligence cost per PR," combining developer salaries plus AI costs into one expense category.

Under this framing, AI accounts for about 2.5% of the cost of creating a unit of merged code (as of August 2026), with salary making up the remaining 97.5%. The dollar value of merged PRs declined 26% over the past year, even including verification time and maintained quality, and despite the 60-fold increase in AI spending; 83% of studied companies saw a decrease in PR costs. Increasing bandwidth lets salaries be distributed across more PRs, and this happens faster than AI costs increase.

Token cost ranges widely by quintile: the lowest-spending group spends about 0.3% of PR cost (~60 million tokens per developer per month), while the top 20% spend 8.5% of PR costs (~2.5 billion tokens per developer per month) — roughly two orders of magnitude more. On throughput, the bottom four-fifths by cost saw about a 40% increase in merged PRs per developer, while the high-spending group saw a 67% increase, indicating higher AI spending led to greater throughput growth.

- AI spending rose 60x year-over-year while consolidated PRs per engineer rose 43%, creating tension between cost and productivity metrics.
- Developer salaries dominate the true cost: AI is only ~2.5% of the cost per merged PR, with salary at 97.5% (as of August 2026).
- The dollar value of merged PRs fell 26% over the past year despite the 60x AI spend increase, with 83% of studied companies seeing lower PR costs.
- Token spend varies by roughly two orders of magnitude across quintiles: ~0.3% of PR cost (~60M tokens/dev/month) at the low end versus 8.5% (~2.5B tokens/dev/month) at the top 20%.
- Higher AI spending correlated with greater throughput growth: ~40% for the bottom four-fifths versus 67% for the highest-spending group.
