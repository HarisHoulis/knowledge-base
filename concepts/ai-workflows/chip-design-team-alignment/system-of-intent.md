# What If Your Chip Design Team Moved Like a Single Body?

The talk uses a soccer analogy to argue that alignment beats individual skills. A single player combines intent and knowledge to self-align, but a team of 11 players wins only when the whole team moves in a coordinated way. For chip design organizations with over 50 engineers, communication overhead grows quadratically, causing throughput to decline despite adding more AI tools; these tools address the linear productivity problem but not the quadratic alignment problem.

- Alignment beats individual skills: successful chip organizations are the most aligned, not the ones with the best engineers.
- Chip design is high-stakes: hardware bugs cost ~$50M on average, and being late to market can be make-or-break.
- Practitioners report spending ~70% of their time on alignment, not on the core design work.
- Current workflows are fragmented: intent lives in meetings/Slack/email, knowledge in stale wikis, and tool inputs/outputs are often lost.
- The proposed solution is a multi-layer AI system with a shared nervous system—a living graph called the 'system of intent' that captures constraints and decisions.
