# Which AI startups actually land enterprise contracts? — Brian Lewis, Millennium

Brian Lewis, a buyer at Millennium, shares his process for evaluating AI startups: for a given pain point he finds 10-15 startups, books 2-3 demos, runs 0-1 pilot, and signs roughly one contract per four pilots—about 5% of demos turn into deals, matching published benchmarks. He emphasizes that the other 95% fail due to concrete operational and trust issues, not model quality. Examples include a vendor asking customers to self-report gateway telemetry for traffic that never touched their infrastructure, integrations requiring read/write access to everything, features permanently in beta to exploit data-retention terms, a team falsely claiming zero data retention, and a breach-response answer of "we haven't had one yet." Lewis argues these are not AI-specific problems: new frontier models land every 11 days while the underlying architecture is a decade old. He attributes roughly 60% of becoming "AI native" to entitlements, integration, and change management, noting that agents simply inherit whatever foundation already exists.

- The buyer funnel: 10-15 startups -> 2-3 demos -> 0-1 pilot -> ~1 contract per 4 pilots (5% demo-to-deal rate).
- Common deal-killers: overreaching integrations, beta-phase data retention, misleading security/retention claims, and undefined breach responses.
- Reliability and auditability are non-negotiable: control planes, audit logs, and no outages during trading hours.
- AI novelty is not the bottleneck; model turnover every 11 days contrasts with a decade-old architecture. 60% of AI-native success depends on entitlements, integration, and change management.
- Agents amplify existing foundation problems, so enterprises must fix basics before adding AI.
