# Which AI startups actually land enterprise contracts? — Brian Lewis, Millennium

Brian Lewis, speaking from the buying side at a hedge fund, describes how AI startup deals actually get done in an enterprise context. For a given internal pain point, he may find 10–15 startups, book two or three demos, run zero or one pilot, and sign about one contract for every four pilots — roughly a 5% demo-to-signature rate, matching published benchmarks (Brian Lewis, Millennium). The rest of the talk is a catalog of where that other 95% fails.

- Roughly 5% of demo calls end in a signed contract, with one contract for every four pilots.
- Common vendor failures include requiring broad read/write access, permanently beta features with data-retention terms, and false no-retention claims.
- Lewis argues this is not really an AI problem: a new frontier model arrives every 11 days, but the underlying architecture is a decade old.
- About 60% of becoming 'AI native' is attributable to entitlements, integration, and change management; agents inherit existing foundational weaknesses.
