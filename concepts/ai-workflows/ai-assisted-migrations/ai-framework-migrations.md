# The Pulse: We need to talk about migrations with AI

The article highlights how AI is enabling engineering teams to tackle long-postponed framework migrations. Asana needed to migrate off the testing framework Enzyme, which required rewriting a massive number of test cases. With AI assistance, the project was completed in just two weeks—work that would otherwise have been deferred indefinitely. Airbnb and Uber report similar success stories, suggesting AI is an excellent fit for large-scale, mechanical rewrite tasks that are typically low priority but high toil. The author argues that these migrations are a natural use case for AI because they involve repetitive patterns and well-defined transformations (Orosz, 2026).

Beyond migrations, the article briefly covers shifts in engineering leadership roles and questions the relevance of Gartner's Magic Quadrant, noting that major AI vendors refuse to pay the 'Gartner tax.' However, the central takeaway is that AI is already delivering concrete value in pragmatic engineering work, particularly for migrations that have been kicked down the road for years (Orosz, 2026).

- AI enabled Asana to complete a massive Enzyme migration in two weeks, instead of postponing it indefinitely.
- Airbnb and Uber have similar stories of using AI for framework migrations, indicating a broader trend.
- Framework migrations are a strong fit for AI due to their repetitive, large-scale, and rule-based nature.
- The article also notes that AI vendors are challenging traditional analyst rankings like Gartner's Magic Quadrant.
- Engineering leadership roles are evolving, but the migration win is the most concrete example of AI's practical impact.
