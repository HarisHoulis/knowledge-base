# Everyone Gets A Software Company — Benjamin Guo, Zo Computer

In his talk, Benjamin Guo reflects on the early internet and argues that modern computing has become a form of "technofeudalism," where users are peasants scattered across SaaS tools that monetize their attention and lock up their data. He contrasts this with the original Finder icon's depiction of human-machine harmony, suggesting AI tools should empower rather than exploit people (AI Engineer, 2026).

- Critiques modern SaaS as technofeudalism where users pay rent via data, attention, and subscription fees.
- Introduces Zo as a personal cloud where users own their data, AI, and hosted services.
- Highlights a real user, Charlotte, who uses Zo to run her business and personal life from one place.
- Envisions a future where everyone has their own software company and home on the internet.
