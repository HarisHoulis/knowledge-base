# Scrimshaw Jukebox

Simon Willison tested whether Claude Opus 5.5 could compose music by prompting it to design a simple text-based music format, build an artifact that could play it out loud, and include example tracks, with the goal of music in the style of the original Secret of Monkey Island ([Scrimshaw Jukebox](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/6/scrimshaw-jukebox/)).

According to Willison, the model leaned much harder into the Monkey Island theme than he intended, but the results were surprisingly good ([Scrimshaw Jukebox](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/6/scrimshaw-jukebox/)).

He wonders whether competent music composition is similar to the recent 3D graphics capability that emerged for text models in past months, and notes that careful experiments with other recent and older models would be needed to confirm whether this ability is new or longstanding ([Scrimshaw Jukebox](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/6/scrimshaw-jukebox/)).

- Claude Opus 5.5 was prompted to design a simple text-based music format, build an artifact that plays it aloud, and include example tracks.
- The prompt requested music of the quality of the original Secret of Monkey Island; the model leaned harder into that theme than intended, but the output was surprisingly good.
- Willison speculates that competent music composition may be a newly emerged capability for text models, similar to recent 3D graphics advances, but says careful experiments are needed to confirm.
