# From Chrome DevTools to AI Engineering, with Addy Osmani

In this podcast episode, Gergely Orosz interviews Addy Osmani, who spent over 14 years at Google working on Chrome, DevTools, Core Web Vitals, and AI developer experience. Osmani shares his path from building a web browser at age 16 to becoming a Director of Engineering, and reflects on what he learned from building tools for millions of developers. He discusses Google's engineering culture, the shift of VPs coding on weekends thanks to AI tools, and the importance of staying hands-on as a manager (Pragmatic Engineer, 2026).

- Addy Osmani built a web browser from scratch at age 16 to speed up browsing by opening multiple connections when fetching webpages.
- Publishing free educational materials about frontend and JavaScript development helped Addy land a job at Google on the Chrome team.
- Chrome DevTools started as a way to help web developers debug applications and evolved to include source-map-aware debugging, service worker tooling, and mobile device emulation.
- A major risk of AI-assisted development is 'cognitive surrender'—the erosion of comprehension and memory; engineers should understand every major decision an LLM makes.
- Osmani advises engineers to develop non-engineering skills like product sense and go-to-market awareness: 'Don't be just an engineer.'
