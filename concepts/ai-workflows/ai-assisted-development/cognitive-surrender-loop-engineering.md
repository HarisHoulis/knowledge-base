# From Chrome DevTools to AI Engineering, with Addy Osmani

In this Pragmatic Engineer interview, Addy Osmani shares lessons from 14 years at Google building Chrome DevTools, Core Web Vitals, and most recently AI developer experience. He recounts his early start building a web browser at 16 and how publishing free educational materials led to a DevRel-and-builder role on the Chrome team. The conversation covers how DevTools evolved to meet web developers' needs, and why memory debugging remains a hard, under-tooled problem despite advances like flame graphs and deep tracing ([source](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/from-chrome-devtools-to-ai-engineering)).

Osmani warns about cognitive surrender—the erosion of comprehension and memory that can come from over-relying on AI agents. He recommends understanding every major decision an LLM makes, even though reading its entire reasoning is no longer practical. He advocates mutual amplification: agents should log their decisions and key learnings so humans can review, internalize, and improve—while also guiding the agent to get better at the task ([source](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/from-chrome-devtools-to-ai-engineering)).

He also discusses Google's culture shift, where VPs and SVPs now code on weekends with AI tools, and argues that human accountability remains essential—designated engineers own parts of codebases and approve contributions even when they didn't write the code. Osmani is bullish on software engineering's future, predicting the total addressable market for builders will expand dramatically. His career advice: engineering roles will unbundle, so engineers should develop product sense, go-to-market awareness, and technical evangelism—"Don't be just an engineer" ([source](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/from-chrome-devtools-to-ai-engineering)).

- Cognitive surrender—losing comprehension and memory when relying on AI—is a key risk; mitigate it by understanding every major LLM decision.
- Aim for mutual amplification: agents log decisions and learnings, while humans review and internalize those outputs.
- Humans remain accountable for AI-written code, similar to designated owners in open-source projects like Chromium.
- Engineering roles will unbundle; adding product, go-to-market, and evangelism skills is crucial for long-term success.
- Google leadership culture now embraces weekend coding with AI, reflecting how AI tools lower barriers to hands-on engineering.
