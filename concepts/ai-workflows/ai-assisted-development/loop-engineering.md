# From Chrome DevTools to AI Engineering, with Addy Osmani

In this episode summary, Gergely Orosz interviews Addy Osmani, a former 14-year Google engineering director known for Chrome DevTools and Core Web Vitals. Osmani recounts his path from building a web browser at age 16 to joining Google after publishing free educational resources. The article also highlights Google's engineering culture, including a recent trend of VPs and SVPs coding on weekends using AI tools, and Osmani's view that moving into a director role makes you accountable on a weekly basis for a top company goal (Orosz, 2026). A central theme is the risk of cognitive surrender, where relying heavily on AI erodes your comprehension of the code. Osmani recommends reviewing the most important decisions made by LLMs rather than trying to read entire reasoning traces, and practicing loop engineering to achieve mutual amplification: the agent logs its decisions and learnings while you internalize what it does (Orosz, 2026). He is bullish on software engineering, predicting AI will expand the number of people building software, but advises engineers to develop product sense, go-to-market awareness, and other non-engineering skills, saying 'Don't be just an engineer' (Orosz, 2026).

- Cognitive surrender is a key risk of AI-assisted development; understand every major decision an LLM makes.
- Loop engineering aims for mutual amplification: the agent improves by logging decisions, and the human improves by reviewing and internalizing them.
- AI tools made coding easier, leading to Google VPs and SVPs coding on weekends and a cultural shift.
- Accountability remains human: designated engineers own and shape parts of codebases even if they didn't write all the code.
- Engineers should broaden beyond pure building into product, technical evangelism, and go-to-market skills.
