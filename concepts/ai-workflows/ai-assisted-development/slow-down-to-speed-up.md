# Slow down to speed up: so much has changed in 6 months’ time

The article highlights a tipping point in software engineering triggered by more capable AI agents released around November 2025, and uses Meta’s recent “most embarrassing outage” as a cautionary tale. Meta’s account-takeover bug is attributed to AI-generated, AI-reviewed code combined with layoffs and reassignments of security/integrity teams to AI labeling, illustrating risks of prioritizing AI over core product health (Orosz, 2026).

Across the industry, data shows dramatic acceleration: teams using agents ship 5x more PRs than two years ago, Cursor users produce 2.5x more code, PR sizes are up 3x, and more changes are accepted without human review. Anthropic and OpenAI are fully embracing AI agents, reporting 70-90% AI-generated code at Anthropic and a “fix this” button at OpenAI; Google uses Gemini widely but with acknowledged capability gaps; Uber built custom AI infrastructure; startups and traditional companies like Cisco and JP Morgan are also adopting AI tools (Orosz, 2026).

These shifts bring trends and warnings: individual productivity is up but team productivity may be flat, code quality is falling, and review overload leads to less stringent oversight. Orosz offers advice for engineers and leaders to future-proof careers, and notes many attendees confirmed “it’s happening here too” (Orosz, 2026).

- Meta’s embarrassing outage was likely caused by AI-generated and AI-reviewed code, compounded by layoffs and gutted security/integrity teams.
- Since November 2025, AI agents like Opus 4.5 and GPT-5.4 became genuinely useful, marking a major shift in software engineering.
- Data shows 5x more PRs, 2.5x more code per developer, 3x larger PRs, and more changes merged without human review.
- AI adoption spans startups and large companies, with Anthropic and OpenAI going all-in, Uber building custom infra, and traditional firms like Cisco and JP Morgan investing heavily.
- Trends include rising individual productivity but flat team productivity, falling software quality, and vanishing middle management, prompting advice to future-proof careers.
