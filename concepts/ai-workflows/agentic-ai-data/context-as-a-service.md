# The Rise of CaaS: Context-as-a-Service for Agentic AI

In this talk, Omer Primor of Bright Data argues that the web is evolving from a mere source of data into a source of context for AI agents. Unlike traditional data extraction, agentic AI requires continuous, fresh context to perform knowledge work, and the web is messy, unstructured, and constantly changing. Primor highlights data decay, noting that social media relevance lasts under a day, and for news, finance, and retail, most data becomes irrelevant within 30 days, making context extraction an ongoing process rather than a one-time snapshot (Omer Primor, AI Engineer, 2026).

Primor traces the shift in search behavior from Google's complete dominance three years ago to the integration of search into LLMs and chatbots, and finally to the emergence of AI-native search companies like Exa, Parallel, you.com, and Tavily that index the web specifically for agents. This convergence has weakened Google's grip on search and opened the door for a new breed of companies offering Context-as-a-Service (CaaS), which supply the real-time, structured context that autonomous agents need to act, draw conclusions, and power downstream applications (Omer Primor, AI Engineer, 2026).

- Web data is now context for AI agents, not just raw data, requiring continuous extraction due to rapid data decay.
- Data decay varies by vertical: social media in under a day, while news/finance/retail mostly lose relevance within 30 days.
- Search has shifted from human-only Google use to LLM-embedded search and agent-first search engines.
- A new category, Context-as-a-Service (CaaS), is emerging to supply fresh, structured web context to agentic AI systems.
