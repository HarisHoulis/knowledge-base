# AI Engineer NYC: Chasing Capabilities While Waiting for the Frontier

This is the opening of the third and final day of the AI Engineer New York conference, MC'd by Kenya Se of Touring Post. The day's program spans finance, compute and infrastructure, evaluations and benchmarking, developer tools, and model costs, with sessions on agent management, self-improving agents, agent permissions, and token economics [source: conference intro]. The MC frames the event around the gap between developing AI technology and understanding the work it needs to do, noting that while superintelligence feels powerful, the ROI is still missing [source: conference intro].

In the first talk, Alexander Shiftchenko of Ramp presents "Chasing Capabilities While Waiting for the Frontier." He describes Ramp as an "extremely bitter lesson-pilled" company: over the last year the number of LLM use cases increased 123%, and while token spend on top features grew, unit economics actually improved because newly released frontier models are cheaper, faster, and better, allowing the same accuracy at lower cost [source: Alexander Shiftchenko, Ramp].

Given this pattern, he raises the question of why a company wouldn't simply wait for the frontier to advance rather than build an applied research function internally [source: Alexander Shiftchenko, Ramp]. The transcript cuts off before he answers.
