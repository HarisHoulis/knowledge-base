# The Reinvention of the Dev Team

Hannah Foxwell argues that the surge of agentic coding is forcing engineering leaders to rethink team dynamics. Rather than treating AI-driven velocity as a pure productivity win, she frames the shift as a leadership challenge that requires new anchors for how teams are structured and what they optimize for.

Foxwell offers three key anchors for navigating AI-driven velocity. The first is building software worth building, a reminder that faster output is only valuable when it targets meaningful problems. The second is prioritizing automated safety over speed, positioning safety mechanisms as the enabler of sustainable velocity rather than a brake on it.

The third anchor is preserving the human element. Foxwell points to generalist "broken comb" skillsets and sustainable on-call practices as ways to keep teams resilient and humane as agents take on more execution work. Together, these anchors suggest that the reinvention of the dev team is less about tooling and more about values, safety, and the shape of human roles.

- Agentic coding forces engineering leaders to rethink team dynamics, not just tooling.
- Three anchors: build software worth building, prioritize automated safety over speed, and preserve the human element.
- Generalist "broken comb" skillsets help teams adapt as agents take on more work.
- Sustainable on-call practices are part of keeping the human element intact.
