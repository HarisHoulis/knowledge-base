# How to increase your leverage with agents

Kent C. Dodds argues that AI agents are not replacing software engineers but instead shifting them to higher levels of abstraction. He notes that tasks agents handle today were done manually by him months ago, and he expects this trend to continue, freeing him to focus on even more impactful work. This pattern mirrors the historical evolution of programming languages, from assembly to Fortran/COBOL to modern languages like JavaScript and Python, with natural language as the next frontier (Dodds, 2026).

Dodds encourages developers to identify their current 'busy work'—the repetitive or time-consuming tasks they are doing now—and delegate those to agents within the next few weeks or months. While some responsibilities, such as taste, judgment, and system design review, remain difficult to automate, agents can handle many high-leverage supporting tasks. He gives an example of using an agent to perform a health assessment on an architecture, where the agent orchestrates sub-agents, each working on a specific idea in its own environment, all initiated from a quick voice command at a theme park (Dodds, 2026).

The key is to continuously project what you are doing now and ask how an agent can do it for you, thereby moving up the abstraction ladder and increasing your overall leverage (Dodds, 2026).

- Agents take over current tasks, pushing engineers up the abstraction ladder rather than eliminating their roles.
- Historical trend from assembly to high-level languages to natural language shows a consistent pattern of increasing abstraction.
- Identify current busy work that can be automated in the near term to focus on higher-leverage activities.
- Tasks involving taste, judgment, and architecture review remain human-centric, but agents can assist with surrounding high-leverage work.
- Example: orchestrating sub-agents for architecture health assessments from a mobile device.
