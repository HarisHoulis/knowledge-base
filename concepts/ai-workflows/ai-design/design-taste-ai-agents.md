# The Missing Layer: Design Taste in AI Agents

Hassan El Mghari, who leads developer experience at Together AI, argues that design and UX are the primary reasons AI applications gain traction. He shares his experience building simple one-page AI apps that reached millions of users, emphasizing that a small extra effort on UI yields a significant competitive advantage. He identifies common 'AI slop' patterns such as purple gradient backgrounds, italic headers, 'scroll to explore' sections, all-caps pills, gradient logos, and excessive emoji usage. To counter this, he built a design skill called Hallmark that codifies anti-slop rules and provides AI models with a variety of themes, helping generate more distinctive and polished UIs. The talk highlights the importance of moving beyond generic AI-generated aesthetics to create products that stand out.

- Design and UX are the key factors driving adoption of AI apps, not just the underlying model.
- AI-generated apps often share recognizable 'slop' patterns (purple gradients, italics, pills, emojis, etc.).
- Hallmark is a design skill that encodes anti-slop patterns and offers diverse themes to guide AI output.
- Investing 10-20% extra effort on UI yields a major competitive advantage for AI products.
