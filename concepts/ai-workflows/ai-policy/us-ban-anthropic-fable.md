# The Pulse: Big implications of US banning Anthropic’s new model, Fable

This article covers several major tech industry events, with a central focus on the US government's decision to restrict Anthropic's new model, Fable, to US citizens only. The author suggests this could boost China's influence in AI, as other countries and non-US companies may turn to capable open models, most of which originate from China. This policy could reshape the global AI landscape by driving non-US entities away from American AI offerings (Orosz, 2026).

The article also provides a follow-up on Meta's engineering culture crisis, noting that non-engineering teams lost more than 10% of their staff, while Integrity teams were already stretched thin before cuts. Additionally, it reports on SpaceX becoming a trillionaire company after its IPO, acquiring Cursor, which in turn acquired Continue, signaling SpaceX's intent to compete with AI labs like Anthropic and OpenAI. Other industry updates include Gemini lead quitting for OpenAI, Epic Games' new open-source version control system, and Microsoft considering dropping OpenAI for DeepSeek, alongside research showing LLMs amplify existing expertise (Orosz, 2026).

- US ban on Fable may push non-US users and companies toward Chinese open-source models, increasing China's AI influence.
- Meta's engineering org continues to suffer, with over 10% staff cuts in non-engineering teams and stressed Integrity teams.
- SpaceX acquired Cursor, and Cursor acquired Continue, positioning SpaceX to directly compete with Anthropic and OpenAI.
- Industry moves include a Gemini lead leaving for OpenAI, Epic's new open-source VCS, and Microsoft reportedly considering DeepSeek over OpenAI.
