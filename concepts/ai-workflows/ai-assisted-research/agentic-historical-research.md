# GPT 6.2 Astra Solves a Historical Mystery

Simon Willison highlights a report from the Generative History Substack in which GPT-6.2 Astra, given only minimal prompting, autonomously identified the creator of an anonymous 1808 sketchbook held at Yale's Beinecke Library. The model completed the task in 22 minutes using publicly available primary and secondary sources, and the digitized sketchbook is linked for reference.

The key move was a cross-source inference: Astra found a diary reference to a visit by the vicomte de Chabot dated five days before a date mentioned in the sketchbook at a nearby river, then hypothesized that Chabot's travel journal—obtainable only by order from Cleveland State University—would confirm the sketchbook as his work. The quoted author notes that this newly surfaced source is itself a significant find for Canadian and fur trade historians.

Willison frames the result against the Deep Blue precedent and quotes Mark's prediction of a mild crisis of confidence for historians by the following spring or summer, as they confront agentic systems capable of feats human historians cannot match. Mark argues the disruption will be less profound than in mathematics, since history involves continual reinterpretation rather than a single universal truth, and historical writing is closer to philosophy and literature—making human authorship something of a requirement.

- GPT-6.2 Astra autonomously attributed an anonymous 1808 Yale sketchbook in 22 minutes using public sources with minimal prompting.
- The model formed a testable hypothesis—that the vicomte de Chabot's travel journal would confirm authorship—and the prediction held.
- The newly discovered source is itself a major find for Canadian and fur trade historians.
- Commentators expect a milder 'Deep Blue moment' for history than for math, since history prizes reinterpretation and literary authorship over a single universal truth.
