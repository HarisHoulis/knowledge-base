# Fragments: June 2

Martin Fowler's June 2 fragments survey recent AI-related essays, highlighting how hard it is to measure developer productivity and forecast AI's impact. Greg Wilson's critique of common metrics is noted: lines of code, tickets closed, and self-reported productivity are all flawed. Fowler defends self-reports as a dim but useful qualitative signal when better measures are unavailable, linking to his own work on measuring developer productivity (Fowler, 2026).

- Measuring AI productivity remains unreliable; qualitative self-reports may be the best available signal.
- Historical automation didn't eliminate professions; AI's job impact is hard to forecast due to Jevons paradox and changing job nature.
- Open-source models are closing capability gaps faster, but closed models still set the frontier.
- Using LLMs in security can find far more bugs (Mozilla: 423 in April 2026), but hallucinated citations in reports poison online knowledge.
- Technical debt compounds with LLMs as models imitate existing code; human attention is the bottleneck orchestrating agents.
