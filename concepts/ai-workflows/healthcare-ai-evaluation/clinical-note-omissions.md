# Inside 847 Production Clinical AI Notes: The Danger of Quiet Errors

AI-generated clinical notes can look flawless while missing critical details. For example, a patient's jaw pain on chewing—a red flag for giant cell arteritis—was omitted from a note, turning an emergency into a routine headache. Conversely, a young man with a sore throat received a fabricated note with chest pain, diabetes medications, and a nonexistent hospital. These are not rare anomalies: the largest real-world study found about 1 in 20 notes contain an error serious enough to cause significant harm, 1 in 5 have an important omission, and over 1 in 10 contain a hallucination (Fox, 2026).

- Most dangerous AI note errors are 'quiet': plausible omissions or inferences that look completely normal to a reader.
- Real-world production data shows ~5% of notes have serious harm-level errors; omission and hallucination rates are even higher.
- AI scribes are already in about a third of US practices, with physician AI use doubling last year, yet there is no adverse event reporting.
- Current automated checks catch only a small fraction of failures; high-stakes errors often sit below the detection line.
