# Reliable, Smart Workflows for Finance, Wealth and Insurance

Karthic Rao, CTO at Perpetual, describes how his asset-management company applies AI to acquired traditional firms under heavy regulatory constraints. He argues that in regulated industries reliability and intelligence are not opposites, and that the architect's job is to decide where to draw the line between them. Perpetual's workflows have four characteristics that each push the architecture in a direction: they are complex and require real-time execution strategies rather than fully pre-scripted steps; the data is confidential, requiring privately hosted models; policy violations carry serious consequences rather than being mere bug reports; and execution must therefore be extremely reliable and deterministic.

- In regulated finance, reliability and intelligence are not opposites—the key decision is where to draw the line between them.
- Confidential data pushes workflows toward privately hosted models, and policy violations carry serious consequences, so execution must be deterministic.
- Token maximization is the wrong goal for regulated industries; intelligence should be used minimally and purposefully.
- Most workflow steps simply follow the next node, with the model choosing a course only at a few decision points.
- A simple instruction like 'Fill out IRS Form W-9 for Mr. Harper' hides search, ambiguity resolution, human input, and external dependencies.
