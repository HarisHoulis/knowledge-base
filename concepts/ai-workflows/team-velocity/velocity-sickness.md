# Velocity Sickness: What Happens When Your Whole Team Gets 10x Faster — Matt Dailey, Ref.

In this talk, Matt Dailey, CEO of Ref, defines "velocity sickness" as the stress caused by sudden output increases thanks to AI, resulting in output without impact. He identifies four key problems that emerge when entire teams speed up: too many PRs to merge, teams moving in many directions at once, "agent bankruptcy" where engineers discard and restart AI agents, and critical decisions being made by agents, which gives agents ownership of the code. These problems are magnified at the organizational level, creating a feeling of high productivity without real progress.

Dailey argues that individual engineers and teams face these issues to different degrees, and the root cause is that AI enables rapid parallel work while surrounding systems—review, coordination, ownership—have not adapted. The solution involves aligning team practices to preserve human ownership and focus, rather than letting agents run in scattered directions. The talk provides a blueprint for getting through these issues and moving faster together as a team.

- Too many PRs to merge overwhelms review processes and breaks merge queues.
- Lack of coordination causes engineers and agents to work at cross-purposes, reducing cohesive progress.
- Agent bankruptcy—killing and restarting agents—wastes time and tokens by redoing the same work.
- When agents make critical decisions, they take ownership of the code, and the team loses ownership of the product.
- Velocity sickness is output without impact: moving fast but not achieving expected outcomes.
