# Adaption Labs: Gradient-Free Continual Learning

In this talk, Sara Hooker explores the historical evolution of scientific discovery, highlighting how the professionalization of science created an "unreasonably narrow path" for AI researchers. She argues that only a select few—those with access to elite PhD programs, industry labs, and substantial compute—have been able to contribute to the frontier of AI, leaving large parts of the world excluded from shaping the technology. Hooker references the "GPU poor versus GPU rich" divide, noting that compute requirements have become a significant barrier to entry, and that this concentration of resources has led to shipping the same model to billions of users rather than tailoring solutions to diverse problems (Hooker, 2026).

Hooker then proposes a revolution in who gets to participate at the frontier of AI, centered on the idea of enabling anyone to build frontier-level intelligence. She specifically discusses gradient-free continual learning as a promising approach to reduce compute dependency and democratize AI development. By moving away from traditional gradient-based methods that require massive computational resources, this technique could allow researchers and practitioners with limited infrastructure to still make meaningful contributions to AI. The talk emphasizes that such methods not only lower barriers but also align with more efficient compute usage, as not all problems require the same level of resource expenditure.

The broader implication is that democratizing AI research and development could diversify the questions being asked and solved, shifting the focus from simply representing the world to answering the questions at the end of that pursuit. Hooker's vision is one where the frontier of AI is shaped by a wider community, breaking the current mold of a few frontier labs controlling the direction of the field.

- The professionalization of science has created a narrow, compute-dependent path for AI researchers, limiting who can contribute to the frontier.
- Compute requirements (the 'GPU poor vs GPU rich' divide) are a major barrier to entry, concentrating breakthroughs in a handful of labs.
- Gradient-free continual learning is presented as a way to lower compute barriers and enable broader participation in AI development.
- Democratizing AI research could lead to more diverse questions and more efficient use of compute resources.
