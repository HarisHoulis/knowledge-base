# Fragments: July 21

Martin Fowler's mid-2026 fragment summarizes the second Future of Software Development Retreat. Thoughtworks' report highlights five headline findings: code generation is no longer the bottleneck—verification is; 'harness engineering' is emerging as a distinct discipline; organizations face an apprenticeship crisis; the executive/engineer expectation gap is a bigger risk than technical limitations; and legacy modernization is the clearest near-term value pool. Fowler recounts an air-filter ML cautionary tale where models trained on desert equipment failed in arctic conditions, causing $100 billion in fire damage from decaying mosquitoes, illustrating the dangers of applying AI in new contexts without proper feedback sensors.

- Code generation is no longer the bottleneck; verification is, and organizations should focus on harness engineering and legacy modernization.
- Applying LLM solutions out of context can cause catastrophic failures; build sensors and rapid feedback loops to mitigate risks.
- Citizen-developer vibe coding creates shadow IT and security risks; companies need guardrails, separate infrastructure, and board-level threat modeling.
- LLMs are valuable for operations and incident analysis, but human oversight remains critical for unpredictable incidents and agent-inserted unrequested features.
- Distinct human writing voice is becoming a competitive advantage; avoid LLM-speak and use techniques like reading aloud to preserve authenticity.
