# The Half Life of Agent Infrastructure — Ben Kus, Box

Ben Kus opens by retracting his previous year's recommendation of a graph-based approach to building agents, noting that better methods have since arrived. He emphasizes that while infrastructure traditionally has a half-life of three to five years, agent infrastructure changes in months, breaking conventional wisdom about picking a stack and going deep. This rapid change primarily hurts people and teams, as engineers are asked to rebuild working systems repeatedly, sometimes the day after shipping.

- Agent infrastructure has a half-life measured in months, unlike traditional infrastructure's 3-5 year cycle.
- Standard advice to pick a stack and switch rarely doesn't apply to agent infrastructure.
- Teams should be told that constant change is normal, not a failure.
- Build abstractions that allow the underlying layer to be swapped out easily.
- Switch technologies based on eval sets, not on trends or exciting papers.
