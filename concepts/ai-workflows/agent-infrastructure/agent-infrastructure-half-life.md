# The Half Life of Agent Infrastructure — Ben Kus, Box

Ben Kus retracts the graph-based agent recommendation he made on the same stage a year earlier, noting that a better approach has since arrived—just as it has for models, agent design, and retrieval (04:48). He contrasts traditional infrastructure's three-to-five-year half life with agent infrastructure's half life of months, which breaks the usual 'pick a stack and go deep' advice (10:31). Kus illustrates the human cost by describing asking an engineer to rebuild working agentic search in a new style, then proposing another rebuild the day after shipping (13:49). His answer is preparation, not technology: normalize change for teams, build swap-friendly abstractions, and switch only when eval sets show an improvement, not because a paper is exciting (14:57, 16:04). Box now reviews AI technologies on a six-month clock (17:13).

- Agent infrastructure has a half life measured in months, not years, invalidating standard infrastructure advice.
- Kus retracts his prior year's graph-based agent recommendation because a better approach arrived.
- The burden of rapid change falls on engineers, so teams must treat change as normal rather than failure.
- Adopt new technology based on eval results, not trends or hype.
- Box reviews all AI technology on a six-month cycle and builds abstractions to make the layer underneath swappable.
