# AI is mass decompiling games: both awesome and scary

Simon Willison highlights a piece arguing that AI-driven decompilation of games and software has both major upsides and serious downsides. On the negative side, if AI can decompile software, the tech industry faces disruption: anyone can decompile others' software and use it however they like, companies can steal from competitors, and paid games or apps can be open-sourced against the creator's will. This applies to any software, not just old video games.

On the positive side, the same capability is a big win for preservation. Old games can be ported, modified, enhanced, and made more accessible than before. With console makers going all-in on digital and games becoming unplayable when servers shut down, this is described as fantastic news, letting people own software they bought regardless of what the company thinks.

- AI decompilation lets anyone decompile software and use it freely, enabling theft and unwanted open-sourcing of paid apps.
- The risk applies to all software, not just retro games.
- AI decompilation is a major win for game preservation, enabling ports, mods, and accessibility.
- It counters digital-only distribution and server shutdowns by letting buyers own software regardless of the company.
