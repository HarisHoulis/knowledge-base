# AI Surprises Outpace Cryptographic Standards Recovery

Matthew Green raises worst-case possibilities about cryptography in an era of rapid AI progress, noting he assigns a 1% chance that we live in Minicrypt—Russell Impagliazzo's hypothetical world in which public-key encryption is impossible—and a 15% chance that we functionally lose confidence in existing public-key encryption algorithms.

Green's core concern is a mismatch of timescales: the speed at which AI produces surprises is orders of magnitude faster than the speed at which humans replace standards, even with the best AI assistance. Because of this gap, recovery from such a surprise is only possible if preparation is done in advance.

- Green estimates a 1% chance we live in Minicrypt, where public-key encryption is impossible
- He estimates a 15% chance we functionally lose confidence in existing public-key encryption algorithms
- AI produces surprises orders of magnitude faster than humans can replace standards, even with strong AI assistance
- Recovering from such a surprise requires preparation done in advance
