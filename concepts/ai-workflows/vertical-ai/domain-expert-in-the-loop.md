# Trading Desks to Clinical Trials: Parallels in Applied Vertical AI

Ayush Bhardwaj describes how applied vertical AI across hedge funds and pharma faces the same core problem: teams can build agents but cannot judge whether their outputs are good. He notes that engineers have trained instincts for code quality, but no equivalent instinct for trade theses or drug candidates. Without that judgment, vertical AI projects quietly die because they look finished but are not adopted (Bhardwaj, 2026).

Bhardwaj explains that using a model as the judge fails because it jargons without understanding domain meaning like 'alpha'. Verifiable rewards work for math and code because answer keys exist, but in fields like finance and clinical trials the necessary data is deliberately withheld: funds file quarterly and suffer from reverse engineering, and roughly 30% of clinical trial sponsors never disclose results, with 2,000+ reminded by the FDA in 2026 (Bhardwaj, 2026).

The solution is to 'hire the user' — bring a senior domain expert into the loop. This expert curates sources, sharpens prompts, and judges outputs starting from error analysis. The moat is never the model or infrastructure; both are commodities. The differentiator is proprietary data and domain expertise, and the effective loop is AI in the loop with the expert rather than human-in-the-loop generic workflows (Bhardwaj, 2026).

- Vertical AI fails when teams cannot judge the quality of outputs; engineering instinct does not transfer to domain-specific fields.
- Verifiable rewards only work where answer keys exist, such as math and code; finance and clinical data are intentionally withheld or incomplete.
- Hiring a senior domain expert is the cheapest way to build judgment and enables error analysis, prompt sharpening, and preference training.
- The moat in vertical AI is proprietary data and domain expertise, not the model or infrastructure.
- The winning pattern is AI in the loop with a domain expert, not generic human-in-the-loop processes.
