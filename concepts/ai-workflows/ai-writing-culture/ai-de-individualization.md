# AI and the De-individualization of Writing

Jack Despain Zhou observes that a major existing effect of AI has been a "great de-individualization" of content people encounter. He describes hearing Claude's voice in YouTube videos, conference presentations, and email pitches alike, noting that even ostensibly serious people with serious credentials and institutions behind them send writing that reads as AI-generated (Zhou, quoted by Simon Willison).

The concern is not just detection but reputation: Zhou frames these pitches as material that would be attached to both the sender's and the recipient's reputations, yet the text itself is recognizably Claude's. The quoted passage captures a broader cultural shift where AI-generated prose becomes the default texture of professional communication.

- AI has produced a noticeable de-individualization of content across media (videos, talks, email)
- Claude's writing style is recognizable across many ostensibly professional contexts
- Even credentialed senders forward AI-generated pitches that carry reputational stakes
- The observation is framed as an already-existing effect of AI, not a future risk
