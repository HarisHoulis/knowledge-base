# How 21 People Built a $100M+ Agent Company — Quinn Slack, Amp

Quinn Slack recounts the moment in January 2025 when, while still CEO of the 200-person enterprise software company Sourcegraph, he first experimented with an agent and watched it follow commands, inspect its own output, and build its own feedback loops. That experience convinced him and teammate Thorsten Boll that everything—how software is built, how companies are organized, how software is sold and distributed—was about to change, and that the right response was to rush forward rather than wait.

Rather than trying to transform the whole company, Slack disconnected from sales calls and management meetings and started building the agent product in isolation. Sourcegraph eventually laid off 20 people and formed a small internal team, the Amp team, to pursue the idea. He frames this as a lesson for developers who want their companies to be more agent-friendly, while also acknowledging sympathy for CEOs facing difficult transformations.

The talk argues that the traditional enterprise playbook—building a sales team and checking off enterprise requirements to satisfy average corporate buyers—would make a product obsolete within roughly three months in this environment. What mattered instead was making users and their CEOs love the product, since budgets were effectively limitless and all bets were off.

Slack also raises the differentiation problem: if agents can create a product or feature, competitors can replicate any capability Amp proves out, so feature-level advantages like cloud agents (Orbs) or multiplayer are not durable. He questions the value of an entire apparatus of product management, design, and engineering teams disconnected from real customers, and describes rethinking many things under the strange constraints of a small team trying to do things differently.

- First-hand agent experience in January 2025 convinced Slack that software creation, company organization, sales, and distribution would all change.
- Instead of transforming the whole 200-person company, Sourcegraph laid off 20 people and formed a small dedicated Amp team that disconnected from normal processes.
- The traditional enterprise checklist and sales-team playbook was seen as a path to obsolescence within about three months.
- Feature differentiation is fragile because any capability Amp proves can be copied by competitors.
- Small teams trying to do things differently face strange constraints and must rethink many assumptions.
