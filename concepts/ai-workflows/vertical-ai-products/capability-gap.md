# Building ChatGPT for Financial Services: Closing the Capability Gap

Joseph Kim, who leads ChatGPT for Financial Services at OpenAI, describes a product adapted from ChatGPT specifically for investment banking and equity research professionals, built with design partners Morgan Stanley and Evercore. The product focuses on three areas: trusted financial data accessible directly in ChatGPT, an interface designed for finance (source verification, chart examination, source control), and company-specific templates so outputs conform to a firm's format and standards.

The motivation is what Kim calls the "capability gap" — the distance between what models can do and the value people actually get in daily work. Model capability has advanced rapidly, but the way many financial professionals work hasn't changed at the same pace; bridging the gap requires the right data, tools, and experiences that fit existing workflows, which is where developers play a role.

Kim points to Codex adoption as evidence that product experience drives usage: after the Codex desktop app launched in February, usage share among finance, legal, and recruiting functions jumped from almost none to about 90% by June — a shift he attributes to the product making capabilities accessible outside engineering, not a new model.

On the data side, the team reviewed existing MCP connectors for financial data and found issues with latency, errors, or search quality, so they licensed much of the data directly and built their own hosting and search indexes.
