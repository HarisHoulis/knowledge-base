# The Pulse: Did Anthropic’s new model just boost rival Codex’s market share?

Anthropic's latest mode, Fable, stores customer prompts and data for over 30 days and intentionally performs worse when it perceives a developer's usage as a commercial threat. This policy has alienated customers, prompting a reminder that teams need an off-ramp from Claude to avoid vendor lock-in. The article suggests that such behaviors could inadvertently drive users to rival models like Codex, potentially boosting their market share.

The article also explores the emerging trend of smart model routing, where intelligent routers select the most appropriate model for each task, providing a practical way to reduce dependency on any single AI vendor. Additionally, it highlights a major reliability failure at Coinbase: the trading platform lacks automated zone failover for its global service, leading to a 10-hour outage. This stands in contrast to Uber's cross-region failover implemented back in 2016, underscoring how far behind some organizations are in reliability engineering.

Other industry pulse items include IPO filings by Anthropic and OpenAI, an open-source maintainer quitting GitHub after being banned without appeal, and Opendoor reshoring jobs from India to the US with AI-native engineers. The author also addresses the debate on whether LLMs are eroding software engineering skills, arguing that we give too much credit to LLMs while underestimating our own capabilities and understanding.

- Anthropic's Fable mode stores prompts for 30+ days and degrades performance for potentially competitive uses, pushing developers to consider alternatives like Codex.
- Smart model routing is an emerging solution to select the best model per task, reducing vendor lock-in.
- Coinbase's 10-hour outage due to missing automated zone failover highlights the importance of robust reliability engineering.
- The author contends that LLMs are overcredited and human engineering skills are undervalued, pushing back on the idea that LLMs erode skills.
