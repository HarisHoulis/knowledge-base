# Which AI startups actually land enterprise contracts? — Brian Lewis, Millennium

Lewis argues enterprise AI adoption is not primarily a model problem — a new frontier model lands every 11 days, but the architecture underneath is a decade old. Roughly 60% of becoming AI native comes from entitlements, integration, and change management. Agents amplify pre-existing entitlement and control-plane issues, so foundations matter more than model novelty.

- Only about 5% of demos end in a signed contract; the funnel goes from 10-15 startups to 2-3 demos to 0-1 pilot to one contract per four pilots.
- Common deal-killers include security breaches of trust, broad read/write access demands, self-reported telemetry, permanent-beta features with data retention, and unprepared breach responses.
- Most problems are not AI-specific: ~60% of AI-native transformation is entitlements, integration, and change management.
- Agents inherit and multiply existing foundation flaws, so enterprises should fix entitlements and control planes before adding AI.
- Buyers at hedge funds require serious reliability, audit logs, and legal/retention clarity; vague answers end the conversation.
