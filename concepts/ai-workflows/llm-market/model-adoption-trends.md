# Anthropic’s best AI model struggles to attract users as cheaper tools thrive

According to Simon Willison's summary of an FT story, Anthropic's annualized revenue reached $65bn in July 2026, up from $47bn in May, and the company expects Q3 to be profitable. However, its most advanced model, Fable 5, has seen limited adoption: Ramp's AI index shows Fable 5 accounts for only 8.0% of Anthropic model spend, while the older Opus 4.8 leads at 28.0%. This suggests that the newest model's cost has made it less popular than cheaper alternatives (Willison, 2026).

The Ramp AI index, which uses billing data from 70,000 companies, reveals a fragmented adoption landscape. Opus 5, released July 24, captures just 3.5% of spend, while legacy models like Opus 4.6 and Sonnet 4.6 retain significant shares. In contrast, OpenAI's annualized revenue jumped 35% to over $40bn after the launch of GPT-5.6, indicating that pricing and practical value weigh heavily in customer decisions (Willison, 2026, citing FT).

The data highlights a key tension in the AI industry: cutting-edge capability does not automatically translate to market dominance. Enterprises appear to favor cost-efficient, proven models, even as vendors push their flagship offerings. Anthropic's profitability forecast suggests the company is managing this dynamic, but the adoption metrics underscore the competitive pressure from cheaper tools.

- Anthropic's annualized revenue reached $65bn in July 2026, up from $47bn in May.
- Ramp AI index shows Opus 4.8 leads Anthropic spend at 28%, while Opus 5 and Fable 5 trail at 3.5% and 8.0% respectively.
- Fable 5, despite being the newest and best model, has low adoption likely due to cost.
- OpenAI's annualized revenue surpassed $40bn after GPT-5.6 launch, a 35% increase in the quarter to date.
- Ramp AI index uses billing data from 70,000 companies to estimate model adoption.
