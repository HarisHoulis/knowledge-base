# The Building Blocks of GTM Orchestration — Arman Vaziri, Ramp

In this talk, Arman Vaziri of Ramp explains go-to-market (GTM) orchestration as the ability to describe a motion—such as a playbook, experiment, or evergreen campaign—and have it automatically executed across channels like outbound, ads, web, and in-app notifications. He illustrates with a campaign targeting golfers at East Coast construction companies, where one could spin up an audience, assign incentives, and generate copy/creative across channels simply by stating intent. The goal is to reduce coordination costs and enable faster execution of ideas from product, data, engineering, and GTM teams.

Vaziri identifies three fundamental challenges: messy and inconsistent data across systems, reps buried in operational busywork, and expensive coordination/distribution. These obstacles made it difficult to coordinate actions across teams and channels, slowing experimentation and creativity. To address this, Ramp built from the ground up: first fixing data ingestion and consistency, then creating vertical efficiency and growth levers to save time and improve conversion, then extending patterns horizontally across teams with similar workflows, and finally tackling multi-channel execution from a single description of intent. Agents have deepened the ability to push automation as close as possible to operators' points of execution, making the system more responsive and scalable.

- GTM orchestration lets teams describe high-level intents that automatically distribute across multiple channels (outbound, ads, web, in-app).
- Data consistency across systems is a foundational requirement; without it, coordinated multi-channel execution is impossible.
- Reducing operational busywork and coordination costs is key to scaling experimentation and creative GTM strategies.
- Agent-based automation can operate close to execution points, increasing the depth of automation and enabling faster response to intent.
- Building GTM orchestration proceeds in layers: data quality, vertical efficiency levers, horizontal pattern sharing, and finally cross-channel coordination.
