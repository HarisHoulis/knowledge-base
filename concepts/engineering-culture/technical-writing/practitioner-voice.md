# Practitioner Voice: The Writing Category Nobody has Named Yet

The article highlights a distinct writing category called practitioner voice, which Jim Highsmith recognized and named. Martin Fowler advocates this style and notes that his contributors mostly follow it. This voice is described as different from both academic writing and thought-leadership content, offering a unique mode of communication for practitioners.

By identifying this category, the article aims to raise awareness of its distinctive qualities and value. Practitioner voice likely emphasizes practical experience and actionable insights over theoretical rigor or high-level vision. This makes it a critical tool for sharing knowledge within engineering and development communities.

The naming of this category is significant because it gives practitioners a way to recognize and cultivate a style that is often overlooked. Fowler's endorsement suggests it is a standard for effective technical writing.

- Practitioner voice is a distinct writing style separate from academic writing and thought-leadership content.
- Jim Highsmith named and identified this category, highlighting its importance.
- Martin Fowler advocates for this style, and his contributors predominantly use it.
- The category fills a gap in how practitioners communicate their knowledge and experience.
