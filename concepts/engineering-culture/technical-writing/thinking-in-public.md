# Why I'm Writing Rachel's Ramblings

Rachel Laycock, Global CTO at Thoughtworks, explains why she is starting a blog called 'Rachel's Ramblings'. She reflects on a realization at the Future of Software conference that she has her own patterns and hypotheses worth sharing, yet she has not been writing them down. Laycock acknowledges she is busy with a demanding job and two young children, but the real reason is that she tends to keep ideas in her head rather than articulating them ([source](https://martinfowler.com/rachels-ramblings/intro.html)).

The blog is an experiment in 'fast, imperfect, thinking out loud' to name ideas early instead of waiting until they are fully formed. She describes her brain as a knowledge graph, constantly making associations and matching patterns, which is useful in conversations and client work but less useful if never externalized. Martin Fowler encouraged her to write, and she notes she hates decks and loves writing. The blog serves as a forcing function to turn thinking into something that exists outside her head, even if some ideas will be wrong or evolve over time. This introspective piece highlights the importance of knowledge sharing and leadership communication in the tech industry.

- Laycock has observed emerging patterns and hypotheses across clients and teams that are not yet well articulated.
- The blog is an intentional exercise in sharing incomplete, evolving ideas publicly rather than waiting for perfection.
- Writing is framed as a forcing function to externalize thinking, especially for someone whose thinking resembles a knowledge graph.
- The author was encouraged by Martin Fowler and prefers writing over presentation decks.
- The piece underscores the value of articulating patterns to help leaders strategize and plan for the future.
