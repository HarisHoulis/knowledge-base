# Fragments: August 18

Martin Fowler's curated fragments cover a range of topics, from organizational writing and conferences to the nature of AI intelligence. He highlights François Chollet's metaphor that intelligence is like a 'conversion ratio' with an optimality bound—making the ball rounder, not taller—suggesting AI may not see dramatic IQ-style gains but can improve through replication and speed. Fowler connects this to Noah Smith's idea that AI may instead unlock 'cloud laws' and understand complex human systems that resist simple rules. [1]

Fowler also reviews two practical analyses. First, 50+1's explainer on designing their 2026 election forecast page praises their use of annotated histograms and dot-based maps to avoid choropleth distortion and handle small screens. Second, Alex Stamos's newsletter on recent AI safety incidents critiques the U.S. government's shutdown of Anthropic's model, which injected political risk and pushed defenders to open-weight models like GLM 5.2. Stamos recommends keeping open-weight defensive models on the shelf and integrating LLM-based security checks into continuous delivery pipelines. [1]

Finally, Fowler summarizes The Economist's briefing on China's AI drive, noting the tension between a national push for AI dominance and the government's concern about workforce displacement amid a shrinking population. He closes with a wry observation that the half-life of engineering paradigms is becoming shorter than his annual leave. [1]

- AI intelligence may be bounded by an optimality curve; gains are likely in replicability and speed rather than unbounded IQ.
- AI could make sense of 'cloud laws' and complex distributed human knowledge, opening new directions beyond human-like intelligence.
- Election forecast visualization benefits from annotations and dot-density maps instead of misleading choropleths.
- AI safety incidents show the risk of depending on closed models; open-weight models are a crucial defensive fallback.
- China's AI push threatens the world's largest workforce while the country also faces demographic decline, creating policy clashes.
