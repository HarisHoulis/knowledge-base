# Fragments: June 16

Martin Fowler's June 2026 fragments survey the evolving role of LLMs in software development. Dave Thomas celebrates how LLMs have restored the joy of programming by removing drudgery, accelerating feedback loops, and enabling exploration of new technologies, countering fears that AI would diminish the craft (Fowler, “Fragments: June 16”). The article also highlights Chelsea Troy's framework of 'conversation registers' for LLM interactions, urging developers to maintain context hygiene by starting fresh conversations when shifting registers. 

Charity Majors raises a critical warning about the growing crevasse between AI enthusiasts and skeptics. Enthusiasts see real discontinuous leaps in capability, while skeptics fear the erosion of trust and reliability from shipping code faster than engineers can read it. Majors advocates for engineering discipline, honest reporting of costs, and engagement with real tradeoffs to prevent maximalist stances (Fowler, “Fragments: June 16”). Simon Willison observes that recent enterprise pricing hikes by Anthropic and OpenAI signal product-market fit for coding agents, marking April 2026 as a new inflection point with material budget impacts. 

Finally, Mike Masnick's analysis of the internet's failed promise of decentralization warns that centralized platforms inevitably lead to enshittification and despotification. He argues for user-controlled data and low exit barriers as a poison pill to exploitation, a lesson that Fowler suggests should guide the AI-enabled future by fostering decentralization and competition (Fowler, “Fragments: June 16”).

- LLMs are making programming more fun by eliminating drudgery and speeding up feedback loops, as reported by Dave Thomas.
- Chelsea Troy's 'registers' of LLM conversation emphasize managing context windows and starting fresh conversations when changing styles.
- Charity Majors warns of a dangerous split between AI enthusiasts and skeptics, calling for engineering discipline and honest reporting of costs and tradeoffs.
- Recent enterprise price hikes by Anthropic and OpenAI signal product-market fit for coding agents, marking April 2026 as a new inflection point.
- Mike Masnick argues that decentralization and user-controlled data are essential to prevent enshittification and despotification in the AI era.
