# Fragments: July 21

In this fragment, Martin Fowler wraps up notes from the Future of Software Development Retreat, referencing the Thoughtworks report which identifies five headline findings, most notably that code generation is no longer the bottleneck—verification is. The article highlights the executive/engineer expectation gap as a bigger risk than any technical limitation, illustrated by a cautionary tale of an ML model optimizing air filter replacements for desert equipment but deployed in the Arctic, where accumulated rotting mosquitoes caused a $100 billion fire loss. Fowler emphasizes the need for sensors and rapid feedback to guard against AI suggestions applied in new contexts. [Source: https://martinfowler.com/fragments/2026-07-21.html](https://martinfowler.com/fragments/2026-07-21.html)

The article discusses the risks of vibe coding by citizen developers, the need for controls and separate infrastructure to prevent security breaches, and the 'lethal trifecta' of data access. Fowler notes that management often overestimates LLM capabilities based on their performance in report writing, while engineers worry about shadow IT and the lack of observability in citizen-developer apps. He also reflects on the AI bubble, comparing it to the dotcom era, and observes that few compelling applications have emerged despite agentic programming hype. [Source: https://martinfowler.com/fragments/2026-07-21.html](https://martinfowler.com/fragments/2026-07-21.html)

Practical uses of LLMs are highlighted: agents help operations teams by analyzing event streams and collating data from repeated incidents, but their ability to auto-remediate is often overestimated because incident resolution is rarely linear. Fowler also points to DSLs as a sweet spot for LLMs, citing examples where custom languages improve token efficiency and enforce security boundaries. Finally, he critiques the prevalence of LLM-speak in writing, urging writers to reject AI-generated prose and instead 'Say Your Writing' to preserve individuality and credibility. [Source: https://martinfowler.com/fragments/2026-07-21.html](https://martinfowler.com/fragments/2026-07-21.html)

- Verification, not code generation, is the new bottleneck in AI-assisted software development.
- Executive/engineer expectation gaps and security risks are more pressing than technical limitations.
- LLMs are effective for operations and DSLs, but agents are overestimated for incident resolution.
- Writers should avoid LLM-speak; reading drafts aloud helps maintain a human voice.
