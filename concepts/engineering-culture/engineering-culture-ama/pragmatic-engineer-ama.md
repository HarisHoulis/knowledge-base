# The Pragmatic Engineer AMA

In this AMA episode, Gergely Orosz answers subscriber questions on the software industry, AI, hiring, engineering organizations, and career growth, with questions read by guest Volodymyr Giginiak. Orosz shares personal stories: The Pragmatic Engineer may not exist without COVID-19, as Uber layoffs prompted him to leave and write a book, eventually discovering he enjoyed writing. He also recounts nearly publishing an exposé on Bunq, but a message from an engineer who appreciated Bunq for sponsoring his visa changed his mind, leading Orosz to adopt a policy of writing about what works inside companies rather than what seems broken. Finally, he discusses his sole investigative piece on Pollen, which uncovered unpaid salaries and other issues after the CEO dismissed him as a 'nobody' (source: https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/the-pragmatic-engineer-ama).

- LeetCode-style interviews persist because they self-select for candidates willing to tolerate corporate nonsense, though AI may push companies back to in-person interviewing.
- MCP became an industry standard partly because Anthropic wasn't seen as a dominant threat at launch; today's competitive landscape would likely prevent similar adoption.
- AI doesn't necessarily make work easier; Orosz avoids AI in writing to prevent skill atrophy while accepting degradation in hand-coding.
- Investigative journalism is not for Orosz; his Pollen piece led to DMCA takedown attempts and taught him to focus on positive engineering culture stories.
- The Pragmatic Engineer's origin was accidental, triggered by COVID-era layoffs at Uber and a stalled book project.
