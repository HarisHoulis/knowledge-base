# Fragments: June 2 - AI Metrics, Automation, and Engineering Friction

Martin Fowler's June 2 fragments survey several recent essays on AI's impact on software engineering and knowledge work. He highlights Greg Wilson's critique of common AI productivity metrics—lines of code, tickets closed, and developer surveys—all of which are flawed in different ways. Fowler acknowledges the problems with subjective self-reporting but defends it as a weak but useful signal when better measures are unavailable, citing his own principle that productivity cannot be measured directly [Fowler, Fragments: June 2](https://martinfowler.com/fragments/2026-06-02.html).

- Common AI productivity metrics are flawed; even self-reported productivity is weak but sometimes the best available signal.
- Historical automation, like in accounting, increased rather than eliminated jobs, making AI job impact forecasts nearly impossible.
- Open AI models are catching up to closed models faster over time, but capability moats are shrinking.
- Hallucinated citations in AI-generated reports 'poison the well' of internet knowledge, and AI can also be used for security defense, as Mozilla showed.
- Technical debt is compounded by LLMs: models see cruft as precedent, and human attention is the 'GIL' bottleneck for AI agents.
