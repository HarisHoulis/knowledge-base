# The Conductor Developer

In this article, Rachel Laycock argues that the advent of AI in software development has shifted the bottleneck from coding to human attention. While AI has been framed as a productivity tool for writing code, the real constraint now is the developer's ability to orchestrate AI agents effectively. Laycock observes that the best developers are moving away from deep flow and toward a conductor-like role, managing multiple AI agents in parallel, providing context, and evaluating output (Laycock, martinfowler.com). 

Drawing an analogy to a CTO's work, Laycock explains that developers now need to manage streams of work, make decisions with incomplete information, and protect their attention and energy. She emphasizes the importance of learning to manage one's own energy rather than just time, and reducing unnecessary decisions. The article concludes that engineering careers must be redesigned for this new reality, where human attention becomes the scarce resource and developers take on more orchestrational responsibilities.

- AI changed what's scarce in software development: human attention is now the bottleneck.
- Great developers are becoming conductors, orchestrating multiple AI agents rather than writing all code themselves.
- Developers need executive-like skills: managing energy, protecting attention, reducing decisions, and creating systems.
- Engineering careers must be redesigned to prepare developers for this orchestrational role.
