# The Pragmatic Engineer AMA

In this AMA episode, Gergely Orosz shares personal stories behind The Pragmatic Engineer. He reveals that Uber layoffs during COVID-19 pushed him to leave and finish a book, which eventually led to writing full-time instead of founding a startup. He also recounts how a message from an engineer at Bunq changed his editorial policy: the engineer credited Bunq with sponsoring his visa and enabling his European tech career, so Orosz decided to focus on what works inside companies rather than publishing critical exposés.

- Orosz's career shift to writing was catalyzed by Uber's pandemic layoffs, not a planned pivot.
- A Bunq engineer's message convinced Orosz to adopt a positive editorial policy: write about what works, not what seems broken.
- Orosz's Pollen investigation uncovered unpaid salaries and a deliberate $3.2M double charge, but he decided investigative journalism wasn't for him.
- LeetCode-style interviews persist because they self-select for candidates tolerant of corporate nonsense and pointless work.
- MCP became an industry standard partly because Anthropic wasn't a dominant threat at launch, unlike Google's Agent2Agent protocol which faced adoption concerns.
- AI can cause skill atrophy; Orosz avoids AI in writing to preserve his skills, but accepts using it for coding despite hand-coding degradation.
