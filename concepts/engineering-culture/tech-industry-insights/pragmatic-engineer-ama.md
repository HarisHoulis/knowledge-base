# The Pragmatic Engineer AMA

In this AMA episode, Gergely Orosz shares personal stories behind The Pragmatic Engineer's origins and editorial choices. He reveals that the COVID-19 pandemic and Uber layoffs led him to leave his engineering manager role and focus on writing, ultimately discovering a passion for it. He also recounts deciding not to publish a critical article about Bunq after an engineer credited the company for giving him a career break, which shaped his policy of writing about what works in companies rather than what seems broken. His sole investigative piece on Pollen's collapse came about after the CEO dismissed him publicly, and led to findings of unpaid salaries and a deliberate double charge, but also convinced him investigative journalism is not for him.

- LeetCode-style interviews persist because they self-select for tolerance of corporate nonsense, and AI-driven solving may push companies back to in-person DSA interviews.
- MCP became an industry standard partly because Anthropic was not yet a dominant threat; Google's later Agent2Agent protocol saw no adoption due to Google's dominant position.
- AI does not make work easier; Orosz deliberately avoids AI in writing to prevent skill atrophy while accepting that his hand-coding ability will degrade.
- Personal editorial policy: focus on what works inside companies, influenced by a Bunq engineer's positive story, and avoid investigative journalism.
- The newsletter's existence was catalyzed by COVID-19 and Uber layoffs, leading to a career shift from engineering management to writing.
