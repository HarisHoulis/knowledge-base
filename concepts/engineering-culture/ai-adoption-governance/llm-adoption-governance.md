# Fragments: July 21 - AI Adoption, Vibe Coding, and Human Voice

The article also covers an experiment where law professors preferred LLM answers to student questions over peer answers (75.33% win rate), illustrating the distinction between interactional and contributory expertise. It endorses using domain-specific languages (DSLs) to make LLM output more reliable and token-efficient, citing examples from Unmesh Joshi and Spender Nelson. Finally, Fowler addresses the prevalence of 'LLM-speak' in writing, noting that it triggers visceral negative reactions and discredits content. He now encourages writers to reject AI polishing, suggests 'Say Your Writing' to preserve authentic voice, and sees an opportunity for those with distinctive human writing to gain credibility.

- Verification, not code generation, is now the bottleneck in AI-assisted software development; harness engineering is emerging as a key discipline.
- The executive/engineer expectation gap over LLM capabilities is a major risk, driven by board-level enthusiasm for cost-cutting and underestimation of security and context risks.
- Vibe coding by citizen developers creates shadow IT; organizations need controls like separate infrastructure, deterministic data-access rules, and threat-modeling sessions with boards.
- LLMs are useful for operations (anomaly detection, cross-referencing code and traces) but unreliable for autonomous incident resolution; agents may add unrequested features and need careful human oversight.
- The prevalence of LLM-speak is a growing problem; maintaining a distinctive human voice in writing is becoming more valuable, and reading drafts aloud helps combat AI-smoothing effects.
