# Fragments: July 21 - AI and Engineering Culture

Martin Fowler wraps up his notes from the Future of Software Development Retreat, pointing to the Thoughtworks report's five headline findings: code generation is no longer the bottleneck, verification is; harness engineering is emerging; there is an apprenticeship crisis; the executive/engineer expectation gap is a big risk; and legacy modernization is a clear near-term value pool. The article illustrates context-sensitivity with a story about ML models trained on desert equipment being used in the arctic, leading to mosquito-related fires and $100 billion in damage, emphasizing the need for sensors and rapid feedback. It also discusses board-vs-engineer divides around LLM adoption, risks of vibe coding, shadow IT, and the need for controls and separate infrastructure for citizen-developer apps.

- Verification is now the bottleneck, not code generation; organizations need governance and controls for vibe coding.
- Executives and engineers have an expectation gap: boards focus on cost-cutting and productivity, while engineers worry about security and context-specific risks.
- LLMs are helpful in operations for anomaly detection and incident understanding, but auto-remediation needs careful documentation and human feedback; agents struggle with non-linear incident resolution.
- DSLs can make LLMs more reliable and token-efficient, enforcing security boundaries; the semantic model underneath matters more than the DSL itself.
- Pervasive 'LLM-speak' is causing readers to reject AI-generated prose; writers should 'Say Your Writing' to preserve a distinctive human voice.
