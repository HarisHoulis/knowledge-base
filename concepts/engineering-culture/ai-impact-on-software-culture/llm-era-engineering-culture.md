# Fragments: June 16

Martin Fowler shares reflections on how LLMs are reshaping programming culture. Dave Thomas, co-author of The Pragmatic Programmer, found that LLMs made programming more enjoyable by removing drudgery, speeding up feedback loops, reviving abandoned projects, and enabling exploration of new technologies—contrary to fears that they would kill the fun. Fowler also highlights that Domain-Driven Design remains relevant, perhaps even more important, in the LLM era, as evidenced by Eric Evans' keynote at DDD Europe 2026 (Fowler, Fragments: June 16).

Chelsea Troy's talk on managing LLM context windows introduced the idea of different conversational registers with LLMs, urging developers to be conscious of which register they are using and to start fresh conversations when changing registers to maintain healthy context. Charity Majors warns of a growing crevasse between AI enthusiasts and skeptics, noting both sides have valid existential threats: enthusiasts see real capability leaps, while skeptics fear degraded reliability and eroded institutional knowledge. She calls for engineering discipline, honest storytelling about costs, and earning credibility through engagement (Fowler, Fragments: June 16).

Simon Willison observes that Anthropic and OpenAI raising enterprise prices signals product-market fit for coding agents like Claude Code and Codex, with April 2026 marking a new inflection point for revenue implications. Mike Masnick reflects on the internet's failed promise of decentralization, arguing that centralized platforms led to enshittification and lock-in. He advocates for user-controlled data and easy exit to foster competition and cognitive liberty, lessons that should guide the AI-enabled future (Fowler, Fragments: June 16).

- LLMs have made programming more fun, not less, by removing drudgery and accelerating feedback loops.
- Domain-Driven Design remains important in the LLM era, as explored at DDD Europe 2026.
- Managing LLM context windows requires awareness of different conversational registers and fresh contexts when switching.
- AI enthusiasts and skeptics both raise valid existential threats; engineering discipline and credible engagement are key.
- Centralization leads to enshittification; decentralization with user-controlled data and easy exit is worth striving for.
