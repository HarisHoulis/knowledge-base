# Headed for the Exit: the Great Engineering Leader Career Break

A significant number of engineering leaders—CTOs, VPs of Engineering, and heads of engineering—are choosing to leave their high-status roles, often without another job lined up. Based on conversations with nearly 20 leaders on career breaks or considering one, the article identifies ten common reasons for this exodus, including deteriorating job conditions, the startup losing value, and the pressure to become AI-native or risk becoming irrelevant. The trend is notable enough that a majority of CTO-level peers the author spoke to are either considering or already taking extended time off (Orosz, 2026).

- Unrealistic expectations from founders, especially around AI transformation, are the leading cause of jobs turning bad for CTOs and VPEs, with demands to cut costs 20-50% and ship AI features at an unsustainable pace.
- Equity often becomes worthless due to liquidation preferences; a CTO with a 2% grant explained that a 2x preference from Series A investors meant the company needed to sell for at least $210M before common shareholders saw any money.
- Many leaders feel they must leave their current roles to gain hands-on AI experience and stay relevant, with some considering downleveling to IC roles to achieve this.
- The VPE/CTO role is increasingly seen as 'low ROI' due to pressure to inflect velocity with AI, while engineering morale suffers from 'founder slop'—low-quality AI-generated code shipped by founders who don't own the consequences.
