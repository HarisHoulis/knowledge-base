# Fragments: July 6 - Agentic Development Matures

At the second Future of Software Development Retreat in Engelberg, participants reported a notable shift from the hesitancy of the first retreat to confidence that agentic development delivers real value. As Giles Edwards-Alexander observed, "the evidence is in," and Greg Herlein noted that everyone was "shipping it. Not slides - production." The debate over whether this changes software engineering is over; the focus is now on how to do it effectively (Fowler, 2026).

A key open question is whether architecture and design still matter. One hypothesis suggests that powerful LLMs can handle poorly structured code, while another, encapsulated by Laura Tacho's phrase "the Venn Diagram of Developer Experience and Agent Experience is a circle," holds that agents benefit from the same modularity and clear naming that help humans. Mathias Verraes adds that good design is a hedge against dependence on AI, given uncertain future costs and potential access restrictions. Additionally, token costs are emerging as a major concern, with 404 Media reporting companies throttling AI usage and even exploring tactics like making models "speak like cavemen" to reduce expenses (Fowler, 2026).

On ethics, Charity Majors argues that renouncing AI use is not an effective ethical stance; instead, engagement and action are needed. The article also notes Gergely Orosz's experience with a fraudulent DMCA takedown, highlighting risks to online content. Overall, the retreat shows agentic engineering is rapidly moving from aspiration to practice, while raising practical and ethical challenges that require ongoing attention.

- Agentic development has shifted from speculation to production, with attendees reporting concrete deployments and real-world evidence.
- Software architecture remains relevant: agents and humans both benefit from clear modularity and naming, supporting the 'Agent Experience' view.
- Token costs are rising sharply, prompting companies to throttle AI usage, seek cheaper models, and even use unconventional prompt tricks.
- Engaging with AI ethically requires participation and action rather than purity or disengagement, according to Charity Majors.
- The long-term availability and cost of AI tools are uncertain, making good design a prudent strategy to mitigate dependence.
