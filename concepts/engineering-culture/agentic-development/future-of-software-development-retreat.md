# Fragments: July 6

Martin Fowler reports from the second Future of Software Development Retreat in Europe, noting a shift from the hesitancy of the first retreat to a confident sense that agentic development's value is now evident. Participants are shipping production systems, and the debate has moved from whether this changes software engineering to how it does. Emerging patterns and practices are being catalogued, but there is still work to establish what is effective and when.

A key concern is rising token costs, with companies throttling AI usage and seeking ways to reduce expenses. Fowler also discusses the importance of good software design, citing the view that what helps humans understand code also helps AI agents. Ethics of AI use are explored through Charity Majors' perspective that withdrawal is not ethical; instead, engagement and building are the way to drive change. The post also covers a fraudulent DMCA claim against Gergely Orosz and a historical reflection on the Declaration of Independence.

- The retreat showed a marked shift from aspirational talk to confident, real-world adoption of agentic development.
- Token costs are escalating rapidly, prompting companies to throttle AI usage and seek cost-control measures.
- Good architecture and naming are as important for AI agents as for human developers, contrary to the idea that AI can handle spaghetti code.
- Ethical engagement with AI requires active participation and building, not purity or withdrawal.
- A fraudulent DMCA claim against Gergely Orosz illustrates vulnerabilities in information control that are exacerbated by AI-era legal tools.
