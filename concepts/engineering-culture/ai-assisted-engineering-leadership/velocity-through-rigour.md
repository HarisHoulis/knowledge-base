# Velocity Through Rigour: Then & Now

Cate Huston draws on her experience scaling remote engineering teams at Automattic and DuckDuckGo, and her recent move to a fractional CTO role at a startup, to argue that the same management principles that make large human teams effective also apply to working with AI. She frames the talk around two questions: what proportion of results should come from 'fuses' (a garbled transcript term, likely referring to AI agents), and which of our usual ways of working remain relevant when the industry is turned upside down.

- The talk compares lessons from leading large human engineering teams with lessons from building software alongside AI agents.
- Huston describes migrating a startup platform from Bubble's no-code solution to a standard architecture with a full database, a four-month effort done by two engineers plus a six-week intern.
- She emphasizes creating the conditions for change before changing the platform, rather than switching immediately on day one.
- Most issues were caught and fixed before release, with relatively few hotfixes, which she attributes to rigour in the process.
