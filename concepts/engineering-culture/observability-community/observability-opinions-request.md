# Got opinions on observability? I could use your help (once more, with feeling)

Charity Majors, author of the upcoming second edition of her book, makes a public call for reader input on observability and software buying. She recounts that a previous plea for advice on buying software received an overwhelming response, teaching her that vendor engineering is a subtle and powerful form of deep technical expertise. She argues that choosing and managing tools is one of the few ways staff+ engineers can develop executive-level communication, strategy, and execution skills while remaining individual contributors. This context frames her new set of questions, which aim to fill gaps in her perspective, especially from engineers who are happy with traditional three-pillars observability tools like Datadog or Grafana.

The article lists a detailed set of open-ended questions covering migration between observability vendors, cost-cutting and control, telemetry pipelines, team structure and ownership, build-versus-buy decisions, OpenTelemetry adoption refusals, reducing instrumentation cognitive overhead, tool consolidation versus proliferation, and open source tooling strategy. She explicitly says respondents need not answer everything and encourages them to raise unasked questions. She asks for email responses within one week, offers to credit contributors by name without mentioning their employers, and notes that the title is a reference to the Buffy musical.

Majors also acknowledges a possible selection bias in her network, noting she knows few people who are satisfied with the three-pillars model, and asks for perspectives from those who are. Her tone is urgent and grateful, with a postscript grammar quiz on the prepositions 'about' vs 'on', reflecting her characteristic informal style.

- Vendor engineering is described as high-leverage work for senior engineers, capable of affecting hundreds or thousands of engineers and all customers.
- The article serves as a public request for reader contributions to the second edition of Charity Majors' book, specifically on observability and buying software.
- Questions cover a wide range of practical observability topics, including vendor migration, cost control, telemetry pipelines, build vs buy, OTel decisions, and team ownership.
- Majors seeks input from people happy with traditional three-pillars observability to counter selection bias in her network.
- Answers are requested within one week via email, with credit given by name but not employer.
