# The Smoothest Reorg: Lessons in Collaborative Organizational Design

Joakim Sundén, who worked at Spotify from 2011 to 2017 as it grew from about eight teams to 200 and later founded Better Product Work, presents lessons from applying a collaborative organizational design approach developed at Spotify and adapted across many other organizations. He opens by noting that audiences associate "reorganization" with words like waste of time, chaos, and inquisition, and asks why reorganizations meant to improve things are so often experienced negatively.

- Top-down reorganizations fail because leaders design around business units rather than real work processes, creating new dependencies and slowing teams down.
- People resist change not for its own sake but when goals are unclear, changes seem arbitrary, or they have no influence over their formation.
- Treating reorganization as a grand upfront plan (waterfall-style) rather than an iterative process of discovery leads to a death spiral of repeated reorganizations.
- Leaders justify directive reorgs via strategy ownership and budget/downsizing constraints, though Sundén argues even layoff contexts can be handled collaboratively.
