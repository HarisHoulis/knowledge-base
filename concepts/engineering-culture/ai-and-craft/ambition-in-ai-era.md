# Execution Is Free: Redefining Ambition in the AI Era

Jason Liu of OpenAI's UI team argues that as AI makes execution cheap, ambition shifts from how you build to how you approach what you build. He frames ambition around three principles: believing you own and created the work, defining your taste and knowing what quality looks like so you can direct the model, and using AI to master new fields rather than fearing replacement. He notes his own role evolved from interface developer to overall experience owner as tools like Codex emerged.

Liu addresses three common fears about AI: that it will produce more junk, erode critical thinking, and replace people. He draws an analogy to the invention of the camera, citing Walter Benjamin's essay on art in the age of technical reproduction—photography freed the hand's reproductive function and transferred it to the eye, just as AI now shifts work toward direction and vision. Early worries that photography would destroy art proved wrong; instead it created new forms like cinema.

He acknowledges that ubiquitous cameras produce countless forgettable photos, but what matters is capturing a few truly important moments. Similarly, AI-generated output may include junk, but the value lies in whether you can direct the tool toward meaningful results. He prefers to think of himself not as a developer but as someone who turns ideas into reality.

- Execution is now free, so ambition means owning your work, defining taste, and knowing what quality looks like
- AI shifts work from hands-on reproduction to direction and vision, much like photography did for art
- The camera analogy: new tools create junk but also new expressive forms like cinema
- Developers should use AI to broaden into other fields rather than fear replacement
