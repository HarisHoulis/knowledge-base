# The Ladder Is Missing Rungs: Engineering Progression When AI Ate the Middle

Alasdair Allan presents empirical research from METR and Anthropic on how AI coding adoption is reshaping engineering career progression. The central concern is the "supervision paradox": as engineers increasingly rely on AI tools, the traditional middle rungs of the career ladder—where juniors build skills through hands-on problem solving—are eroded.

Because AI absorbs much of the intermediate work, junior developers get fewer opportunities to develop the judgment and expertise needed to eventually supervise autonomous systems. Allan argues this disrupts junior skill development and risks institutional context loss, as the tacit knowledge embedded in that middle layer is no longer transferred through practice.

The long-term consequence is a weakened engineering pipeline: organizations may lack engineers capable of effectively overseeing increasingly autonomous AI systems, precisely because the formative experiences that produce such oversight capability are being removed.

- AI coding adoption creates a "supervision paradox": reliance on AI tools undermines the skill development needed to supervise AI.
- The middle rungs of the engineering career ladder—where juniors develop expertise—are being eroded.
- Institutional context and tacit knowledge risk being lost as intermediate work is automated.
- A weakened long-term pipeline threatens the ability to oversee autonomous systems effectively.
