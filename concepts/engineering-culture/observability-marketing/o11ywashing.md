# From Cloudwashing to O11ywashing

Charity Majors argues that the term 'observability' has been co-opted by vendors and executives, becoming a buzzword divorced from its original meaning. She recounts a panel where an executive described traditional observability tools as those that check if systems are up or down, and then expressed a need to measure customer experience from each individual's perspective—unaware that this is precisely what observability was meant to be. This illustrates how far the industry has strayed from the concept, reducing it to mere monitoring. Majors coins 'o11ywashing' to describe the marketing phenomenon where any telemetry-related product is rebranded as observability, similar to how IBM labeled mainframes as 'cloud' in 2008. She asserts that true observability requires unifying app, business, and system telemetry to enable slicing by customer ID or device, something traditional metrics-logs-traces tools cannot do because they remain siloed. The article concludes that the industry must communicate observation results in terms of business outcomes to engineering executives, not just technical details, and offers a simple litmus test: if a tool cannot help understand product quality from each customer's perspective, it is not observability—just monitoring wearing a marketing disguise.

- The executive's desire to measure each customer's experience is the original definition of observability, but the term has been reduced to uptime monitoring in mainstream usage.
- O11ywashing is the practice of rebranding monitoring tools as observability, analogous to cloudwashing with mainframes.
- Traditional three-pillar tools (metrics, logs, traces) cannot solve the problem of customer-centric quality because they are not unified.
- To counter o11ywashing, the industry must tell results-oriented stories to executives, not just engineers.
- A litmus test: if your 'observability' tool doesn't let you understand each customer's perspective, it's not observability.
