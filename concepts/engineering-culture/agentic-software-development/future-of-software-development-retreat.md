# Fragments: July 6

Martin Fowler reflects on the second Future of Software Development Retreat in Europe, noting a shift from aspirational discussions to concrete production use of agentic development. Participants reported shipping agentic systems, and the debate about whether this changes software engineering is over, replaced by debates on how to do it effectively. Fowler also observes the emergence of new terms like 'harness engineering' and growing concerns about token costs, which were barely mentioned at the previous retreat. (Source: https://martinfowler.com/fragments/2026-07-06.html)

A key ongoing question is whether architecture and design remain important in the agentic age. Two contrasting hypotheses are presented: one that AI models (the 'Genie') can handle poorly structured code, and another that agent experience is a circle overlapping with developer experience, meaning good modularity and naming help both humans and AI. Mathias Verraes adds that good design is a hedge against dependence on AI, given uncertainties about costs, government restrictions, and public opposition. (Source: https://martinfowler.com/fragments/2026-07-06.html)

Charity Majors' post on the ethics of working with AI resonates with Fowler. She acknowledges harms like training on stolen data and slop, but argues that renouncing AI provides no ethical gain; instead, one should engage and build. The article also highlights rising token costs, citing 404 Media reports of companies throttling AI usage and even making AI 'speak like cavemen' to reduce expenses. Finally, Fowler shares a link to Bret Devereaux's reading of the Declaration of Independence, using history to gain perspective on current affairs. (Source: https://martinfowler.com/fragments/2026-07-06.html)

- Agentic development has moved from aspiration to production; practitioners now debate how, not whether.
- Architecture and design remain important: good modularity helps both humans and AI agents, and serves as a hedge against AI dependency risks.
- Token costs are rapidly escalating, leading companies to throttle AI usage and seek cost-reduction measures.
- Ethical engagement with AI involves showing up and building, not purity or renunciation.
