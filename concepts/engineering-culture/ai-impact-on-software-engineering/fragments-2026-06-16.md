# Fragments: June 16

Simon Willison interprets recent enterprise pricing increases by Anthropic and OpenAI as evidence of product-market fit, particularly for coding agents like Claude Code and Codex, with April 2026 possibly a new inflection point. Finally, Mike Masnick's retrospective on the internet's decentralization argues that new middlemen replaced old gatekeepers but built attention-driven models that led to enshittification and lock-in. Masnick advocates for decentralization and low exit barriers to preserve cognitive liberty and competition, a lesson Fowler suggests should guide the AI-enabled future (Fowler, 2026).

- LLMs are making programming more enjoyable and productive by removing drudgery, accelerating feedback loops, and enabling exploration.
- When working with LLMs, be conscious of conversational registers; changing register calls for a fresh context window.
- AI enthusiasts and skeptics both identify real threats, so we need engineering discipline, honest storytelling, and engagement to bridge the gap.
- Rising enterprise pricing for AI coding agents signals product-market fit and a potential inflection point in April 2026.
- Decentralization, user control over data, and easy exit barriers remain important to counter enshittification and preserve competition.
