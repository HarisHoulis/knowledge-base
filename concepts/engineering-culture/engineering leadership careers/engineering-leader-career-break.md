# Headed for the Exit: the Great Engineering Leader Career Break

Gergely Orosz reports on an unprecedented wave of engineering leaders—CTOs, VPs of Engineering, and heads of engineering—opting out or taking prolonged career breaks, often without a next job lined up. Based on conversations with nearly 20 such leaders, he identifies ten common reasons, including worsening job conditions, startup decline, burnout, and the pressure to be 'AI-native' or risk becoming irrelevant. Many leaders feel their roles have become low-ROI due to unrealistic founder expectations around AI, such as expecting a CTO to magically transform the company, cut costs by 20-50%, or ship 'founder slop'—half-baked AI prototypes treated as full products (Orosz, 2026).

- A record number of engineering leaders are quitting or taking career breaks, driven by worsening conditions, AI pressure, burnout, and worthless equity.
- Founder 'AI psychosis' and unrealistic expectations—like shipping slop PRs or cutting 20-50% of costs—are making CTO/VPE roles increasingly difficult and low-ROI.
- Equity often becomes worthless due to liquidation preferences and declining startup valuations, removing a major financial incentive to stay.
- Leaders feel they must get AI experience to stay relevant, even if it means moving to IC roles or quitting; but many lack the change-management skills to lead AI transformations.
- Engineering leaders should evaluate whether their company is a 'winner' in the AI era and consider exits or breaks when the trajectory is unfavorable.
