# Fragments: May 27

Martin Fowler's May 27 fragment post collects observations on LLM-augmented programming, open source policy, cognitive load, and the societal impact of AI. He highlights a case study where a legacy codebase was restructured using AI assistance, emphasizing that a foundation of tests, static analysis, and good architecture was essential to move from 'in-the-loop' micromanagement to 'on-the-loop' curation. Fowler also discusses Adam Tornhill's concept of 'compressed cognition'—how agentic coding increases decision density and mental fatigue—and the need for small tasks, automation, and verification to manage it.

- AI code assistants become far more effective when the codebase already has characterization tests, static analysis, and clear architectural patterns, enabling a shift from reviewing every edit to curating outcomes.
- Agentic coding is mentally expensive because it increases decision density; developers should keep agent tasks small, automate ruthlessly, and rely on verification rather than reading every line.
- Closing open source repositories as an AI security measure is counterproductive; transparency and secure-by-design practices are better defenses.
- Early evidence suggests AI is already affecting graduate employment, with more AI-exposed fields seeing larger employment drops, and regulation remains weak and erratic.
- Technical expertise is almost absent from government AI policy discussions, which is why Fowler endorses a software developer, Beth Anders-Beck, for Congress.
