# State of the software engineering job market in 2026, part 2

The 2026 tech job market continues to recover overall, but with stark divergences. Top AI labs—especially Anthropic and OpenAI—are now more attractive to candidates than Big Tech. Anthropic leads in interview coaching requests, driven by its market momentum, IPO filing, and perceived principled stance. These labs are also hiring from a wide range of companies, with Anthropic showing standout 2-year retention at 80% (Orosz, 2026). Early-career engineers face a tougher landscape: intern intakes have fallen even as overall software engineering hiring rebounds, and new graduate hiring as a share of all SWE hires dropped to one in ten in 2025. Pedigree from elite universities is increasingly important (Orosz, 2026). Role demand is shifting—AI engineering and Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) positions are surging, while frontend-only and native mobile roles are declining. Compensation follows this shift: AI engineers command higher salaries, especially at the 80th percentile where senior AI roles can exceed $300K base, outpacing general SWE compensation. Management layers continue to flatten, with fewer engineering managers per engineer, and Big Tech tenure is rising as fewer employees switch jobs (Orosz, 2026).

- Anthropic and OpenAI are the most desired AI employers, with Anthropic capturing 35% of interview coaching requests and OpenAI 16%, outpacing Google and other Big Tech.
- Intern and new grad hiring have declined despite overall SWE hiring recovery; only 1 in 10 SWE hires in 2025 were recent grads, down from nearly 3 in 10 in 2023, making elite university credentials more decisive.
- AI engineering and Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) roles are the fastest-growing job titles, while frontend-only and native mobile engineer roles are shrinking.
- AI engineering compensation exceeds general SWE compensation; senior AI engineers at the 80th percentile in the US often earn $300K+ base salary.
- The 'great flattening' of management continues, with fewer engineering managers per engineer, and Big Tech employees are staying longer in their roles since 2023.
