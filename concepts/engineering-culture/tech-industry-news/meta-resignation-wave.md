# The Pulse: Meta’s self-inflicted resignation-wave

According to The Pulse by Gergely Orosz (2026), Meta's layoffs and forced reassignments have triggered a resignation wave among engineers who were not directly impacted. Despite Meta offering large equity retainers to keep these employees, the retention efforts appear ineffective, as many are choosing to leave anyway. This self-inflicted wave highlights the unintended consequences of broad restructuring on morale and loyalty (Orosz, 2026).

The article also covers Grok Bot, a generic AI harness built by the Cursor team, described as the 'Codex experience, but for knowledge work.' The author experimented with it and found it highly useful for automating daily workflows, predicting that more AI vendors will copy the harness. This segment shifts focus to AI-driven productivity tools, suggesting a growing trend of agent-based automation for knowledge workers (Orosz, 2026).

- Meta's layoffs and forced reassignments have prompted unaffected engineers to seek new jobs, creating a resignation wave.
- Meta is offering large equity retainers to retain talent, but these efforts are not proving successful.
- The Cursor team released Grok Bot, an AI harness for knowledge work, which the author describes as a 'Codex experience' for general tasks.
- The author predicts more AI vendors will copy the Grok Bot harness due to its utility in automating workflows.
- The Pulse series covers events and trends in Big Tech, with this issue focusing on talent retention and AI agents.
