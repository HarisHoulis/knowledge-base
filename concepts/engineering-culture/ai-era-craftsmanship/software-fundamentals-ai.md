# Software Fundamentals in the Age of AI

In this live conversation, Matt Pocock interviews Robert C. Martin (Uncle Bob), a software engineer with over half a century of experience, beginning with his first program in 1964 at age 12. Uncle Bob shares how he wrote programs on paper and executed them in his head before entering the industry, and he reflects on the lasting importance of clean code principles, which he popularized through his book 'Clean Code' [1]. The discussion turns to AI: Uncle Bob describes being initially unimpressed, then experimenting with early agents like Grock and finding that while the AI could generate code quickly, it left 'little dog doo-doo' behind and required constant cleanup [1]. His key point is that AI accelerates coding but does not remove the need for rigorous software fundamentals; a skilled engineer must be able to clean up, refine, and direct the AI's output, reinforcing that craftsmanship remains essential in the age of AI [1].

- Uncle Bob's career spans more than 50 years, starting with small analog machines and learning programming from books before having access to actual computers.
- He observed that early AI agents like Grock could write code but made a mess, requiring the developer to constantly clean up after them.
- AI tools can be useful assistants, but they do not replace the engineer's ability to maintain code quality and apply foundational software principles.
