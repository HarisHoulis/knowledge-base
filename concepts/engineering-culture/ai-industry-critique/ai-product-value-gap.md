# Nilay Patel on the AI Industry's Cost-Benefit Gap

In a quote shared by Simon Willison, Nilay Patel argues that current AI products do not justify their costs. He points to the environmental burden of data centers, low-quality outputs like "hilarious deep fake slop," and the gap between spending and revenue across AI companies, noting that agents "aren't real" and may not be used as intended (Willison, 2026).

Patel connects these costs to political corruption, describing CEOs courting the White House and funding projects the public does not want as "crypto-corruption" that still produces slop. His conclusion is that the industry cannot escape this dynamic through marketing or persuasion—only by building products that visibly make people's lives better.

- AI products currently fail to justify their environmental and financial costs
- Companies are spending more than they earn on AI products, and agents remain unproven
- Political influence and corruption compound the cost problem without improving output quality
- The industry's path out is better products, not ad campaigns or persuasion
