# Hello World

Charity Majors announces her decision to stay on Substack after initially planning to migrate from WordPress, responding to critics who accused her of supporting Nazis by using the platform. She explains that she paused migration to research the LeaveSubstack movement and consult colleagues, ultimately concluding that her primary responsibility is to re-engage with the technical community, which currently still largely resides on X. She sees Substack as a "light dove grey" platform, not comparable to outright extremist sites, and feels more ethically compromised by other services like YouTube. She outlines practical measures to minimize Substack's revenue from her presence, such as not accepting payments and encouraging RSS/email use, while acknowledging that some readers may still object. Ultimately, she frames this as a necessary trade-off to fulfill her job of mixing it up in mainstream technical discussions.

- Majors decided to stay on Substack after considering the LeaveSubstack arguments, concluding it is far from extremist platforms.
- Her primary goal is to re-engage with the tech mainstream, which still exists on X, but she prefers to use Substack and LinkedIn.
- Substack reduces friction for posting compared to WordPress, enabling her to write more frequently.
- She plans to minimize financial support to Substack by not accepting paid subscriptions and promoting RSS/email consumption.
- She respects that others may view the platform differently and choose not to read her content.
