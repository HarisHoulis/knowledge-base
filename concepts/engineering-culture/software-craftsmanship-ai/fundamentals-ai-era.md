# Uncle Bob on Software Fundamentals in the Age of AI

In this live interview, Robert C. Martin ("Uncle Bob") reflects on his long career in software development and shares his evolving perspective on AI tools. He acknowledges that he was initially unimpressed with early AI code generators but later began experimenting with agents like Grok. While these tools can write code quickly, Uncle Bob stresses that they often produce messy output, requiring developers to constantly clean up after them. He implies that deeply understanding software fundamentals—such as clean code principles—is more important than ever when working with AI, because the human developer remains responsible for guiding and correcting AI-generated contributions.

Throughout the conversation, Uncle Bob draws on his half-century of programming experience, from his first program in 1964 to his famous book "Clean Code." His central concern is that AI amplifies the need for disciplined engineering practices rather than replacing them. The interview highlights a key tension: AI accelerates coding but introduces new kinds of mess, so developers must rely on foundational skills to maintain quality, security, and maintainability.

- Emphasizes that AI-generated code is fast but messy, requiring programmers to clean up after it.
- Argues that software fundamentals and coding discipline become more important in the age of AI.
- Draws on personal experience with AI agents like Grok to illustrate both their potential and their shortcomings.
- Uncle Bob's long career gives weight to the view that foundational principles do not change with new tools.
