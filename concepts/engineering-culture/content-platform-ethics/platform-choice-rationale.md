# Hello World

Charity Majors explains her decision to stay on Substack after facing criticism for moving from Wordpress. She initially paused migration after backlash but, after research and conversations, decided to stay because her primary goal is to re-engage with the technical community, which increasingly exists on Substack and other platforms. She acknowledges the ethical concerns but argues Substack is not a platform solely for extremists, comparing it to a 'light dove grey' among darker alternatives. To minimize benefit to Substack, she refuses paid subscriptions, encourages RSS/email use, and plans to auto-post to Wordpress. She respects those who disagree and choose to boycott entirely.

- Charity Majors decided to remain on Substack despite criticism, after pausing migration and researching the LeaveSubstack movement.
- Her main motivation is to return to active technical discourse, which she sees as a core part of her job, and Substack lowers friction for writing.
- She argues Substack is not equivalent to platforms that exclusively host extremists, but acknowledges it operates on a continuum of grey.
- To reduce the financial benefit to Substack, she accepts no paid subscriptions and directs readers to use RSS or email.
- She respects individual choices to boycott Substack but states this battle is not hers to fight.
