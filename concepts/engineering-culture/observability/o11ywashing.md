# From Cloudwashing to O11ywashing

Charity Majors criticizes industry executives who separate 'traditional observability' (uptime monitoring) from measuring customer experience, noting that true observability inherently includes understanding service quality from each customer's perspective. She argues that the three-pillar model (metrics, logs, traces) is monitoring, not observability, and that custom tooling is unnecessary if vendors would unify telemetry. This failure to recognize observability's original definition leads to 'o11ywashing'—a term borrowed from 'cloudwashing' where vendors rebrand legacy tools as observability.

- Traditional 'observability' tools are really monitoring tools; they only answer whether systems are up or down.
- True observability means combining app, business, and system telemetry to understand each customer's experience.
- Vendors are 'o11ywashing' by rebranding monitoring as observability, similar to IBM calling mainframes 'cloud'.
- The industry must tell outcome-focused stories to engineering executives, not just technical details.
- If tooling can't slice by customer ID or device, it isn't observability—it's marketing.
