# From Cloudwashing to O11ywashing

Charity Majors argues that the term "traditional observability" has been effectively co-opted to mean basic monitoring, while the original promise of observability—understanding system quality from each individual customer's perspective—remains unsolved. She recounts a panel where an executive praised traditional tools for detecting outages but then described building custom solutions to measure customer workflows, unaware that he was re-describing the core definition of observability (Majors, 2025). This disconnect highlights a widespread failure: vendors label their monitoring products as observability, a phenomenon Majors dubs "o11ywashing," analogous to the cloudwashing of the past. She emphasizes that executives need to hear about outcomes, not just technical details, and warns that if "observability" tooling doesn't help you understand quality from every customer's perspective, "it isn't fucking observability" (Majors, 2025).

- Observability is a systems problem, not just about uptime or nines; it requires unifying app, business, and system telemetry.
- The three-pillar model (metrics, logs, traces) is essentially monitoring, which is insufficient for customer-centric quality analysis.
- O11ywashing is the practice of rebranding monitoring as observability, mirroring cloudwashing in the cloud era.
- The industry must communicate the value of observability to engineering executives in terms of business outcomes, not data structures.
- If your tooling doesn't help you understand the quality of product from each customer's perspective, it's not observability—it's monitoring dressed up in marketing.
