# Martin Fowler told me the second edition should be shorter (it's twice as long)

Charity Majors recounts meeting Martin Fowler, who advised that second editions of books should be shorter, as shorter books are better. However, she admits that the second edition of "Observability Engineering" is nearly twice as long as the first, with almost completely rewritten content. The new edition incorporates lessons learned since 2018, including the rise of OpenTelemetry, the expansion of observability definitions, and the growing role of AI in both instrumenting and analyzing production systems.

The book is now written primarily for software engineers, covering instrumentation fundamentals, analysis workflows with and without AI, observability use cases, and technical deep dives. It features contributions from numerous experts on topics like mobile/frontend observability, fast feedback loops with LLMs, ClickHouse for observability workloads, and telemetry pipelines. A new final section on observability governance targets platform and observability teams, addressing cost containment, tool migrations, and organizational transformation. Majors describes the challenges of writing this section, including a period of writer's block that led her to scrap and restart her work.

- The second edition of Observability Engineering is almost twice as long, contrary to Martin Fowler's advice that second editions should be shorter.
- Almost all content from the first edition was rewritten, with only about 10% carried over, reflecting massive industry changes since 2018.
- The book now targets software engineers and includes sections on instrumentation, analysis workflows with and without AI, use cases, technical deep dives, and observability governance.
- OpenTelemetry has won the integrations war, and most companies still lack real observability, which the book addresses.
- The governance section was the hardest to write, causing a multi-week writer's block that eventually led to starting over.
