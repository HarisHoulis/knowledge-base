# Got opinions on observability? I could use your help (once more, with feeling)

The article also invites feedback on building versus buying observability tools, reasons for not adopting OpenTelemetry, reducing instrumentation cognitive overhead, tool consolidation versus proliferation, and the role of open source tools. Majors asks for email responses, noting she may print contributors' names but not employers, and mentions a week-long window for feedback. The post includes a playful grammar note about "opinions on" versus "opinions about" and references a Buffy musical.

- Vendor engineering is a subtle and powerful form of deep subject matter expertise, offering high leverage for individual contributors.
- Migration between observability vendors is complex; experience-sharing can reveal surprising challenges and hard-won lessons.
- Managing costs in three-pillars observability (metrics, logs, traces) requires deliberate strategy, including cardinality control and telemetry pipelines.
- Observability team size, placement, and ownership vary significantly by organization size and structure, especially in large enterprises.
- OpenTelemetry adoption may be rejected for technical or philosophical reasons, not just resource constraints.
