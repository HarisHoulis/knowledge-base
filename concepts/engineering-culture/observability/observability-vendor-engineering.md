# Got opinions on observability? I could use your help (once more, with feeling)

Charity Majors follows up on a previous request for advice about buying software, sharing that the response was overwhelming and informative. She argues that vendor engineering is a subtle and powerful form of deep technical expertise, allowing staff+ engineers to exercise executive-level strategy and communication while remaining individual contributors (Majors, 2025). She is incorporating these insights into the second edition of her book and is now soliciting input on observability-specific topics (Majors, 2025).

She poses a wide range of questions to address potential selection bias in her network, particularly around traditional three-pillar observability models. Questions cover vendor migrations, cost control, OpenTelemetry adoption decisions, instrumentation cognitive load, tool consolidation, and open-source strategies. She also asks about organizational structures like observability team size and reporting lines, and how to guide engineers on data routing and cardinality management (Majors, 2025).

She requests email responses within a week, offering to credit contributors by name but not employer. The post ends with a grammar note on “opinions about” vs. “opinions on,” and a Buffy musical reference (Majors, 2025).

- Vendor engineering is a high-leverage application of engineering expertise, capable of influencing many engineers and customers per dollar spent.
- Majors is gathering diverse opinions on observability to counter selection bias in her network, especially from users of traditional three-pillar tools.
- Key topics include vendor migration lessons, cost control strategies, OpenTelemetry trade-offs, automation of instrumentation, and build-vs-buy decisions.
- She asks about organizational ownership of observability, team sizes, and the role of telemetry pipelines in large enterprises.
- Contributors can be named but not tied to their employers, and submissions are needed within a week.
