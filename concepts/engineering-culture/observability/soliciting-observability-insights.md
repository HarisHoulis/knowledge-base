# Got Opinions on Observability? I Could Use Your Help (Once More, with Feeling)

Charity Majors asks readers for their experiences and strong opinions on observability to inform the second edition of her book, following an earlier request about buying software. She argues that vendor engineering is a subtle and powerful form of engineering expertise, allowing staff+ engineers to develop executive-level skills while remaining individual contributors and making decisions that leverage the work of hundreds of engineers ([source](https://charity.wtf/p/got-opinions-on-observability-i-could-use-your-help-once-more-with-feeling)). She explicitly notes her network's selection bias and wants to hear from people who are happy using the "three pillars" model, which she knows few of personally.

The post includes a detailed list of open questions covering observability vendor migrations, cost control in traditional three-pillar architectures (Datadog, Grafana, Chronosphere), telemetry pipelines, observability team structure and size, build vs buy decisions, reasons for not adopting OpenTelemetry, reducing instrumentation cognitive load, tool consolidation vs proliferation, and open-source observability strategies ([source](https://charity.wtf/p/got-opinions-on-observability-i-could-use-your-help-once-more-with-feeling)). She invites readers to answer any subset of questions and to raise topics she missed.

Practical logistics are included: submissions go to her first name at honeycomb dot io with subject "Observability questions," contributors may be named but their employers will not be mentioned, and responses must arrive within a week. She closes with a grammar musing about "opinions on" versus "opinions about" observability, tying the post title to a Buffy the Vampire Slayer musical reference ([source](https://charity.wtf/p/got-opinions-on-observability-i-could-use-your-help-once-more-with-feeling)).

- Vendor engineering is a high-leverage, underappreciated engineering skill that affects every engineer and customer.
- The author explicitly seeks perspectives from teams happily using the three pillars model, acknowledging her own selection bias.
- Open questions span migration, cost cutting, telemetry pipelines, OTel adoption, instrumentation, build vs buy, and tool consolidation.
- Contributors can be named in the book, but their employers will not be disclosed; deadline is one week from posting.
- The post is a call for practitioner input, not a technical guide, due to an upcoming second edition deadline.
