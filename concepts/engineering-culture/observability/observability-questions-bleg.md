# Got opinions on observability? I could use your help (once more, with feeling)

Charity Majors, author of an upcoming second edition of her observability book, follows up on a previous plea for help by asking the community for insights on observability and software vendor engineering. She explains that vendor engineering is a subtle but high-leverage skill for senior engineers, allowing them to make decisions that affect many engineers and customers. The article emphasizes that writing about buying software has become one of the most exciting parts of her new edition.

Majors then lists a series of open questions for readers, covering topics such as migrating between observability vendors, controlling costs, managing instrumentation, adopting OpenTelemetry, build vs. buy decisions, telemetry pipelines, and tool consolidation. She also asks about organizational structures, including how observability teams are sized and where they report. She invites direct email responses and asks for permission to credit contributors without naming their employers.

- Vendor engineering is a powerful practical application of deep subject matter expertise and a high-leverage activity for staff+ engineers.
- Majors is seeking community input to strengthen weak or uninformed areas of her book, especially around traditional 'three pillars' observability architectures.
- Questions span observability vendor migration, cost management, telemetry pipelines, instrumentation, OpenTelemetry adoption, and consolidation vs. proliferation.
- She asks about observability team structures and ownership in large enterprises, including build vs. buy decisions.
- Contributors are credited by name but not by employer if they wish.
