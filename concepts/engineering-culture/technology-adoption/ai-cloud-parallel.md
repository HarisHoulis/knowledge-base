# 2025 was for AI what 2010 was for cloud

Charity Majors draws a direct parallel between the state of AI in 2025 and the state of cloud computing around 2010, arguing that AI has shifted from an experimental, satellite technology to a foundational part of mainstream developer tooling. She reflects on her experience at Linden Lab in 2006, where cloud services like EC2 and S3 were initially used for experimental projects, only to quickly become the center of gravity by 2010. She sees the same trajectory happening with AI today, moving from side projects and conference filler to core infrastructure (Majors, 2025).

Majors addresses skepticism about AI, acknowledging the hype bubble but insisting that froth does not negate underlying value. She references a keynote she and Fred Hebert delivered at SREcon Americas, titled "AIOps: Prove It! An Open Letter to Vendors Selling AI for SREs," where they urged fellow SREs to engage seriously with AI rather than dismiss it as all hype. She emphasizes that realism and risk-awareness are crucial, but critiques must be grounded in expertise to be heard. This marks a notable evolution in her own position over just eight months (Majors, 2025).

- AI in 2025 has transitioned from experimental to mainstream foundational technology, analogous to cloud in 2010.
- Hype bubbles exist for every major innovation, but they do not disprove the value of the underlying technology.
- SREs and engineers should engage with AI constructively, grounding critiques in expertise rather than dismissing it outright.
- Practical adoption of cloud started with small experimental projects, a pattern now repeating with AI in developer tools.
