# Bliki: Mythical Man Month

Martin Fowler reflects on Fred Brooks's classic book "The Mythical Man-Month", which remains influential despite being published in 1975. He notes that while some parts feel outdated as of 2026, many lessons still apply to modern software development. A central idea is Brooks's law: "Adding manpower to a late software project makes it later." This occurs because communication paths between team members grow exponentially as the team size increases, and without careful design of these paths, work quickly falls apart (Fowler).

Fowler emphasizes conceptual integrity as the most important consideration in system design. He quotes Brooks: "It is better to have a system omit certain anomalous features and improvements, but to reflect one set of design ideas, than to have one that contains many good but independent and uncoordinated ideas." He connects conceptual integrity with simplicity and straightforwardness—the ease of composing elements—and notes that this pursuit has strongly influenced his own career (Fowler).

- Brooks's law: adding manpower to a late software project makes it later because communication overhead grows exponentially.
- Conceptual integrity is more valuable than packing in many independent, uncoordinated features.
- Conceptual integrity is achieved through simplicity and straightforwardness.
- The anniversary edition also includes Brooks's influential 1986 essay 'No Silver Bullet'.
