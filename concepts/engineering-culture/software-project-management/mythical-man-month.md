# Bliki: Mythical Man Month

Martin Fowler reflects on Fred Brooks's classic 1975 book 'The Mythical Man-Month', which remains relevant despite some outdated aspects. The central principle is Brooks's law: adding manpower to a late software project makes it later, due to the exponential growth of communication paths as team size increases. Fowler emphasizes conceptual integrity as the book's most enduring lesson: a system should reflect one set of design ideas, even if it omits some features, rather than combine many independent and uncoordinated ideas. He links conceptual integrity to simplicity and straightforwardness, and notes that the anniversary edition includes Brooks's influential 1986 essay 'No Silver Bullet'.

- Brooks's law: adding people to a late project slows it down due to communication overhead.
- Conceptual integrity is the most important consideration in system design.
- It is better to omit features than to have a system with many uncoordinated ideas.
- Conceptual integrity involves both simplicity and straightforwardness.
- The anniversary edition also contains the essay 'No Silver Bullet'.
