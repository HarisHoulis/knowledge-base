# Thoughts on Motivation and My 40-Year Career

Charity Majors reflects on her unlikely path from a fundamentalist, homeschooled childhood in Idaho to a career in tech and founding Honeycomb. She recounts leaving home at 15, forging her own transcript to get into university, and working multiple minimum-wage jobs to afford school before discovering computers and the internet. Majors emphasizes that work has been a source of liberation and meaning, contrasting it with the subsistence farm labor of her youth, and notes that she is driven less by happiness than by doing work that feels worth doing (Majors, 2025).

Majors also discusses her struggle with faith and eventual rejection of religion, which she says unlocked her career by freeing her from compartmentalization. She describes how starting a company shifted her perspective, teaching her that "creation is harder than critique" and that running a business requires making peace with multiple narratives while honoring one's ideals. She finds motivation in solving systems problems and building enduring value, and she pushes back against the trend of disdaining hard work, while acknowledging that not everyone wants or needs work to be a vehicle for personal growth (Majors, 2025).

- Work was a source of liberation and meaning for Majors, enabling her to escape poverty and build a career, in contrast to subsistence farm labor.
- Leaving fundamentalism and becoming an atheist was a turning point that allowed her to fully engage with her career and personal values.
- Starting Honeycomb was a near-whimsical decision that led to profound growth, teaching her that creation is harder than critique.
- She values work as a place for collaboration across ideological differences and finds joy in solving systems problems and building companies.
- Majors rejects the notion that work must be depersonalizing, but also respects those who work for a paycheck rather than personal fulfillment.
