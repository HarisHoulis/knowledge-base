# Thoughts on Motivation and My 40-Year Career

Charity Majors reflects on her unconventional path from a fundamentalist homeschooling upbringing in rural Idaho to a successful tech career. She left home at 15, fleeing poverty and a life that felt too small, and found liberation through work. Her motivation, she explains, is not just about advancing the industry—though that matters—but about living a meaningful life, acting with integrity, and building things of enduring value. She contrasts her perspective with Will Larson's "advancing the industry" framing, emphasizing that her drive is more personal and existential.

Majors describes how her career only took off after she resolved an internal crisis, reconciling her childhood beliefs with her adult understanding. Work became a source of growth, community, and joy, contrary to the current cultural trend of viewing jobs as mere exploitation. She acknowledges that not everyone wants work to be a vehicle for personal growth, but she finds deep satisfaction in solving problems, building companies, and creating value. Starting Honeycomb forced her to confront the ethical and practical complexities of leadership, where "creation is harder than critique." She values the challenge of trying to win in the marketplace while honoring her ideals, and she sees work as one of the last places where people with different worldviews can collaborate toward common goals.

- Work can be a source of liberation and meaning, especially for those escaping restrictive or impoverished backgrounds.
- True motivation often involves integrity and building enduring value, not just industry advancement or personal success.
- Overcoming personal crisis and aligning one's actions with one's values is crucial for career fulfillment.
- Running a company is an exercise in balancing ethics with the imperative to succeed; reality resists easy answers.
- Creation is harder than critique, and embracing that challenge is a key part of meaningful work.
