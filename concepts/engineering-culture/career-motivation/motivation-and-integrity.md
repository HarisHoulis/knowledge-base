# Thoughts on Motivation and My 40-Year Career

In this personal essay, Charity Majors reflects on her 40-year career, tracing her journey from a fundamentalist homeschooled upbringing in Idaho to becoming a tech leader and co-founder of Honeycomb. She describes leaving home at 15, forging her own path through university and minimum-wage jobs, and eventually finding computers and the internet as a means of escape from poverty (Majors, 2025). Her early life instilled a desire for autonomy and impact, which she later channeled into engineering and entrepreneurship.

- Personal background and escape from poverty shaped her drive for meaningful work.
- Motivation is less about advancing the industry and more about living with integrity and building enduring value.
- Work can be a source of liberation and personal growth, contrary to current anti-work sentiments.
- Creation is harder than critique; running a company requires balancing ethical ideals with practical market demands.
- Finding joy in work came gradually, but the sense of doing something worth doing has been a constant.
