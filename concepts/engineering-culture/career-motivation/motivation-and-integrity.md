# Thoughts on Motivation and My 40-Year Career

Majors argues that her core motivation is living a meaningful life, acting with integrity, and building things of enduring value—contrasting with Will Larson's 'advancing the industry' framing. She emphasizes that work has been a source of liberation and growth, even as she acknowledges the modern backlash against over-identifying with one's job. She loves the problem-solving nature of engineering and, despite initial ambivalence about management, found deep satisfaction in building Honeycomb and facing the complex reality of running a company. 'Creation is harder than critique,' she quotes, and running a startup forces a reconciliation of ideals with practical business demands (Majors, 2025).

- Work has been a source of liberation and meaning for Majors, shaped by her escape from poverty and fundamentalism.
- Her motivation centers on integrity and enduring value, not just advancing the industry.
- Running a company forces ethical ideals to collide with marketplace realities, which she finds motivating.
- Creation is harder than critique, and that challenge is part of the joy of building.
- She acknowledges that not everyone wants work to be a vehicle for growth, and that is valid.
