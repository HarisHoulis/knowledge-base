# Fragments: June 16

In his June 16 fragments, Martin Fowler reflects on how LLMs are reshaping software engineering. Dave Thomas shares that LLMs have made programming more enjoyable by removing drudgery, speeding up feedback loops, and enabling exploration of new technologies, countering the fear that they would ruin the craft. Fowler also recounts highlights from DDD Europe, where Eric Evans discussed his LLM experiments and Domain-Driven Design remains crucial, and Chelsea Troy offered practical advice on managing LLM context windows by consciously switching between four conversation registers while keeping context fresh.

- LLMs can make programming more enjoyable by reducing drudgery and accelerating feedback loops (Dave Thomas).
- Domain-Driven Design remains relevant and may become even more important in LLM-driven development.
- Chelsea Troy emphasizes managing LLM context windows by using distinct conversational registers and starting fresh when changing modes.
- Charity Majors warns of a dangerous divide between AI enthusiasts and skeptics, urging a balanced approach that treats AI as an engineering problem and shares both wins and costs.
- Simon Willison sees AI pricing hikes as evidence of product-market fit for coding agents, marking a new inflection point for AI's commercial impact.
