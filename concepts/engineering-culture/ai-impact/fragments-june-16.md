# Fragments: June 16

Martin Fowler shares a collection of links and observations about the current state of software engineering, focusing on the impact of LLMs. He highlights Dave Thomas's essay on how LLMs have made programming more enjoyable by removing drudgery and speeding up feedback loops, contrasting with earlier fears that they would kill creativity [1]. Fowler also recounts highlights from the DDD Europe conference, including a talk by Chelsea Troy on managing LLM context windows; Troy emphasized being aware of different 'registers' of conversation with an LLM and starting a new session when switching registers to maintain context health.

Charity Majors warns of a growing divide between AI enthusiasts and skeptics, noting that enthusiasts see real productivity leaps while skeptics point to rapid shipping that degrades reliability and institutional knowledge. She argues both sides have merit and calls for honest reporting of costs, treating AI as an engineering problem, and recognizing that engineering discipline becomes more important as AI amplifies current practices [2]. Simon Willison observes that Anthropic and OpenAI have raised enterprise pricing, likely because they have found product-market fit with coding agents like Claude Code and Codex; he suspects April 2026 marked an inflection point where revenue implications became clear [3].

Mike Masnick reflects on the internet's failed promise of decentralization, explaining how new middlemen replaced old gatekeepers but then built advertising machines around attention, leading to enshittification and digital despotism. He argues that decentralization, despite ergonomic issues, is essential for cognitive liberty, and that user control over data and ease of exit promote competition and prevent exploitation [4]. Fowler connects these historical lessons to the AI-enabled future, urging an environment that encourages decentralization and competition.

- LLMs can make programming more fun by removing drudgery and accelerating feedback loops, as reported by Dave Thomas and Fowler's peers.
- When working with LLMs, be conscious of conversational 'registers'; switching registers should prompt a fresh context window.
- AI enthusiasts and skeptics are both correct in different ways; avoid maximalist stances and instead focus on engineering discipline and honest cost-benefit analysis.
- Anthropic and OpenAI's enterprise price increases signal product-market fit for coding agents, marking a revenue inflection in April 2026.
- Decentralization and ease of exit are key to avoiding enshittification and digital despotism, a lesson that should guide AI adoption.
