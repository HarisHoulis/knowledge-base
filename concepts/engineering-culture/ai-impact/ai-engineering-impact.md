# Fragments: June 16

Charity Majors highlights a growing divide between AI enthusiasts and skeptics, arguing both sides are right: enthusiasts see real capability jumps, while skeptics fear degraded reliability and lost institutional knowledge. She recommends telling the whole story including costs, treating AI integration as an engineering problem, and grounding arguments in actual engagement—engineers who shape AI use will gain credibility by understanding tradeoffs and consequences. Simon Willison notes that Anthropic and OpenAI have raised enterprise pricing, signaling product-market fit for coding agents, with April 2026 as a new inflection point. Finally, Mike Masnick reflects on the internet's failed promise of decentralization, explaining how centralized platforms became enablers that monetize attention, leading to enshittification and lock-in, and argues that user control over data and easy exit are essential to fostering competition and cognitive liberty.

- LLMs make programming more enjoyable by eliminating drudgery and shortening feedback loops (Dave Thomas).
- Managing LLM context windows requires awareness of conversation registers; change registers means starting a new session (Chelsea Troy).
- AI enthusiasts and skeptics both have valid existential concerns; engineering discipline is increasingly important (Charity Majors).
- Enterprise pricing hikes by Anthropic and OpenAI indicate product-market fit for AI coding agents (Simon Willison).
- Centralized internet platforms enable enshittification; user data control and ease of exit promote competition (Mike Masnick).
