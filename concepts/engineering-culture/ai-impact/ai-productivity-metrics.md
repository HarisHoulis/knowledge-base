# Fragments: June 2 – AI Metrics, Automation, and Developer Productivity

Fowler also covers Stephen O'Grady's analysis of open vs. closed models, noting that closed models lead in capability but open models are catching up faster over time (Fowler, 2026). He warns about hallucinated citations in AI-generated reports, citing GPTZero's investigation of Ernst & Young Canada, and discusses Mozilla's success in using AI to fix an unprecedented number of security bugs. He introduces concepts like 'generative debt' and Andy Osmani's analogy of the developer as the GIL for AI agents, while Jamie Hurst shares concerns that AI-driven productivity gains have been captured by output volume, reducing time for thinking and mentoring (Fowler, 2026).

- Common AI productivity metrics are flawed; subjective self-reports may be the best available evidence.
- Historical automation did not eliminate professions, making AI job impact forecasts unreliable.
- Open AI models are closing capability gaps with closed models at accelerating speed.
- Hallucinated citations in AI reports can poison the internet's knowledge pool.
- AI can multiply both good and bad code; human attention is a serial bottleneck for AI agent workflows.
- AI-driven productivity gains often come at the cost of strategic thinking and mentoring.
