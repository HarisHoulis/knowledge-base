# Prototyping as Leadership: How a CTO Ships with AI Agents

In a talk for AI Engineer, Hursh Agrawal, CTO of The Browser Company, argues that AI agents have turned the manager schedule into viable building time. Despite fifteen recurring meetings a week, seven direct reports, and a toddler at home, he ships between two and ten pull requests. This was not possible a year ago, and Agrawal frames building itself as a leadership responsibility rather than a hobby squeezed around the edges of the job.

- AI agents let a CTO ship 2–10 pull requests per week despite a heavy meeting load, making prototyping part of leadership.
- Hands-on building is necessary for calibrating judgment as frontier models change every few months.
- Showing engineers a working prototype beats arguing about model capability, especially because leaders hold more business context.
- The core workflow is an overnight loop: a coworker agent assembles context into a prompt, a coding agent works at 5pm, and the morning is spent reviewing.
- Reusable for features, prompt hill-climbing against eval sets, and training small classifiers, but requires trustworthy CI, feature flags, and a prototype branch.
- Avoid adding reviewers to code you have not read.
