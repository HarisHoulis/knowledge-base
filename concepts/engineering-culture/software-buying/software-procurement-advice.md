# Are you an experienced software buyer? I could use some help.

Charity Majors announces that she and her co-authors are working on the second edition of "Observability Engineering" and have observed a significant shift since the first edition: observability engineering teams are now widespread, yet the phrase didn't even appear in the original book. To address this, they are adding a substantial new section on "Observability Governance" covering topics like team staffing, org placement, tool buying, integration, cost management, and making business cases to executives.

Majors admits she has never personally bought software at scale nor worked in a large enterprise tech company, so she feels unqualified to advise on key procurement questions. She specifically asks for practical advice from experienced engineers and VPs on how to shop for options, identify when to change vendors, structure proof-of-concept evaluations, identify stakeholders and their concerns, and drive consensus when millions of dollars are at stake.

The author solicits advice via email, guaranteeing anonymity and no vendor mentions. She emphasizes that this is an O'Reilly book project, not a Honeycomb marketing effort, and requests guidance to share with readers of the upcoming second edition.

- Observability engineering teams have become common, warranting a new governance section in the book's second edition.
- Majors openly states her lack of hands-on experience in large-scale software buying and asks for help from practitioners.
- Specific topics requested include vendor evaluation, POC design, stakeholder management, and consensus-building for major purchases.
- Advice will be used for the O'Reilly book, not for Honeycomb promotion, and will keep vendors anonymous.
- Contact details are provided for readers to submit their experiences.
