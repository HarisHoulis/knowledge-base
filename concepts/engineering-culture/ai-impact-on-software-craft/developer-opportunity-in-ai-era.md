# The craft "dying" is your OPPORTUNITY

Kent C. Dodds addresses concerns that AI is 'destroying' the craft of software development by making developers lazy. He acknowledges that some developers are abdicating their responsibilities, but argues this is not entirely new—people have always deferred judgment to libraries or big companies. The key difference is that AI agents are now non-deterministic collaborators, yet humans are also non-deterministic, so this shouldn't be the primary worry.

Dodds sees AI as an opportunity for developers to spend more time on critical decisions like system design, product vision, and building the 'software factory' and primitives that make agents more effective. He cites his own experience of working on a half-million-line codebase as a solo developer, made possible by AI assistance. The real differentiator will be developers who intentionally design systems with agents in hand, taking ownership and accountability for outcomes rather than blindly handing off decision-making.

Ultimately, the human developer's value lies in responsibility and judgment—qualities that were always essential but are now more important than ever as agents take over routine tasks. Those who rise to this challenge will stand out.

- Concerns about AI making developers lazy are valid but incomplete; abdicating judgment is a pre-existing human tendency.
- Agents will handle more routine work, freeing developers to focus on system design, product decisions, and building effective primitives.
- AI enables solo developers to tackle projects of unprecedented scale, like a 500k-line codebase.
- Critical thinking and intentional design with agents are key differentiators in the evolving industry.
- Ownership, accountability, and responsibility are the core contributions of human developers in an AI-assisted world.
