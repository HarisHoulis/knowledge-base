# The Pragmatic Engineer AMA

In this AMA episode, Gergely Orosz shares personal stories behind The Pragmatic Engineer, including how the COVID-19 pandemic and Uber layoffs led him to leave his engineering manager role and pursue writing full-time (Orosz, 2026). He explains that he initially planned to start a VC-funded startup but instead discovered a passion for writing, eventually publishing several books. He also reveals that he chose not to publish a critical exposé on the Dutch neobank Bunq after an engineer credited the company for giving him a visa and a chance to break into European tech, leading Orosz to adopt a new editorial policy focused on what works inside companies rather than what seems broken.

Orosz discusses his only investigative piece, on the startup Pollen, which began after its CEO dismissed him as a “nobody.” His reporting uncovered unpaid salaries, silently cancelled health insurance, and a CTO-triggered $3.2M double charge to customers with no postmortem (Orosz, 2026). He also shares opinions on engineering culture: LeetCode-style interviews persist because they self-select for candidates who tolerate corporate nonsense, and MCP became an industry standard partly because Anthropic was not yet a dominant AI lab at launch. He warns that AI can cause skill atrophy, noting he uses zero AI in his writing to preserve his craft, while accepting that his hand-coding skills will degrade as he uses AI for coding.

- The Pragmatic Engineer originated from Orosz leaving Uber after COVID-19 layoffs and pivoting from a planned startup to writing.
- A reader’s personal story about Bunq changed Orosz’s editorial approach: he now highlights what works in companies instead of publishing negative exposés.
- His sole investigative piece on Pollen uncovered unpaid salaries, cancelled health insurance, and a $3.2M double charge with no postmortem.
- Orosz argues LeetCode interviews persist because they select for tolerance of meaningless work, and that MCP’s adoption was aided by Anthropic not being a market leader at the time.
- AI use involves a tradeoff: Orosz avoids AI in writing to prevent skill atrophy, but accepts degradation in hand-coding skills.
