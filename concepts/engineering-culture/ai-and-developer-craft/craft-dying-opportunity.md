# The craft 'dying' is your OPPORTUNITY

Kent C. Dodds addresses the widespread concern that AI is destroying the craft of software development by making developers lazy. While acknowledging that some developers are abdicating their responsibilities, he argues that this is not a new phenomenon caused by AI; developers have long deferred judgment to popular libraries or other authorities. The real shift is that AI agents are becoming more autonomous, and the challenge is to maintain confidence in our software systems while allowing agents to work independently without compromising user experience or product quality (Kent C. Dodds, 2026).

Dodds reframes the situation as an opportunity rather than a threat. As AI handles more of the routine implementation details, developers can focus on higher-level product engineering, system design, and critical decision-making. He shares his personal experience of building a half-million-line codebase as a solo developer with AI assistance, something previously unimaginable. The key differentiator for developers will be those who intentionally design systems with agents in mind and take ownership of the overall architecture and user value, rather than simply handing off all judgment to AI (Kent C. Dodds, 2026).

- AI is not deterministic like a compiler, but neither are humans; the perceived 'laziness' is not new, just easier to execute now.
- The real opportunity is to shift from micromanaging implementation details to designing systems that enable autonomous agents to build good products.
- Developers who retain critical thinking and ownership of system design will stand out, while others who abdicate judgment to AI will become less relevant.
- AI enables solo developers to take on projects of unprecedented scale, as demonstrated by Dodds's own half-million-line codebase.
- The core value of a human developer is ownership, accountability, and responsibility—qualities that become even more important in an AI-assisted workflow.
