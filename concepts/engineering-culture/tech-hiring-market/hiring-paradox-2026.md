# Tech jobs market in 2026, part 3: hiring managers & job seekers

The tech job market in 2026 is described as 'weird' and paradoxical: hiring managers struggle to find senior talent while experienced engineers report being ghosted by employers. This Catch-22 is fueled by a flood of AI-generated resumes and fake candidates, which has made inbound applications nearly useless. As a result, many companies now rely heavily on personal networks and referrals; job seekers confirm that cold applications rarely lead to interviews, while referrals are a lifeline (Orosz, 2026).

The market is sharply bifurcated. AI engineers, ML specialists, and forward-deployed engineers are in high demand and can command strong offers, especially in the US, where a talent shortage is a common complaint. In contrast, generalist engineers and senior engineering leaders face a much tougher market, with higher hiring bars, lower compensation, and more competition. Regional differences also emerge: ghosting and fake applicants are bigger issues in the UK, EU, and elsewhere, while US-based candidates with AI experience often experience the 'best market ever' (Orosz, 2026).

- Hiring managers and job seekers are stuck in a Catch-22: managers say senior talent is unavailable, while experienced engineers get no replies to applications.
- Inbound applications are overwhelmed by AI-generated resumes and fake candidates, leading many companies to ignore them and hire through networks and referrals.
- The market is bifurcated: AI engineering, ML, and forward-deployed engineer roles are hot, while generalist and senior-level roles are much harder to land.
- AI has eroded trust in resumes and cover letters; candidates often inflate AI-related skills, and fake interviewees (e.g., North Korean scammers) are increasingly common.
- Regional differences: the US has a talent shortage for AI skills, while the UK/EU see more ghosting, fake applicants, and an extinction of remote roles.
