# From Chrome DevTools to AI Engineering, with Addy Osmani

In this episode of The Pragmatic Engineer, Gergely Orosz interviews Addy Osmani, who spent over 14 years at Google working on Chrome, DevTools, Core Web Vitals, and AI developer experience. Osmani shares his journey from building a web browser at age 16 to becoming a Director of Engineering, including how publishing free educational materials on frontend and JavaScript development helped him land a job at Google. The conversation covers Google's engineering culture, the evolution of Chrome DevTools, and the challenges of memory debugging in web development (Pragmatic Engineer, 2026).

- Cognitive surrender is the erosion of comprehension when relying heavily on AI; engineers should understand every major decision the LLM makes.
- Aim for mutual amplification: have AI agents log their decisions and key learnings so you can review and internalize them.
- Software engineers remain essential because an AI model cannot be accountable; designated engineers own parts of the codebase and shape contributions.
- The role of the engineer is unbundling: develop product sense, go-to-market awareness, and other business skills to stay successful.
- Google's culture has shifted, with VPs and SVPs coding on weekends—a sign that AI tools are making coding more accessible and changing engineering leadership.
