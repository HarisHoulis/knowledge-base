# From Chrome DevTools to AI Engineering, with Addy Osmani

In this episode, Gergely Orosz interviews Addy Osmani, a long-time Google engineer and director known for his work on Chrome DevTools and Core Web Vitals. Osmani recounts building a web browser at age 16, publishing free educational resources that caught Google's attention, and eventually joining the Chrome team to build DevTools as a way to meet web developers in the browser. He reflects on how the tool evolved to support source maps, mobile emulation, and service workers as web development grew more complex (Orosz, 2026).

The conversation then shifts to engineering culture at Google. Osmani notes that the biggest change when becoming a director was being on the hook for a top company goal and reporting on it weekly. He observes that in the past two years, VPs and SVPs at Google have started coding on weekends, partly because AI tools make coding easier. He also highlights that memory management debugging remains a difficult, underdeveloped area despite improvements in runtime performance tooling (Orosz, 2026).

Osmani shares his perspective on AI-assisted development, warning about 'cognitive surrender'—the erosion of a developer's comprehension and memory of the codebase. He recommends understanding every major decision an LLM makes, and aiming for 'mutual amplification': using AI agents to improve both the task and the engineer's own learning. He believes software engineers will remain essential because an AI model cannot be accountable; designated engineers own parts of codebases and remain responsible for approving changes (Orosz, 2026).

Finally, Osmani gives career advice: engineering roles are being unbundled, so engineers should develop product sense, go-to-market awareness, and other business skills. He is bullish on software engineering's future, arguing that as tools make software easier to create, the total addressable market grows. His key advice: 'Don't be just an engineer' (Orosz, 2026).

- Addy Osmani built a web browser at 16 and later joined Google after sharing free educational resources on frontend development.
- Chrome DevTools evolved from a simple debugging tool into a comprehensive IDE-like suite as web complexity grew.
- The biggest shift when becoming a director at Google was being held accountable for a top company goal on a weekly basis.
- AI-assisted development risks 'cognitive surrender': engineers must understand key LLM decisions and pursue 'mutual amplification' to keep learning.
- Engineers should broaden beyond coding—develop product, go-to-market, and other business skills: 'Don't be just an engineer.'
