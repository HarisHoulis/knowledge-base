# Fragments: June 16

Martin Fowler's June 2026 fragments collect observations on how LLMs are changing programming. Dave Thomas, co-author of The Pragmatic Programmer, describes how LLMs restored his love of programming by removing drudgery, speeding feedback loops, reviving abandoned projects, and enabling exploration of new technologies (Thomas, articles.pragdave.me). Fowler also recounts DDD Europe 2026, where Eric Evans discussed his LLM experiments and Chelsea Troy presented a framework for managing LLM context windows; she distinguishes four conversational registers and advises starting a fresh context whenever you change registers (Fowler, martinfowler.com/fragments/2026-06-16.html).

- LLMs make programming more enjoyable by removing drudgery and accelerating feedback loops.
- Conversations with LLMs have distinct registers; switching registers requires a fresh context to keep the context window healthy.
- A crevasse is forming between AI enthusiasts and skeptics: enthusiasts see real capability leaps, while skeptics see degradation of trust and reliability; both are existential threats.
- AI adoption must be treated as an engineering problem, with engineering discipline becoming more important.
- Anthropic and OpenAI's enterprise price increases signal product-market fit for coding agents, marking April 2026 as a potential new inflection point.
- Decentralization and easy exit between services are key to preventing enshittification and protecting cognitive liberty.
