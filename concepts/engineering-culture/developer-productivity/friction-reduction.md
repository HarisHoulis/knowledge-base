# Developer Productivity at a Developer Productivity Startup

In this GOTO 2025 talk, R.J. Huijsman shares how his eight-person startup, Reboot, achieves high output despite being distributed across time zones from Warsaw to Hawaii. He argues that developer productivity hinges on reducing friction and managing non-determinism, both in coding and in the human interaction that precedes it. Friction is defined as anything that slows you down from reaching your next result, with the biggest source being waiting for other humans. To combat this, Reboot deliberately prioritizes throughput over latency: they maintain many parallel work streams and switch between tasks whenever blocked by a colleague's unavailability, rather than idling. Additionally, they schedule a daily two-hour overlap window where everyone is online to resolve questions and unblock each other synchronously, while other activities like code reviews remain asynchronous.

- Developer productivity is framed around reducing friction (anything slowing you down) and managing non-determinism.
- The biggest friction in human collaboration is waiting for others, especially across time zones.
- A throughput-focused approach uses parallel work streams: when blocked on one task, switch to another unblocked task.
- Daily overlap hours provide a synchronous window for unblocking, while async processes like code reviews continue outside that window.
