# Why is Meta destroying its engineering organization?

Gergely Orosz argues that Meta's historically high-performing engineering culture, which empowered engineers for two decades, is being rapidly dismantled in 2026 due to an aggressive AI pivot. The article contrasts Meta's early 'move fast and break things' ethos with the later 'move fast with stable infra' approach, highlighting how engineers previously operated with autonomy, high impact, and minimal process. However, starting in late April 2026, Meta engineers began experiencing forced reassignments, keystroke tracking, and a clear shift from seeing engineering as a profit center to a cost center (Orosz, 2026).

The core issues include mandatory enrollment in a system that tracks every keystroke and mouse click for AI training data, with no initial opt-out, and the forceful reassignment of 30-50% of engineers from core product and infrastructure teams to the new Agent Data Optimisation (ADO) org for data labeling and RLHF tasks. Those reassigned include experienced engineers working on infrastructure and security, many of whom feel treated like 'tributes' in a Hunger Games scenario. The work, while sometimes technically involved, is repetitive and widely seen as menial, with career implications (Orosz, 2026).

The article ties this to Mark Zuckerberg's determination not to miss the AI platform opportunity, after missing mobile. Meta acquired a 49% stake in Scale AI and brought in its CEO, Alexandr Wang, to reboot AI efforts. Yet the strategy relies on engineering labor for data annotation, and Zuckerberg's investment appears to prioritize AI capability over preserving engineering culture. The piece also notes that the keystroke tracking was scaled back after employee backlash, and that the ADO org now has more people than OpenAI and Anthropic, indicating a major internal shift that may hurt product quality and morale (Orosz, 2026).

- Meta's engineering culture historically offered high autonomy, low process, and focus on impact, making engineers feel valued as a profit center.
- Starting in 2026, engineers are being forcefully reassigned to AI data labeling and RLHF work, with 30-50% of core team members moved to the new ADO org.
- Meta rolled out keystroke and mouse click tracking for AI training data with no opt-out, later scaling back after employee pushback.
- Infrastructure and security teams were hit hardest by reassignments, raising concerns about service reliability and morale.
- The changes are driven by Zuckerberg's AI spending, including a $14.8B investment in Scale AI, but may harm Meta's long-term engineering strength.
