# Software Engineering at Optiver: A Deep Dive into Prop Trading

Optiver faces unique challenges such as sub-nanosecond trading systems and a cautionary tale of Knight Capital's $440M bug. The company is increasingly integrating AI models, both in trading strategies and internal tooling. The business is also attractive to AI labs due to its infrastructure and low-latency custom hardware expertise. The article provides a comprehensive look at the engineering organization, tech stack, physical infrastructure, and AI adoption at a modern proprietary trading firm.

- No external customers and latency are the defining drivers of engineering decisions at prop shops like Optiver.
- The industry has shifted from automated trading to quantitative, data-driven trading; ML and AI models are now key differentiators.
- Optiver is consolidating fragmented regional systems into global platforms, rebuilding CI and platform engineering for scale.
- A single bug can be catastrophic, so engineering culture balances extreme speed with rigorous caution and risk management.
- Custom hardware, FPGAs, and co-located infrastructure are central to achieving competitive latency advantages.
