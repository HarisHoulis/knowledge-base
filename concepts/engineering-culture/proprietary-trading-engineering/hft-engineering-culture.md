# Software engineering at a proprietary trading company: Optiver

Engineering practices emphasize risk management, testing, and knowledge sharing, while AI is being integrated into coding workflows and trading strategies. The 'build and own' culture gives engineers responsibility over the entire stack, and machine learning is increasingly important alongside mathematical models like Black-Scholes for options pricing. The article also notes that AI labs like Anthropic and OpenAI recruit from prop shops because of their expertise in custom high-performance hardware and on-prem infrastructure. (Orosz, 2026)

- Optiver has no external customers; the business itself is the customer, creating unique incentives focused on internal motivation and caution to avoid market-disrupting bugs.
- Latency is the central engineering concern, driving investment in custom hardware, FPGAs, and even radio links, though it is now considered a baseline rather than a differentiator.
- The competitive edge has shifted from raw speed to quantitative models and AI, with machine learning and data-driven decision-making becoming core to trading strategies.
- Optiver is reorganizing from regional systems to global platform engineering, aiming to standardize CI and deployment pipelines across all offices.
- Engineering culture values 'build and own' ownership, risk awareness, and knowledge sharing, with a strong emphasis on testing and monitoring to prevent catastrophic failures.
