# Let's Open Source the World — Brandon Thomas, ArtCraft

Brandon Thomas, an engineer with 15 years of experience (eight at Square on critical infrastructure, then startups), describes how a project cloning Adobe Photoshop and other creative apps went viral and drew venture interest. He frames this as part of a larger mission: making the whole world open source and using AI as a chance to 'take back the web' from platforms that eroded user ownership.

He traces the early web (90s and early 2000s) as an open ecosystem: IRC servers you could run yourself, chat clients that tolerated third-party tools, and sites like Reddit, Flickr, and Twitter offering APIs that enabled mashups. Users bought MP3s and felt ownership; MySpace let people edit HTML and CSS directly; Wikipedia emerged from this ethos. Most early users were technical experts, and creativity flourished in Flash animations and PHPBB communities.

With Web 2.0, broadband, and smartphones, non-technical users arrived and the rough edges were smoothed away. Protocols were replaced by platforms competing to capture users, APIs were withdrawn, and large providers took control of distribution. Prices rose as startups gained market power—streaming fragmented into something like cable TV—and companies at the limits of growth began inventing 'crazy traps.' Thomas positions open source, powered by AI, as the path back to user ownership.

- The early web was open by default: self-hosted IRC, permissive third-party clients, and public APIs enabling mashups.
- Web 2.0 and smartphones brought non-technical users, and platforms smoothed away technical openness in exchange for reach.
- Protocols gave way to platforms; APIs were withdrawn and distribution consolidated under large providers.
- Thomas argues AI offers a chance to 'take back the web' by open-sourcing creative software, starting with his viral Photoshop clone.
