# 2025 was for AI what 2010 was for cloud

Charity Majors draws a parallel between the adoption of cloud computing in 2010 and AI in 2025, arguing that AI has transitioned from experimental, satellite technology to the mainstream foundational layer, particularly in developer tools. She recalls early cloud adoption at Linden Lab, where S3 was initially a desperate experimental project to offload asset storage, but by 2010 the center of gravity had shifted completely away from datacenters to the cloud (Majors).

Majors acknowledges the hype and bubble surrounding AI, but asserts that froth does not disprove value. She references a recent SRECon keynote with Fred Hebert titled "AIOps: Prove It!" where she and Hebert urged SREs to engage with AI seriously, noting that their realism and risk focus is needed more than ever, but their critique must be grounded in expertise to remain relevant (Majors). She admits her own position has shifted significantly in just eight months, from viewing AI as separable from tech mainstream to recognizing it as the new center of gravity for building technology (Majors).

- AI in 2025 has become mainstream foundational technology, much like cloud computing was in 2010, not merely experimental or satellite tech.
- Adoption cycles for major innovations always include hype bubbles, but froth doesn't negate underlying value.
- SREs and professional pessimists should engage with AI constructively, grounding their critique in expertise to be heard and remain relevant.
- Even skeptical engineers like Majors have shifted their position as AI's centrality has become undeniable.
- The transition to AI as core infrastructure is happening especially in developer tools.
