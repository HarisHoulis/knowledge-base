# Fragments: July 6

Martin Fowler reports on the second Future of Software Development Retreat, noting a marked shift from the hesitancy of the first retreat to a confident embrace of agentic engineering. Attendees are now shipping production software rather than discussing possibilities, and the debate about whether AI changes software engineering is over; the focus is on how to do it effectively. Patterns and practices are emerging, though there is still work to be done to establish what works best (Fowler, 2026).

A major theme is the rising cost of token usage. Companies like Citi and Amazon are throttling employees' AI use, with token bills soaring from $5 million to $15 million in a year at one company. Efforts to control costs include using less powerful models, cutting off frontier models, and even 'caveman' prompts. Fowler notes the emergence of harness engineering and ongoing concerns about architecture and design, with a key question being whether AI can handle spaghetti code or whether good modularity helps both humans and AI agents (Fowler, 2026).

Fowler also discusses Charity Majors' ethics of working with AI, which argues for engaging with the technology to drive change rather than renouncing it. He highlights the practical and ethical complexities of AI adoption, and he touches on other issues like fraudulent DMCA takedowns and the historical significance of the Declaration of Independence, but the core of the article is about the state of AI in software engineering and its economic and cultural implications (Fowler, 2026).

- Agentic engineering has moved from speculation to production, with retreat attendees showing confidence in its value.
- Token costs are becoming a critical concern, leading companies to throttle AI usage and seek cost-control measures.
- Good software design remains important because AI agents understand code similarly to humans, and it hedges against dependence on AI.
- Ethical engagement with AI is preferable to purity; using AI while working to mitigate harms is the recommended approach.
- Emerging practices like harness engineering and pattern libraries are still evolving, with more work needed to establish effectiveness.
