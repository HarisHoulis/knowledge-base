# Fragments: September 8

Martin Fowler's September 8 fragments open with Christian Catalini's argument that AI dramatically reduces the cost of generating things but not verifying them, which explains why AI products first appeared in chat, image generation, and code assistance: these outputs are relatively easy to inspect. Catalini posits that the new automation boundary is not routine versus non-routine work, but measurable versus non-measurable work. Fowler connects this to persistent challenges in measuring software productivity, warning that incomplete measurements can create 'counterfeit utility' and a 'Hollow Economy' of impressive activity hiding weakened capabilities and hidden technical debt (https://catalini.com/ideas/economics-of-ai/, https://martinfowler.com/bliki/CannotMeasureProductivity.html).

- AI's value is currently constrained by verification costs, not generation costs; measurable work is the new frontier.
- Incomplete productivity measurements can lead to counterfeit utility and a hollow economy.
- Readers are revolting against LLM-generated writing; 78% stop reading immediately and 71% blacklist the writer once detected.
- AI agents writing code require even stronger emphasis on objective verification (tests) rather than understanding every line.
- New AI systems like OpenAI's Astra may show better alignment but lower monitorability, complicating oversight.
