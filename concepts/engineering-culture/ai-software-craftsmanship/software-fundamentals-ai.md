# LIVE: Uncle Bob on Software Fundamentals in the Age of AI

In this live stream, host Matt Pocock interviews Robert C. Martin ('Uncle Bob'), a programmer who began writing code in 1964. Martin discusses his initial encounters with AI agents, specifically an early Grock agent that produced code but left behind a mess he had to clean up, as he recounts in the interview. He also reflects on how AI has taken him by surprise since late 2024, though he remains skeptical of its reliability.

Martin also explains the origin of his morning bathrobe rants, which began with a 6 a.m. critique of SQL. He argues that SQL's text-based nature is a security problem, contributing to injection attacks. Despite the arrival of AI, he suggests that the fundamentals of software craftsmanship remain relevant, as the need for clean code and careful oversight doesn't disappear with AI-generated code.

- Uncle Bob has been programming since 1964, giving him over half a century of experience.
- Early AI agents like Grock could write code but made a mess that required constant cleanup.
- Martin criticizes SQL's text-based format for database access, citing security risks like injection attacks.
- The bathrobe rant tradition started from his early morning critique of SQL.
- Software fundamentals remain important in the age of AI.
