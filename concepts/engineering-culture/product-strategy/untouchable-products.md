# Build What They Can't: Making Your Product Untouchable by Big Players

Kent C. Dodds argues that builders shouldn't panic when large platforms ship features similar to theirs, because big players can't replicate certain structural advantages. He cites the 2001 Watson app, which Apple's built-in Sherlock 3 killed the following year, versus Raycast, which survived by going cross-platform to Windows and iOS where Apple would never add Spotlight. The lesson: move to a "safe harbor" where big players will never go, making yourself untouchable by design.

Trust is a second moat. Despite built-in password managers from Google and Apple, 1Password raised $620 million at a $6.8 billion valuation in 2022 and keeps growing, because its only job is protecting your data—no ads, no incentive to betray trust. Google's manager lives inside the world's largest advertising company, and Apple's only works if you stay in its ecosystem; a company that needs to keep you "on the hook" shouldn't be fully trusted with your secrets.

Dodds applies this to his own product, Cody, in the AI era. AI labs (Anthropic, OpenAI, and others) want users inside their apps using their memory, tools, and integrations, because everyone who leaves is a user they may never get back. He frames the strategic question as identifying what none of them will ever create, and building that.

- Big platforms shipping your feature isn't fatal—Watson died to Sherlock, but Raycast survived by moving to platforms Apple would never support
- Find a "safe harbor" where incumbents structurally won't go to become untouchable by design
- Trust earned over years, with no ads or conflicting incentives, is a durable moat (1Password vs. built-in password managers)
- AI labs are incentivized to lock users into their ecosystems, so build what they structurally can't or won't
