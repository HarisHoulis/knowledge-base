# Canon 3X: Explore/Expand/Extract

Kent Beck's Canon 3X article frames the growth of any idea—product, company, or movement—as a logistic (S-shaped) curve, but argues the curve's smoothness is a dangerous illusion: the beginning, middle, and end require completely different approaches even though they look superficially similar. He describes the S curve as the interplay of a reinforcing growth loop that drives early growth and an inhibiting loop that later slows and caps it.

A successful idea moves through three phases. Explore finds a new growth loop (it must be new, or someone else would already be running it). Expand keeps that loop operating long enough to scale while avoiding potentially fatal inhibiting loops. Extract finishes growing as the ultimate inhibiting loop kicks in, and ideally uses the resources from the first curve to kick off the search for new curves.

The central thesis is that each phase demands its own approach to finance, team size, project management, personnel, technology, risk management, implementation, marketing, and sales—and applying one phase's approach to an idea in another phase kills ideas. In Explore, the risk is that nobody cares and the idea dies for lack of fuel; the goal is to find the growth loop experimentally via rapid experiments, maximum creativity, and conceptual blending, using tiny teams with no dependencies that quickly discard failures, exiting when one experiment works way better than others. In Expand, the risk is failure to scale; the goal is avoiding fatal obstacles while scaling furiously, with intense focus on the next emerging growth bottleneck, throttling growth, discarding non-essential features, and good-enough-for-now scaling, exiting when cause and effect of growth become predictable. In Extract, the risk is inability to sustain; the goal is growth with profit, safely optimizing while growing through small safe experiments, rolling out successes, and optimizing costs, exiting when there is no further return on investment.

The tricky trick of 3X is managing projects in different phases in their matching styles: Extract products pay the bills and fund a portfolio of Explore projects, and when a project hits Expand it should be treated as a priority even over profitable Extract activities—simple to say and apparently nearly impossible to execute. Beck closes by asserting that most teams don't have a strategy problem but an adaptation problem.

- Growth follows an S curve driven by a reinforcing growth loop early and an inhibiting loop later; each phase needs a distinct approach.
- Explore finds a new growth loop through rapid experiments and tiny teams; Expand scales furiously while avoiding fatal bottlenecks; Extract optimizes for profitable growth.
- Applying one phase's approach (finance, team size, tech, risk, etc.) to an idea in another phase kills the idea.
- Fund Explore projects with Extract profits, and prioritize Expand projects even over profitable Extract work.
- Most teams have an adaptation problem, not a strategy problem.
