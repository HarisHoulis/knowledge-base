# Programming as Problem-Solving and Complexity Control

Carson Gross argues that computer programming is fundamentally about two things: problem-solving using computers, and learning to control complexity while solving those problems. This framing reduces the discipline to its durable core rather than any particular language, framework, or toolchain.

From that definition, Gross concludes that programming will remain a viable career even with the advent of AI tools. His reasoning is that knowing how to solve problems with computers and how to control the complexity of those solutions is unlikely to become less valuable than it is today.

The quote is short and makes no specific claims about which AI tools exist or how they will be adopted; its argument rests entirely on the persistence of problem-solving and complexity management as the essential skills of the field.

- Programming is defined by two fundamentals: solving problems with computers and controlling the complexity of those solutions.
- Gross cannot imagine a future where those skills are less valuable than they are today.
- He therefore expects programming to remain a viable career despite AI tools.
