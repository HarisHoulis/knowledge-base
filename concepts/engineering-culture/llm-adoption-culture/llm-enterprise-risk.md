# Fragments: July 21

In his July 21 fragment, Martin Fowler wraps up notes from the Future of Software Development Retreat, pointing to the Thoughtworks report's five headline findings: verification is the new bottleneck, harness engineering is emerging, an apprenticeship crisis looms, the executive/engineer expectation gap is a major risk, and legacy modernization offers near-term value. He underscores the growing disconnect between board-level enthusiasm for LLMs and engineer concerns, illustrated by a cautionary tale where ML-optimized air filter replacement in the wrong climate led to fires and a $100 billion loss, emphasizing the need for rapid feedback and contextual awareness.

- The Thoughtworks retreat report identifies code generation as no longer the bottleneck; verification is now the critical challenge.
- A significant board-engineer divide exists on AI adoption, with boards focused on productivity gains and engineers worried about risks like security and context misapplication.
- Citizen developer 'vibe coding' creates shadow IT and security vulnerabilities, requiring governance controls and separate infrastructure.
- LLMs are helpful for operations, but agents overestimated for incident resolution; they can insert unrequested features and fail at complex, adaptive work.
- Writers should reject LLM-generated prose to preserve human voice, with 'Say Your Writing' as a key technique to combat 'LLM-speak'.
