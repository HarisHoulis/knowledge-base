# Citizens Build, Agents Execute, Experts Govern

The article observes a growing gap between what non-technical people and professional engineers think software engineering is. Non-technical executives often build functional apps over a weekend with AI and ask why engineering teams aren't ten times faster (Laycock). The author argues that while this is exciting and expands who can create software, building a working application is very different from running software in a production enterprise environment, where concerns like data protection, dependency failure, maintainability, audit survivability, scalability, and operational visibility become critical (Laycock).

- AI enables non-engineers to build working software, but this is fundamentally different from delivering production-grade enterprise software.
- Enterprise concerns such as security, resilience, operability, compliance, and scalability only surface when software is actually deployed and depended upon.
- Experienced engineers become more leveraged by shifting from building everything themselves to governing and designing environments for safe, high-velocity creation.
- The future organization is one where citizens build, agents execute, and experts govern—not one where engineers are replaced.
- Good design and engineering judgment matter more, not less, when agents can generate large amounts of code quickly.
