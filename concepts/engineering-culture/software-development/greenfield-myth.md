# There is no such thing as greenfield

Matt Pocock argues that the distinction between greenfield and brownfield codebases is largely meaningless in modern software development. He points out that even new projects are constrained by real-world factors like existing company conventions, deployment infrastructure, and team workflows, making them more like brownfield projects than idealistic greenfield ones. Furthermore, documentation design and legacy integration affect both types of projects equally, so developers should not assume a fresh start is unencumbered. Pocock emphasizes that acknowledging these constraints early leads to better planning and more honest engineering culture.

- The greenfield/brownfield dichotomy oversimplifies real-world software development.
- New projects inherit conventions and constraints from the surrounding organization.
- Documentation design and integration complexity matter regardless of project age.
- Recognizing this reality helps teams avoid unrealistic expectations and plan better.
