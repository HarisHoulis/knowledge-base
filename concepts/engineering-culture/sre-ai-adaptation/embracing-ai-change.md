# My (hypothetical) SRECon26 keynote

Charity Majors reflects on how her views on generative AI have shifted in the year since her SRECon25 keynote. She argues that AI is no longer just another integration to manage but a fundamental shift in software development, and SREs should 'swim out to meet it' instead of being begrudging or cynical. She quotes Adam Jacob, who advises engineers and ops folks to start working in the new AI-centric way as much as possible to avoid high career risk as laggards.

- Generative AI is a durable technology shift; SREs should proactively engage with it rather than take a reflexive antagonistic stance.
- The advice to 'swim out to meet it' means seeking out ways to work with AI now, even on personal projects, to stay on the frontier of change.
- Know your cognitive biases and lean against them: pessimists should seek wonder, optimists should heed cautionary tales.
- SREs' outcome orientation is excellent preparation for a world where the 'how' of software matters less than whether it works and what it achieves.
- Extraordinary claims like AGI still require extraordinary evidence; the future is uncertain and not predetermined.
