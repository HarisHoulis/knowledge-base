# The Socio-Technical Staff+ Engineer: Architecture, Culture and Organisational Change

Vanessa Formicola argues that technical leaders should move beyond "wishful architecture" by practicing holistic socio-technical engineering. Rather than treating architecture as a purely technical exercise, she frames the staff-plus role as designing the conditions for technology to thrive, not merely picking tools.

Her central claim is that engineering leaders must account for non-technical forces that shape whether architecture succeeds: organizational maturity, reward structures, the geopolitical climate, and team dynamics. These factors determine how technology is adopted and sustained, so ignoring them produces plans that look good on paper but fail in practice.

This reframing positions the staff-plus engineer as a designer of organizational context as much as of systems. The talk emphasizes that culture and organizational change are legitimate parts of the architecture problem, not distractions from it.

- Move beyond "wishful architecture" toward holistic socio-technical engineering
- Account for organizational maturity, reward structures, geopolitical climate, and team dynamics
- Frame the staff-plus role as designing conditions for technology to thrive, not just picking tools
- Treat culture and organizational change as part of the architecture problem
