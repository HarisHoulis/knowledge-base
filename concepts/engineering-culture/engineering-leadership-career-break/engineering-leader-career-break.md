# Headed for the Exit: the Great Engineering Leader Career Break

The article reports an unprecedented wave of engineering leaders—CTOs, VPs of Engineering, and heads of engineering—opting for prolonged career breaks or quitting without a next role lined up. Based on conversations with nearly 20 such leaders, the author identifies ten common reasons: jobs getting worse, startups losing value, pressure to become AI-native, watching predecessors exit, long hours (rarely decisive), smaller teams reducing need for leaders, preference for fractional CTO work, AI startups paying ICs more than executives, launching one's own business, and burnout. A striking finding is that 6 out of 10 CTO-level leaders surveyed say they are on the way out.

- Unrealistic AI expectations from founders—such as expecting a CTO to make the company 'AI-native' overnight or cut 20–50% of engineering costs—are a leading cause of leader dissatisfaction.
- Equity often becomes worthless due to liquidation preferences; one CTO with 2% common shares realized that a 2x Series A preference meant a $210M exit was needed before common holders saw any money.
- Founder 'slop'—shipping large, low-quality AI-generated PRs—creates accountability, quality, and tech debt problems that leaders are expected to manage.
- Many leaders are quitting to gain hands-on AI experience, because staying in role may make their skills obsolete; Charity Majors advises getting AI on your resume even if it means taking an IC role.
- Most VP/CTO roles require change-management skills that many incumbents lack, making the push to AI-native transformation a common failure point and trigger for exit.
