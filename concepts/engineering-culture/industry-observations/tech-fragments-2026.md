# Fragments: August 18

Martin Fowler shares updates from Thoughtworks, including Rachel Laycock's new 'Rachel's Ramblings' blog and XConf Europe 2026 sessions on agentic systems, compliance, sovereign models, data migrations, legacy code, and jam-oriented programming (Fowler, 2026). He also cites Noah Smith's perspective on AI intelligence, quoting François Chollet's metaphor that intelligence is like making a ball rounder, not a tower taller, suggesting diminishing returns. Smith posits that AI could still be valuable by comprehending 'cloud laws' and tacit knowledge that humans cannot easily articulate, making AI 'smarter' in different ways (Fowler, 2026).

Fowler then examines 50+1's design of their 2026 election forecast page, praising their annotated simulation histogram, dot maps that avoid choropleth pitfalls, and thoughtful tabular data design. He also summarizes Alex Stamos's critique of recent US government actions that shut down Anthropic's 'Fable' model, which pushed organizations toward open-weight models like GLM 5.2 and Kimi K3. Stamos argues for keeping open-weight models available for defense and shifting focus from finding bugs to fixing them, integrating AI security checks into the build pipeline (Fowler, 2026).

Finally, Fowler discusses The Economist's briefing on China's all-out AI push and its potential impact on the world's largest workforce, noting the government's concern about job displacement. He ends with a humorous observation on the accelerating half-life of paradigms, from Loop Engineering to Graph Engineering, predicting 'neuro-symbolic engineering' by the end of August (Fowler, 2026).

- Rachel Laycock launched 'Rachel's Ramblings' for quick, imperfect idea-sharing.
- XConf Europe 2026 will feature sessions on agentic systems, compliance, sovereign models, and more.
- AI intelligence may have diminishing returns (Chollet's 'ball rounder' metaphor), but AI can offer value through 'cloud laws' and tacit knowledge.
- 50+1's election forecast page demonstrates effective data visualization, including dot maps and annotated histograms.
- Alex Stamos emphasizes the importance of open-weight models and integrating AI-based security fixes into the continuous delivery pipeline.
- China's AI drive threatens the world's largest workforce, echoing concerns about job displacement.
