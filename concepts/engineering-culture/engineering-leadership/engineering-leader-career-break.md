# Headed for the Exit: the Great Engineering Leader Career Break

The article, based on interviews with nearly 20 engineering leaders, reports an unprecedented wave of CTOs, VPs of Engineering, and similar senior leaders quitting or taking prolonged career breaks, often with no next job lined up. It identifies ten common reasons, including the job deteriorating due to unrealistic founder expectations around AI, the startup losing value or becoming worthless, lack of AI-native experience, and burnout. A key theme is "AI psychosis" among founders, who ship low-quality AI-generated PRs and demand drastic cost cuts, making engineering leadership roles increasingly difficult and low-ROI (source: https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/the-great-engineering-leader-career-break).

Equity is a major factor: many leaders realize their shares may never pay out due to investor preference stacks. For example, a CTO with a 2% equity grant at a company with a 2x liquidation preference calculated the company would need to sell for over $210M for common shareholders to see any money, making the equity effectively worthless. This rational calculation pushes many to leave. Additionally, the rise of "founder mode" and the devaluation of craft and quality in favor of speed have diminished the traditional influence of engineering leaders (source: https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/the-great-engineering-leader-career-break).

To stay relevant, leaders feel they must gain hands-on AI experience, sometimes taking IC roles or joining AI-native companies, as Charity Majors advises. The article suggests that if a current company is not becoming AI-native, staying may pose a career risk. Some leaders prefer fractional CTO work or launching their own businesses, and many are carefully assessing whether their company is a "winner" worth staying with, given the harsh realities of VC funding and equity dilution (source: https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/the-great-engineering-leader-career-break).

- An unprecedented number of senior engineering leaders are quitting or taking career breaks, driven by AI-related pressures, unrealistic founder expectations, and burnout.
- Equity often becomes worthless due to investor preference stacks, making it rational for leaders to leave if they see no realistic path to a large exit.
- Founder "AI psychosis" and hands-on shipping of low-quality code undermine engineering leadership and create toxic dynamics around quality and accountability.
- Many leaders are leaving to gain AI-native experience, sometimes moving to IC roles or joining AI-focused companies, while others prefer fractional CTO work or launching their own ventures.
- If a company doesn't become AI-native, staying may pose a career risk; leaders must weigh their equity upside against the pace of industry change.
