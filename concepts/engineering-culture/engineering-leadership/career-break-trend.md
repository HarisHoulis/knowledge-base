# Headed for the Exit: the Great Engineering Leader Career Break

Gergely Orosz reports on a 2026 trend of senior engineering leaders (CTOs, VPs of Engineering) taking prolonged career breaks or quitting without a lined-up job, based on interviews with ~20 such leaders. He identifies ten common reasons, including deteriorating job conditions, founder 'AI psychosis' leading to unrealistic expectations, and equity becoming worthless due to liquidation preferences. A key insight is that the CTO/VPE role has become 'low ROI' as AI-driven founder slop erodes quality and accountability, while companies either race to become AI-native or risk becoming irrelevant. Orosz explains how equity math makes staying unattractive: with preferences like 2x, common shareholders may see zero exit proceeds unless the company sells for far above its raised capital, as in the example of a startup raising $110M but needing a $210M+ exit for common shareholders to profit.

- Founder 'AI psychosis' and slop PRs make the CTO/VPE role unpredictable and low-ROI.
- Equity in VC-funded startups is often worthless to common shareholders due to liquidation preference stacks (e.g., 2x preferences).
- Many leaders quit to gain hands-on AI experience, as staying put risks becoming irrelevant in their careers.
- Only a minority of companies will truly become AI-native; most VPEs lack the change-management skills to pull it off.
- The trend is significant: 6 out of 10 surveyed senior leaders are considering or have already taken a career break.
