# Headed for the Exit: the Great Engineering Leader Career Break

According to Orosz (2026), engineering leaders are leaving their roles at an unprecedented rate. Through interviews with nearly 20 CTOs, VPs of Engineering, and other leaders, the article identifies ten common reasons for taking a break or quitting without a next job. Chief among them are deteriorating job conditions driven by unrealistic founder expectations around AI, the threat of AI-native startups making equity worthless, and the desire to gain relevant AI experience. Founder behavior, such as shipping massive AI-generated pull requests, creates tech debt and quality issues while eroding the leader's authority.

A significant factor is financial: many leaders hold equity that is unlikely to pay off due to liquidation preferences, making the risk of staying high. Additionally, leaders feel pressured to become AI-native, but most organizations are not set up for that transformation, and many VPEs/CTOs lack the change-management skills to pull it off. This leads to a deadlock where leaders either burn out or exit to seek roles at AI-native companies or start their own ventures.

- Engineering leaders are increasingly quitting or taking breaks due to worsening job conditions, AI pressure, and worthless equity.
- Founder 'AI psychosis' and hands-on shipping of AI slop create tech debt and quality issues, making CTO/VPE roles harder.
- Equity in VC-backed startups often holds little value due to liquidation preferences; leaders may see no upside.
- Many leaders feel compelled to quit to gain AI experience and stay relevant, even considering IC roles.
- Most VPEs/CTOs are not equipped to lead large-scale AI transformations, leading to a deadlock.
