# Fragments: July 6

Martin Fowler reports on the second Future of Software Development Retreat in Europe, noting a marked shift from the hesitancy of the first retreat to confidence: participants are now shipping agentic development in production, not just discussing hypotheticals. Giles Edwards-Alexander observed "the evidence is in," and Greg Herlein similarly noted that "everybody in the room was doing it. Shipping it. Not slides - production." New topics at the retreat included harness engineering and the rising cost of tokens, reflecting how quickly the field is evolving.

- Agentic software development has moved from aspiration to production, with retreat attendees actively shipping real systems.
- Architecture and design remain important; the Genie's understanding of code bases parallels human understanding, so modularity and naming matter more than ever.
- Good design serves as a hedge against AI dependence, given uncertainties around model availability and cost.
- Token costs are becoming a major concern, with companies like Citi, Amazon, and Accenture throttling AI usage or seeking ways to reduce expenses.
- Ethical engagement with AI is preferable to purity; as Charity Majors argues, showing care means showing up and building, not withdrawing.
