# Fragments: July 6

Martin Fowler reports from Thoughtworks' second Future of Software Development Retreat in Europe, noting a shift from the hesitant exploration of the first retreat to a confident sense that the value of agentic development is now evidence-based. Participants are shipping production systems, not just discussing possibilities. Fowler highlights the rapid emergence of new practices such as 'harness engineering' and growing concerns about token costs, which were barely on the radar at the previous event (Fowler, 2026).

- The retreat moved from aspirational discussion to real-world production adoption of agentic development.
- Architecture and design remain important because AI agents use the same code structures as humans; good modularity and naming help both.
- Token costs have become a major operational concern, with companies throttling AI usage and seeking ways to reduce spending.
- Engaging with AI ethically—rather than renouncing it—is seen as a more practical way to drive positive change.
- Historical perspective, such as reading the Declaration of Independence, can help make sense of current technological transformations.
