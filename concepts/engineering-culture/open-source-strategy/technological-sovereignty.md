# Technological Sovereignty Requires Choice, Skills, and Support, Panelists Say During OSS EU

A panel at OSS EU argued that European organisations can strengthen technological sovereignty by building credible alternatives and acquiring the necessary skills, rather than attempting to avoid global providers entirely. The discussion framed sovereignty as a matter of practical choice rather than isolationism.

Panelists stressed the importance of understanding dependencies across cloud and hardware, noting that effective operational sovereignty depends on making informed, practical software choices. The takeaway is that sovereignty is achieved through capability and awareness, not blanket avoidance of external providers.

- European organisations can enhance technological sovereignty by developing credible alternatives and skills rather than avoiding global providers.
- Understanding dependencies in cloud and hardware is essential to sovereignty.
- Effective operational sovereignty requires practical choices in software.
