# Trust Is the Binding Constraint

Kent Beck argues that teams accumulate code faster than they accumulate trust, and that this gap is where projects fail. Trust builds slowly and disappears instantly, and most of the work that creates it is invisible. AI has accelerated the visible output—code—while the practices that manufacture trust (collaboration, testing, review, shared understanding) get sacrificed for solo speed.

He frames trustworthy software in economic terms: it lets customers spend less time guarding against risk and more time using the product. The XP practices—testing, pairing, continuous integration, planning, customer on the team, continuous deployment, refactoring, observability—are presented not as a checklist but as a system that manufactures trust, underpinned by principles like humanity, mutual benefit, improvement, flow, and redundancy.

The part he most wants to convey is trust-optimized AI-assisted development. He cites a "tweak paradox": trustworthiness of code falls with the square of the size of changes, so AI's preference for big batches conflicts with trust's need for small verified steps. He warns against being pushed into solo (single-player) AI development and argues that slowing down in the right places is how you go faster. The post also announces a live ByteByteGo class on these themes.
