# Headed for the Exit: the Great Engineering Leader Career Break

In a newsletter article, Gergely Orosz reports a growing trend of engineering leaders—CTOs, VPs of Engineering, and heads of engineering—opting for prolonged career breaks or quitting without a next job lined up. Based on conversations with nearly 20 leaders, the article identifies ten common reasons for leaving, including the job getting worse due to unrealistic AI expectations, startup equity becoming worthless, feeling left behind on AI skills, burnout, and a preference for fractional CTO work. The author notes that six out of ten CTO-level leaders he spoke to are considering or already taking a break, marking an unprecedented shift in the industry.

- Many engineering leaders are quitting or taking breaks due to unrealistic AI expectations, equity becoming worthless,, and burnout.
- Founder 'AI psychosis' leads to quality degradation and tech debt, making leadership roles harder and lower in ROI.
- Equity in VC-funded startups is often worth less than expected due to liquidation preferences: a 2% stake may yield nothing unless the company exits above investor preference stacks.
- Leaders feel they must gain AI experience to stay relevant, sometimes needing to step down or take an IC role to do so.
- The trend is widespread: 6 out of 10 CTO-level leaders considered or took a career break in 2026.
