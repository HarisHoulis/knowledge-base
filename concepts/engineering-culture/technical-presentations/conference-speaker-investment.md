# The Conference Speaker Investment

The article argues that conference speaking is an investment of time, energy, and knowledge, and that presentation and conference quality can be measured by this investment. Speakers should carefully select conferences based on factors like audience size and topic focus; a small, specialized conference demands deeper expertise, while a large generalist event allows broader appeal. Additionally, recording expands the potential audience by 1000x, so the venue's recording quality and technical facilities are essential criteria when choosing where to speak. The author cites Devoxx as an example of exceptional recording and venue quality, noting that poorly planned rooms or equipment can harm otherwise strong presentations. [The Conference Speaker Investment](https://jakewharton.com/the-conference-speaker-investment/)

- Speaking is an investment; choose conferences aligned with your expertise and audience expectations.
- Prioritize venues with professional recording, large screens, and good audio/video facilities to expand reach.
- Slides should be widescreen 1080p, use large fonts and simple layouts, and avoid starting with introductions or outlines.
- Visualizations and code should be large, animated gradually, and use syntax highlighting; desaturation helps draw attention to changes.
- Publish slides separately (e.g., on SpeakerDeck) for high-quality review, and prepare exports carefully.
