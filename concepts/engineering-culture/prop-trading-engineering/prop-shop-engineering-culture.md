# Software engineering at a proprietary trading company: Optiver

This article from The Pragmatic Engineer explores how software engineering works at Optiver, a proprietary trading firm. Unlike typical tech companies, Optiver has no external customers—its own trading business is the customer. The primary engineering driver is minimizing latency, described as 'enemy number one,' which leads to custom hardware, kernel-level optimizations, and even co-located data centers with microwave links. The article notes that while ultra-low latency was once a competitive advantage, today it is just the floor, and AI models are becoming the differentiator for trade decisions (Orosz, 2026).

Optiver's engineering culture emphasizes risk caution due to historical industry failures like Knight Capital's $440M bug loss. The company is also undergoing a shift from regional systems to global platforms, investing heavily in platform engineering to reduce fragmentation and duplication. The deep dive covers the tech stack, hardware engineering, network infrastructure, engineering practices, AI adoption, and hiring culture, providing a comprehensive view of a niche but lucrative engineering environment (Orosz, 2026).

- Proprietary trading firms like Optiver have no external customers; the business itself is the customer, shaping a unique engineering culture.
- Latency minimization is the top priority, driving custom hardware, FPGA development, and low-latency software stacks.
- AI models are becoming a differentiator over pure latency, shifting focus to model quality and real-time decision-making.
- Risk caution is paramount; a single bug can be catastrophic, as seen with Knight Capital's $440M loss.
- Optiver is transitioning from regional systems to global platforms to address fragmentation and duplication.
