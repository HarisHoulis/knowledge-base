# Fragments: May 27

Martin Fowler's May 27 fragments reflect on the state of LLM-augmented programming and AI agent workflows. He opens with his GOTO Copenhagen 2025 conversation with Kent Beck, where they shared experiences with AI-assisted development and expressed frustration that decades-old software principles still need repeating, while noting that any manifesto reunion should be led by a younger generation (Fowler, 2026).

- Establish characterization tests, static analysis, and architectural patterns before delegating more control to AI agents.
- Shift from 'in-the-loop' micromanagement to 'on-the-loop' curation to gain real leverage from AI coding agents.
- Agentic coding increases decision density, causing cognitive fatigue; keep tasks small and avoid parallelizing human attention.
- Closing open source repositories as a security measure against LLMs is ineffective and reduces scrutiny.
- AI's impact on graduate employment is emerging, with more AI-exposed fields seeing larger drops in hiring rates.
