# Headed for the Exit: The Great Engineering Leader Career Break

The article, by Gergely Orosz, reports on an unprecedented trend of engineering leaders—CTOs, VPs of Engineering, and heads of engineering—opting out of high-status roles, often without a next job lined up. Based on interviews with nearly 20 leaders, it identifies ten common reasons for quitting, including the job worsening, startup value declining, feeling left behind in AI adoption, and burnout. A key theme is the rise of 'AI psychosis' among founders, who ship large, low-quality PRs and set unrealistic expectations, making leadership roles significantly harder.

- 6 out of 10 engineering leaders interviewed are considering or taking a career break, a pattern not seen in the past 20 years.
- The most common driver is unrealistic founder expectations around AI, such as demanding 'magical' AI transformation or slashing engineering costs by 20-50%, often leading to founder-shipped slop and quality degradation.
- Equity is a major factor: many leaders hold options that are likely worthless due to liquidation preferences (e.g., 2x preference) and the difficulty of achieving a large exit, especially when startups are not growing fast enough.
- Leaders are quitting to gain AI experience because staying put risks their careers; some even consider moving to IC roles to become hands-on with AI tools.
- The VPE/CTO role is becoming 'low ROI' due to 'founder mode' and the difficulty of leading AI transformation, with many leaders lacking the change-management skills required to succeed.
- A bright spot: some companies like Ramp, Stripe, and Notion are cited as positive examples of integrating AI into engineering culture, while 'fractional CTO' work is increasingly preferred over full-time roles.
