# Fragments: May 27

Fowler endorses Beth Anders-Beck for Congress in MA-06, emphasizing the need for software expertise in government. The fragments conclude with pithy social media quotes and a critique of tech oligarchy, tying together themes of AI practice, cognitive load, and governance.

- Establishing tests, static analysis, and architectural patterns before letting AI agents work autonomously transforms the developer role from writer to curator, yielding better leverage.
- Agentic coding increases decision density, causing cognitive fatigue; keeping tasks small and limiting parallel agent orchestrations helps manage mental strain.
- AI's economic impact is beginning to show, with graduates in AI-exposed fields seeing larger employment drops, and public backlash is growing among younger generations.
- Regulatory efforts like the cancelled US executive order and NHS repo closures are seen as inadequate or counterproductive, highlighting a lack of technical expertise in government.
- Political leaders with software backgrounds, such as Beth Anders-Beck, are needed to bring necessary expertise to policy decisions.
