# The Augmented Forest: Setting Boundaries for AI Programming Tools

In this GOTO Copenhagen 2026 talk, Kent Beck reflects on the shifting mood around AI among developers over three consecutive years: from excitement, to job anxiety and despair, to a current state he describes as edgy, a little angry, yet active. He frames anger as a message to oneself that it is time to set boundaries—the central theme of the talk—applied to the AI tools (which he calls the "genie") that have reshaped programming. He notes he loves programming with the genie but is also disappointed in it, since it grants wishes but not in the way you expect.

- Beck traces a mood arc across three GOTO talks: AI excitement, then job panic and despair, now edgy anger that signals it's time to set boundaries
- He calls AI the "genie": it fulfills wishes, but not in the way you expected when asking
- He introduces "tool grains"—sharing the prompt/dialogue that created a tool (e.g., a D3 call-graph visualizer) rather than a packaged product, so it adapts to whoever uses it
- He argues today's AI tools and their culture will change within months, and criticizes the Andreessen-style ideal of all-day, four-hours-of-sleep programming as dystopian
