# Modern Enterprise Architecture: Architecting for Outcomes

Simon Rohrer, Head of Enterprise Architecture at Saxo Bank, argues that traditional enterprise architecture must be replaced with a modern approach centered on outcomes. He defines a modern enterprise as sizable (teams of teams), digitally interacting with customers, operating in constant change, and running a heterogeneous mix of old/new, monoliths/distributed systems, vendor and internal systems. Rohrer distills this into an 'ABCDE' framing: Aligning people, value, and technology; Better value sooner, safer, happier; Continuous and conversational; DevOps at enterprise scale; and Evolutionary enterprise architecture (Rohrer, GOTO 2025).

Rohrer grounds this in the historical shift from 1990s waterfall delivery—where months of planning preceded months of coding, testing, and handoffs—to Agile, DevOps, cloud, and systems thinking. Citing the Standish Group chaos report, Scrum's emergence in 1995, and Extreme Programming in 1999, he says 'software ate the world' and product/software development underwent a paradigm shift. Modern enterprise architecture must therefore be continuous, conversational, and evolutionary rather than a one-time blueprint, with the ultimate goal of delivering better value sooner, safer, and happier.

- Modern enterprise architecture aligns people, value, and technology, not just IT strategy.
- The guiding outcomes are 'better value sooner, safer, happier'.
- EA should be continuous, conversational, and evolutionary, mirroring agile and DevOps practices.
- DevOps principles need to be scaled across the entire enterprise.
- The historical shift from phased, throw-it-over-the-wall delivery demands a break with traditional EA.
