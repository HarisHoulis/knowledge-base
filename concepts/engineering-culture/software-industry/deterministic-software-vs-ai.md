# I Spent Two Years Building the Median — Hariprasad Sudharshan

Hariprasad Sudharshan argues that the modern world runs on invisible, deterministic, rule-based software rather than AI. He opens with a thought experiment: if New York's airports, subways, contactless payments, elevators, phones, and Wall Street all froze at once, the only plausible cause would be a software error affecting the entire planet. From commuting to watching a video, from nuclear reactors to the space station, the common factor powering a $125 trillion economy is software — the traditional, reliable kind built by hand for over 70 years, not the AI software most people discuss.

Sudharshan contends that software is the only truly new industry in human history, since navigation, agriculture, construction, and astronomy existed in earlier forms. He traces a cross-feedback loop where assemblers created C, C created Unix, Unix created everything, and then came Kafka, Zookeeper, and Kubernetes — the systems that run the world today. In this picture, the only judgment is human judgment, and it is from human differences that every product advantage arises.

In the AI age, engineers are being helped, guided, or sometimes said to be completely replaced by models he calls "rented." This creates a level playing field: teams that previously could not afford Google- or Meta-level talent can now gain intelligence through APIs. But under the hood, everything around the model — tools, agents, MCP — is the same old deterministic software, perhaps built using models. The end product remains deterministic software that reliably does the same job every time.

AI's role, then, is twofold: accelerate development of deterministic software at the lowest possible cost, and write deterministic software that did not exist before due to budget constraints or overly complex tasks. The bottom line is that we will use a probabilistic mechanism to create reliable deterministic software, supplementing and some would say replacing human judgment — a question he promises to explore further.

- The modern world depends on invisible deterministic software, not AI; a planet-wide freeze would most likely be a software error.
- Software is arguably the only truly new industry in human history, built through a cross-feedback loop from assemblers and C to Unix, Kafka, Zookeeper, and Kubernetes.
- AI models are "rented" and create a level playing field, but the tools, agents, and MCP around them are still deterministic software.
- AI's role is to accelerate creation of deterministic software at lower cost and enable software previously blocked by budget or complexity.
- The core tension: using probabilistic mechanisms to produce reliable deterministic software, supplementing or replacing human judgment.
