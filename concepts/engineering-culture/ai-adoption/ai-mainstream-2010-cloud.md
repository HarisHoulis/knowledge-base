# 2025 was for AI what 2010 was for cloud

Charity Majors draws a direct parallel between the adoption of cloud computing in the late 2000s and the current state of AI in developer tools. She recalls how, at Linden Lab in 2006, cloud services like EC2 and S3 were initially used for experimental projects—offloading garbage collection to S3, rebuilding server images, running tests, and load testing. By 2010, the cloud had shifted from experimental to the mainstream foundation, with datacenters becoming legacy. Majors argues that 2025 marks the same inflection point for AI: it has stopped being satellite or experimental tech and has become a mainstream, foundational technology in developer tools. She acknowledges that this shift comes with a bubble and hype, but insists that froth does not disprove value, just as the cloud's bubble did not negate its transformative potential.

Majors also reflects on her own evolution, from co-delivering a skeptical SREcon keynote eight months earlier to now recognizing AI's central role. In that keynote, she and Fred Hebert urged SREs to engage with AI despite their pessimism, emphasizing that their risk-aware critique must be grounded in expertise to be relevant. She admits that her position has since shifted, and she now sees AI as an exciting time for builders, even while maintaining an SRE's duty to mind the rear guard. The article uses historical analogy to argue that AI, despite its hype, is a fundamental technological shift that professionals should engage with rather than dismiss.

- AI in 2025 is analogous to cloud in 2010: moving from experimental to foundational mainstream technology.
- Early cloud adoption was driven by practical, experimental projects that solved real problems, similar to current AI use cases.
- Hype and bubbles accompany all major tech shifts, but froth does not negate underlying value.
- SREs and pessimists should engage with AI, grounding their critique in expertise to remain relevant and heard.
- Majors' own perspective evolved from skepticism to excitement over the course of eight months.
