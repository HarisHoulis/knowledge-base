# Stop being skeptical about AI for development with Charity Majors

In this episode, Charity Majors recounts how her view on AI evolved from early 2025 skepticism—when she advised engineers to try vibe coding mainly to complain better—to a November 2025 turning point where she recognized AI as a generational shift (Orosz, 2026). She credits improved coding harnesses like Claude Code as much as model quality, and compares AI's impact in 2025 to cloud computing's impact in 2010, arguing that AI will fundamentally change how software is built. She also acknowledges that skeptics had rational reasons, citing past hype cycles such as COBOL and no-code tools.

Majors contends that the key question is not whether engineers will ship code they have not read, but when, and that the industry is transitioning from hand-editing software to generating new code akin to the 'pets to cattle' shift in infrastructure. She shares contrarian views: code review is overrated, and DevOps has failed to make software engineers understand their code in production. Because AI-produced code is non-deterministic, she argues that engineering discipline must shift toward validation, observability, and testing. Her career advice for engineering directors is to get AI experience immediately and consider returning to individual contributor roles for more agency. She also reflects on AI fatigue and advocates for small acts of control, such as Honeycomb's no-AI Wednesdays, and implores both AI enthusiasts and skeptics to honestly share the costs as well as the wins.

- AI's 2025 impact parallels cloud in 2010; coding harnesses (e.g., Claude Code) are as important as model capabilities.
- Skepticism about AI was rational given past hype cycles, but shipping code never read by engineers is now a 'when' not 'if'.
- Code review is overrated compared to deciding what to build and having conversations; validation and observability are the new bottlenecks.
- DevOps has failed to create a true feedback loop connecting developers to production, and AI demands even more engineering discipline.
- Engineering directors should acquire AI experience immediately, consider returning to IC roles for agency, and combat AI fatigue with deliberate small acts.
