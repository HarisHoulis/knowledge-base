# Stop being skeptical about AI for development with Charity Majors

In this podcast episode, Charity Majors, CTO of Honeycomb, shares her evolving perspective on AI in software engineering. She admits that skepticism was rational in 2025, but by late 2025 she saw AI as a generational change, comparing its impact to that of cloud computing in 2010. The turning point was Opus 4.5 and, more importantly, the maturity of coding harnesses like Claude Code, which transformed AI coding from shell scripts into serious infrastructure (Orosz, 2026).

Majors argues that AI is changing the economics of code generation: generating hundreds of function variants is now faster than hand-writing one. She draws a parallel to the 'pets to cattle' shift in infrastructure, predicting that code itself will soon be treated as disposable, with new code generated and verified rather than edited and repaired. However, she emphasizes that non-deterministic systems require more engineering discipline, especially in validation through tests, evals, and conformance testing. She provocatively calls code review 'the least valuable part of what humans add to software engineering,' and claims that 20 years of DevOps failed on the 'software engineers understand production' front (Orosz, 2026).

Majors offers practical advice: engineers and directors should 'run towards the waves' and get AI experience on their resumes, since future job interviews will filter out those without it. She suggests that engineering directors considering a return to IC work should do so now, as agency is higher there. On AI fatigue, she recommends small acts of control, like Honeycomb's 'no AI Wednesdays.' She also urges both AI enthusiasts and skeptics to tell the whole story—including costs—rather than focusing only on wins, and she personally follows a rule: never send a human a message you haven't read in full (Orosz, 2026).

- Skepticism about AI was justified in 2025, but by late 2025 AI became a generational shift comparable to cloud in 2010, driven by mature coding harnesses like Claude Code.
- AI changes the economics of code generation, making it cheaper to regenerate code than to hand-edit it, leading to a 'pets to cattle' transition for software.
- Reliability and verification are the new bottlenecks; non-deterministic AI-generated code demands more engineering discipline, not less.
- Code review is considered overrated by Majors; instead, humans should focus on decisions and conversations, while automated systems validate code.
- Engineers should embrace AI to maintain career relevance, and engineering leaders should regain agency by considering IC roles or taking control through practices like no-AI days.
