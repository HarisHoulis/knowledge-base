# Learning Out Loud: Managing AI Adoption Anxiety in Engineering Teams

Laura Savino's InfoQ presentation addresses the psychological friction senior engineers and architects face when adopting AI tooling. Rather than framing adoption as a purely technical transition, she treats it as an identity shift: experienced engineers built careers on static expertise, and AI disrupts that foundation, generating anxiety and resistance.

Her central prescription is "learning in public"—leaders and senior engineers modeling visible, ongoing learning rather than performing mastery. This lowers anxiety across the team, accelerates fluency with new tools, and helps prevent burnout during what she characterizes as near-existential upheaval.

The talk targets leaders, offering strategies to support teams through the transition, push back on tech zealotry (uncritical hype in either direction), and cultivate an adaptive engineering culture that holds up under rapid change.

- AI adoption creates psychological friction for senior engineers whose identity rests on static expertise
- Learning out loud—modeling visible learning—reduces anxiety and speeds tooling fluency
- Leaders should actively support teams to prevent burnout during rapid change
- Debunk tech zealotry and foster an adaptive engineering culture
