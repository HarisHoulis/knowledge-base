# Stop being skeptical about AI for development with Charity Majors

For engineering leaders, Majors advises running toward AI, getting AI experience on your resume immediately, and considering a return to IC work to regain agency in an anxious time. She recommends small acts of control against AI fatigue, such as Honeycomb's no-AI Wednesdays. She also urges both AI enthusiasts and skeptics to tell the whole story, acknowledging the wins and the costs, and offers a personal rule: never send a message or email to a human that you have not read in full yourself, as anything faster to produce than to read is likely slop (Orosz, 2026).

- Charity Majors' skepticism shifted in November 2025 due to Opus 4.5 and Claude Code, which she now sees as a generational change comparable to cloud computing in 2010.
- AI may lead to a 'pets to cattle' model for code, where generating and verifying new code replaces hand-editing, requiring strong validation systems.
- Majors believes code review is the least valuable human contribution and that DevOps has failed to connect engineers with production; non-deterministic AI code demands more discipline, not less.
- Career advice: engineers and directors should embrace AI now to avoid being filtered out in future interviews, and taking small acts of control like no-AI Wednesdays helps with AI fatigue.
- Both AI skeptics and enthusiasts need to tell the complete story, including the costs of AI adoption, to avoid polarized misunderstandings.
