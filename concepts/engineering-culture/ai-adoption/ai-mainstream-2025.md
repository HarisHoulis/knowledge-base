# 2025 was for AI what 2010 was for cloud

Charity Majors draws a parallel between the state of AI in 2025 and the state of cloud computing in 2010, arguing that AI has shifted from experimental, satellite technology to mainstream, foundational infrastructure—at least within developer tools. She recalls how at Linden Lab, S3 was initially an experimental solution for a data storage problem, but quickly became the center of gravity, with datacenters relegated to legacy status. She suggests the same transition has now happened for AI, moving from conference buzzword to core technology that builders rely on (Majors, 2025).

The author reflects on her own evolution, noting that just eight months prior, she and Fred Hebert had delivered a keynote at SRECon urging SREs to engage with AI rather than dismiss it. They argued that AI is not all hype, and that the realism and risk-awareness of SREs are needed now more than ever to keep AI grounded. Her recent realization, with some shock, is how far her position has shifted since then, now seeing AI as inseparable from the mainstream of engineering (Majors, 2025).

While acknowledging that there is undeniably a bubble—just as there was with cloud and the internet—Majors insists that the presence of froth does not disprove the existence of value. She frames 2025 as the year AI stopped being a satellite and became foundational, at least in developer tools, and expresses excitement about the possibilities this opens for builders. She encourages professional pessimists to engage with AI rather than take potshots from the outside, so their critiques remain relevant and grounded in expertise (Majors, 2025).

- AI crossed from experimental to mainstream foundational technology in developer tools during 2025, mirroring cloud's trajectory in 2010.
- The hype bubble around AI is real, but it does not negate the underlying value and utility of the technology.
- SREs and other technological pessimists should engage with AI actively, not dismiss it, to ensure their risk-focused critiques are relevant and grounded.
- The author's own perspective shifted significantly from initial skepticism to excitement within just eight months.
- It is an exciting time to be a builder, as AI becomes core infrastructure rather than peripheral novelty.
