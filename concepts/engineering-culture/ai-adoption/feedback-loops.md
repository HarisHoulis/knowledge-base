# AI Enthusiasts vs. Skeptics: Bridging the Reality Gap

Charity Majors examines the growing chasm between AI enthusiasts and skeptics in engineering teams. Enthusiasts point to real, discontinuous productivity leaps from AI-assisted development, while skeptics see the hidden costs: degraded reliability, knowledge loss, and cleanup work that lands on others. The author argues that both sides are right, but the problem is structural—wins accrue to the AI user, while costs are borne downstream. She cites Fin's (Intercom) 2x/3x productivity gains as evidence that AI can amplify a high-performing org, but only when paired with existing engineering discipline and fast feedback loops.

The article urges teams to rebuild shared reality by telling the whole story, inviting feedback on unintended consequences, and framing disagreements as engineering problems rather than existential debates. Instead of 'ship without review' vs 'never', ask 'What would it take to feel comfortable?' The author emphasizes that AI amplifies both strengths and dysfunctions (citing the 2025 DORA report), so investing in discipline and closing feedback loops between cause and effect is essential for sustainable AI adoption.

- AI is an amplifier: it magnifies strengths in high-performing orgs and dysfunctions in struggling ones.
- Enthusiasts and skeptics hold valid, legitimate concerns; the asymmetric visibility of wins vs costs creates conflict.
- Fin/Intercom's 3x output in 9 months shows AI can boost productivity, but only with strong engineering discipline and measurement.
- Rebuilding shared reality requires telling the whole story and inviting feedback on downstream cleanup work.
- Productive conversations shift from 'you're wrong' to 'what would it take'—treating AI integration as an engineering problem.
