# Fragments: July 6

Martin Fowler reflects on the second Future of Software Development Retreat, noting a marked shift from the hesitant, aspirational tone of the first event to a confident, production-oriented mindset. Attendees were now actively shipping agentic engineering solutions, and the debate over whether AI changes software engineering is over; instead, the conversation centers on how to implement it effectively. Emerging patterns and practices are being catalogued, but there is still work to establish what works and when.

- The retreat showed a transition from hesitation to confidence; participants are now shipping agentic engineering in production.
- Architecture and design remain important; good modularity and naming help both humans and AI agents understand codebases.
- Token costs are becoming a major concern, leading companies to throttle AI usage and seek ways to control spending.
- Ethical engagement with AI is preferred over renunciation; the focus should be on action and solidarity rather than purity.
- The article also highlights a fraudulent DMCA takedown and a historical perspective on the Declaration of Independence.
