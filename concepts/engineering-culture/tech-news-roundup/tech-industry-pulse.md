# The Pulse: Big implications of US banning Anthropic’s new model, Fable

The article opens with a follow-up on Meta's ongoing engineering culture decline. Non-engineering teams lost more than 10% of their staff, while Integrity teams were already stretched before the cuts and reallocation. Tuesday's deepdive into Meta's engineering org received positive feedback from inside the company, along with helpful clarifications (Orosz, 2026).

The central story concerns the US government's decision to ban Anthropic's new model, Fable, restricting its use to US citizens only. This could make China more influential as other countries and non-US companies turn to capable open models, most of which come from China. This regulatory move may inadvertently boost China's position in the global AI landscape (Orosz, 2026).

SpaceX also makes headlines: Elon Musk became a trillionaire with SpaceX's IPO, while SpaceX acquired Cursor, and Cursor acquired Continue. These moves suggest SpaceX intends to compete head-on against AI giants Anthropic and OpenAI (Orosz, 2026).

The Industry Pulse section covers additional news: Gemini lead quits for OpenAI, Epic Games releases a new open source version control system, Microsoft considers dropping OpenAI for DeepSeek, and research confirms that LLMs amplify existing expertise (Orosz, 2026).

- Meta's engineering org continues to deteriorate, with non-engineering teams losing over 10% staff and Integrity teams overstretched.
- The US government's ban on Anthropic's Fable model, limiting it to US citizens, may increase China's influence via open models.
- Elon Musk became a trillionaire in SpaceX's IPO, and SpaceX acquired Cursor, which itself acquired Continue.
- SpaceX appears poised to compete with Anthropic and OpenAI in AI development.
- Other industry trends: Gemini lead's move to OpenAI, Epic Games' open source VCS, Microsoft's potential switch to DeepSeek, and LLMs amplifying expertise.
