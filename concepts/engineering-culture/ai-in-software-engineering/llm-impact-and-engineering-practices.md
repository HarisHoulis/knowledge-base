# Fragments: June 16

Martin Fowler's June 16 fragments open with Dave Thomas's reflection that LLMs have made programming more enjoyable, not less. Thomas, who originally feared the LLM revolution would strip away the joy of programming, found the opposite: LLMs remove drudgery, speed up feedback loops, revive abandoned projects, and make exploring new technologies easier. Fowler also reports from DDD Europe, where Eric Evans's keynote described his experimentation with LLMs, suggesting Domain-Driven Design remains relevant—and may become even more important—in the LLM era.

- LLMs have made programming more fun for many experienced developers by reducing drudgery and accelerating exploration.
- Chelsea Troy advises being conscious of conversational registers when working with LLMs; switching registers requires a fresh context.
- Charity Majors warns of a growing divide between AI enthusiasts and skeptics, each with valid existential concerns; engineering discipline and honest reporting are essential.
- Simon Willison suggests that rising enterprise pricing for AI coding agents signals product-market fit in the programming domain.
- Mike Masnick argues that decentralization and user control over data are crucial to counter enshittification and digital despotism.
