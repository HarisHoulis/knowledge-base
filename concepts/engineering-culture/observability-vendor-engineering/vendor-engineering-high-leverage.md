# Got opinions on observability? I could use your help (once more, with feeling)

Charity Majors asks for community input to strengthen her writing on observability and buying software, especially from those who use the traditional “three pillars” model. She frames vendor engineering as a subtle but powerful form of deep technical expertise, describing it as one of the highest-leverage activities an individual contributor engineer can do, with executive-level impact on strategy and execution.

- Vendor engineering is a powerful practical application of deep expertise, enabling staff+ engineers to develop executive-level communication, strategy, and execution skills while remaining individual contributors.
- Majors requests specific anecdotes and advice on migrating observability vendors, cutting costs, managing telemetry pipelines, and structuring large-scale tool changes.
- She asks about organizational models: how large observability teams are, who owns observability at scale when there's no dedicated team, and whether companies use service tiers or an OTel strategy.
- She invites opinions on build vs buy, OpenTelemetry adoption barriers, instrumentation cognitive load, tool consolidation vs proliferation, and open source tooling strategies.
- The post includes a note that contributions will be credited by name but not employer, and a deadline of one week from publication.
