# The Pragmatic Engineer AMA

In this AMA episode, Gergely Orosz shares personal stories that shaped The Pragmatic Engineer. He recounts how pandemic-era Uber layoffs led him to leave Big Tech and pursue writing, eventually producing multiple books and the newsletter. He also describes nearly publishing an exposé on Dutch neobank Bunq, but after hearing from an engineer who credited Bunq for sponsoring his visa and enabling his European tech career, Orosz chose not to publish and adopted an editorial policy of writing about what works inside companies rather than what seems broken (Orosz, 2026).

- Orosz's editorial policy is to write about what works inside companies, influenced by a Bunq engineer's positive experience.
- LeetCode-style interviews persist because they self-select candidates willing to tolerate pointless corporate work.
- MCP became an industry standard partly because Anthropic was not yet a dominant lab; a launch today would likely face adoption resistance.
- AI use involves skill-atrophy tradeoffs: Orosz avoids AI in writing to keep his writing sharp, but accepts degradation in hand-coding.
- Investigative journalism is not for Orosz, though his Pollen piece uncovered unpaid salaries and a deliberate $3.2M double charge.
