# Meta’s Self-Inflicted Resignation Wave: Equity Retainers Fail to Stem Engineer Exodus

Meta's recent layoffs and forced reassignments have inadvertently triggered a resignation wave among engineers who were not directly impacted. The article reports that these unaffected engineers are proactively seeking new opportunities, driven by the cultural and psychological fallout of the restructuring. Meta has responded by offering large equity retainers to encourage these employees to stay, but internal signals suggest the strategy is not working, as the retainers are insufficient to offset the erosion of trust and morale.

This event illustrates a broader pattern in big tech: restructuring actions can have cascading effects on the workforce beyond the targeted cuts. Employees who survive layoffs often reassess their loyalty and risk exposure, leading to voluntary attrition that compounds the original headcount reduction. Meta's experience underscores the difficulty of using financial incentives to retain talent once organizational trust has been damaged, and highlights the need for leaders to consider the indirect consequences of their workforce strategies.

- Meta's layoffs and forced reassignments caused even unaffected engineers to seek new jobs.
- Meta offered large equity retainers to keep these engineers, but the tactic appears ineffective.
- The resignation wave demonstrates that restructuring can trigger voluntary attrition beyond the intended cuts.
- Financial incentives are often insufficient to repair damaged trust and morale after layoffs.
