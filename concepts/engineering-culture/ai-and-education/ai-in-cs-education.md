# AI Is Disrupting CS Education, and the Feedback Loop Is Slow

Commenting on a Hacker News discussion titled "AI is destroying my CS degree," Simon Willison expresses sympathy for educators facing the arrival of AI in the classroom. He argues that AI clearly requires changing how students are taught and evaluated, but that after only a few years it remains extremely unclear what effective teaching with these tools actually looks like.

The core difficulty he identifies is the evaluation loop: measuring whether a new educational approach works takes multiple years, since a robust answer only emerges once the current cohort graduates in three to six years. As a result, students effectively become guinea pigs for untested new ways of learning and teaching.

- AI forces educators to rethink both teaching and evaluation methods.
- It is still unclear what effective AI-assisted teaching looks like after only a few years.
- Educational experiments take 3-6 years to evaluate, since results only appear when a cohort graduates.
- Students end up as guinea pigs for untested approaches.
