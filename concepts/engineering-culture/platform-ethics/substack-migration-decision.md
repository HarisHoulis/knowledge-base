# Hello World

Charity Majors explains her decision to stay on Substack after moving from WordPress, despite criticism that the platform supports Nazis. She initially paused her migration after reading LeaveSubstack and consulting with peers, but ultimately decided to stay, citing her need to re-engage with the technical community as a core part of her job. She argues that Substack is not in the same category as explicitly extremist platforms like Gab or Parler, and that maintaining an active presence in the tech mainstream is essential for her work, even if it means using a platform she considers a 'light dove grey.' Majors also highlights practical motivations for the switch, noting that WordPress felt too heavyweight for frequent posting, whereas Substack feels more like writing an email, lowering friction for her to share ideas more regularly. To mitigate ethical concerns, she commits to not accepting money from Substack, encourages readers to use email or RSS, and plans to auto-post to WordPress. She acknowledges that some may disagree and respects those who choose to boycott the platform entirely, but she sees this as a battle she's not willing to fight.

- Majors decided to stay on Substack after considering the ethical arguments, concluding it is not an extremist platform and that her primary job of community engagement overrides her discomfort.
- She plans to become more active on LinkedIn and Substack to return to the tech mainstream, avoiding X if possible.
- Substack reduces posting friction compared to WordPress, making it easier for her to publish frequently.
- She will not accept money from Substack and suggests email/RSS as ways to engage that benefit Substack less.
- She respects conscientious objectors but does not feel she is 'supporting Nazis' by using Substack.
