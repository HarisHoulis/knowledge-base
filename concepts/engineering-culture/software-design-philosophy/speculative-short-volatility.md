# Speculative Short Volatility & Neglectful Short Volatility

Kent Beck frames timing of structure investments through a volatility lens, arguing that the Tidy First worldview seeks a Goldilocks balance: neither too early nor too late. The common bias is investing in structure too late, which he labels neglectful short volatility.

The contrasting error is speculative short volatility—making structure investments too early, before the volatility they hedge against is realized. Beck uses the volatility framing to distinguish these two failure modes of timing.

- Timing of structure investments is a core concern of the Tidy First worldview.
- The usual bias is investing in structure too late (neglectful short volatility).
- Investing too early is speculative short volatility.
- The goal is a Goldilocks balance between too early and too late.
