# Software engineering at a proprietary trading company: Optiver

The article by Gergely Orosz (2026) provides a detailed look at how Optiver, a proprietary trading firm, approaches software engineering. Unlike typical tech companies, Optiver has no external customers; its own trading business is the customer. This shapes a culture focused on minimizing latency, which is considered "enemy number one," and on building bespoke hardware and software stacks to gain a competitive edge (Orosz, 2026). The firm operates in a high-stakes environment where a single bug can be catastrophic, as illustrated by the Knight Capital incident, leading to a cautious yet fast-moving engineering culture (Orosz, 2026).

The engineering organization is divided into three main areas—engineering, research, and trading—with a strong emphasis on platform engineering to support global scalability. Optiver has transitioned from regional systems to global platforms since 2020, aiming to reduce fragmentation and duplication (Orosz, 2026). The tech stack spans software, hardware, and network infrastructure, including custom FPGAs and dedicated fiber optics. While ultra-low latency was once a moat, today AI and machine learning models are becoming key differentiators, and Optiver invests heavily in building better models rather than solely reducing latency (Orosz, 2026).

Engineering practices at Optiver emphasize risk management, testing, and monitoring, with a culture of knowledge sharing. The company is also adopting AI tools, including agentic coding, to improve productivity. The article highlights how prop shops like Optiver are attractive to AI labs due to their infrastructure expertise and custom hardware skills. Overall, the piece reveals a unique engineering environment where speed, caution, and innovation coexist, offering insights for engineers interested in high-performance computing and trading systems (Orosz, 2026).

- Proprietary trading firms like Optiver have no external customers, and the business itself is the sole customer, fostering a culture of personal motivation and technical excellence.
- Latency minimization is a core engineering focus, driving custom hardware, FPGA development, and kernel-level optimizations; however, AI models are becoming a new competitive differentiator.
- The engineering organization is split into engineering, research, and trading roles, with a strong platform engineering team built to support global systems and reduce duplication.
- Risk management is paramount, especially after industry-wide incidents like Knight Capital's $440M loss, leading to cautious engineering practices despite the need for speed.
- Optiver's adoption of AI tools, including agentic coding, and its partnerships with hardware vendors like NVIDIA reflect its continuous evolution and investment in cutting-edge technology.
