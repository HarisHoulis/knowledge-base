# Software engineering at a proprietary trading company: Optiver

Optiver is a proprietary trading firm that trades only its own capital, with no external customers. This unique position means engineering decisions are driven by minimizing latency and gaining a competitive edge, not by external deadlines or customer feature requests. The company operates in a world where nanoseconds matter, and its fastest systems operate in the sub-nanosecond realm, making measurement noise itself a challenge. Unlike typical startups or Big Tech, the primary 'customer' is the business itself, and the culture values personal motivation to improve over external pressure (Source: https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/optiver).

- Optiver has no external customers; the business itself is the customer, leading to different incentives and a focus on internal motivation.
- Latency is the 'enemy number one,' but ultra-low latency is no longer a sufficient competitive moat; focus has shifted to AI models and trading strategies.
- The engineering organization has moved from regional 'unblock yourself' systems to global platforms, increasing investment in platform engineering.
- Hardware engineering, including custom FPGAs and custom hardware, is a key differentiator, with partnerships like AMD.
- AI labs like Anthropic and OpenAI recruit from prop shops due to their infra expertise and custom high-performance hardware skills.
