# Bring Back Ops Pride

Charity Majors argues that the term "ops" has been unfairly degraded in software engineering, despite operations being a vital and technically demanding function. She contends that the distinction between dev and ops is not about coding ability—since everyone writes software in 2026—but about separation of concerns: devs focus on building features and generating revenue, while ops focuses on protecting the resilience, scalability, and efficiency of the systems that support those features (Charity Majors, "Bring Back Ops Pride," https://charity.wtf/p/bring-back-ops-pride).

- Ops is not toil; it means getting things done as efficiently as possible, and it is a critical function in every industry.
- The dev/ops split is about separation of concerns, not about who can write code.
- Operations engineering absorbs the toughest technical problems, making product development simpler, and cost is a first-class citizen in ops decisions.
- Platform engineering is functionally ops, and renaming teams doesn't improve outcomes—naming and claiming the work does.
- Reclaiming "ops" as a term of pride is essential to building operational excellence and avoiding the feedback loop where engineers flee ops roles.
