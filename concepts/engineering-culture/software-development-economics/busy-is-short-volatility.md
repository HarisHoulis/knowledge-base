# Busy is Short Volatility

Kent Beck extends his series on volatility in software development by framing constant busyness as a hidden way of going short volatility. Drawing on queueing theory, he introduces the Kingman Formula as the counter-intuitive mathematical basis for why being perpetually busy is risky rather than productive.

The piece builds on earlier discussions of how volatility shows up in software work and what it means to go short or long volatility, positioning busyness as a bet against variability that carries hidden costs.

- Being constantly busy is a hidden form of going short volatility in software development.
- The Kingman Formula from queueing theory provides the mathematical basis for why busyness is risky.
- The argument continues a series framing software development decisions in terms of short vs. long volatility.
