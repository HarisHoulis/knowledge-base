# Climbing Inside the Machine: Play, Storytelling, and Computer Science Education

Linda Liukas, a children's book author, illustrator, and playground designer, argues that technology should be made tangible and understandable for six-year-olds and adults alike. Revisiting ideas she presented at GOTO nearly a decade earlier, she notes that while the industry has changed dramatically, much has stayed the same—and the big ideas of computer science remain surprisingly similar to those behind the first computers built about 80 years ago.

- Code is a language: teach poetry alongside grammar, not just syntax
- Playgrounds teach collaboration, conflict resolution, and imagination—qualities worth investing in
- Play's iterative, exploratory, failure-resistant nature matters more in the age of AI optimization
- Stories, not transmission, are how knowledge is actually learned
