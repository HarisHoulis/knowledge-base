# The Pulse: Meta’s self-inflicted resignation-wave

Gergely Orosz's article in The Pulse discusses how Meta's layoffs and forced reassignments have triggered a 'resignation-wave' among engineers who were not directly impacted. These engineers, seeing the instability and lack of control, started looking for new jobs. Meta attempted to counter this by offering large equity retainers, but according to Orosz, these measures are not effectively retaining talent (Orosz, The Pulse). The article notes this was a predictable outcome of the company's restructuring approach.

The same newsletter also covers Grok Bot, an AI harness built by the Cursor team. Orosz describes it as the 'OpenClaw moment' for managed AI agents, offering a 'Codex experience, but for knowledge work.' He personally tested it and automated daily workflows, expressing enthusiasm for the product. He predicts more AI vendors will copy this harness, highlighting a trend in the AI workflows space (Orosz, The Pulse).

- Meta's layoffs and forced reassignments inadvertently prompted unaffected engineers to seek new jobs, creating a resignation wave.
- Meta's equity retainers are failing to keep employees, likely due to eroded trust and perceived instability.
- Grok Bot from the Cursor team is a generic AI harness for knowledge work, reminiscent of the Codex experience.
- Orosz praises Grok Bot for automating workflows and expects it to be widely imitated by AI vendors.
