# Headed for the Exit: the Great Engineering Leader Career Break

The article reports an unprecedented number of engineering leaders (CTOs, VPs of Engineering, heads of engineering) choosing to quit or take extended breaks, often without a next role lined up. Based on interviews with ~20 leaders, it identifies ten common reasons, including the job getting worse due to AI pressures, startups losing value, equity turning worthless, and burnout (Orosz, 2026).

- A record number of engineering leaders are quitting or taking career breaks, driven by AI-related pressures and diminishing returns.
- Unrealistic founder expectations about AI ("AI psychosis") are degrading leadership jobs, with founder-shipped code and tech debt becoming common issues.
- Equity compensation often isn't worth staying for due to liquidation preferences, making exits rational when a large acquisition or IPO is unlikely.
- Many leaders believe they need to exit to gain AI-native experience and stay relevant, sometimes even taking IC roles to get hands-on.
- Companies like Ramp, Stripe, and Notion are cited as better at integrating AI into engineering culture, while many others are not.
