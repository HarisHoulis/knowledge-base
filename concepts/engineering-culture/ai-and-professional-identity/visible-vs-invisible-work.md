# Mathematicians, Here's a Way To Think About Your Existential Crisis

Kent Beck draws a parallel between programmers' recent AI-driven identity crisis and the one now facing mathematicians: both fields have a visible part (features in programming, proofs in math) and a large invisible part (understanding, education, simplification, enabling abstractions). If only the visible part is worked on, progress on it slows to a crawl, yet nobody gets credit for the invisible work, so it depends on an ethos of work to get done. Beck calls this hidden dimension "futures" (or "optionality"), and notes it is the inverse of what programming calls technical debt, coined by Ward Cunningham—sometimes you must pay down debt so interest payments are low enough to resume progress on the principal.

Beck speculates that mathematicians' angst comes from three things combined: the genie (AI) is very good at the visible stuff; without invisible work, visible progress eventually stalls regardless of the genie; and nobody gets credit for invisible work. Together these produce a world where mathematicians no longer get credit—machines do the visible part better, and the invisible part is invisible.

For programmers, Beck offers a constructive response to the identity crisis: be the person who keeps the genie on course. With the genie's help you learn faster, your honed intuition still lets you quickly stop useless directions, and your reach is extended—your work changes, but your strategic decisions become more valuable because they come more frequently. He visualizes this as taking breaks between visible progress to make invisible progress, and hopes the framing helps mathematicians use the new tools without losing their reason for being mathematicians.

- Both programming and math have a visible part (features, proofs) and a large invisible part (understanding, simplification, enabling abstractions) that gets no credit but is required for sustained visible progress.
- Beck calls this hidden dimension "futures" or "optionality"; it is the inverse of technical debt, and debt sometimes must be paid down to resume progress on the principal.
- Mathematicians' angst stems from AI excelling at visible work, invisible work being uncredited, and visible progress stalling without it.
- A constructive programmer response is to keep the genie on course: use AI to learn faster and apply honed intuition to cut useless directions, making strategic decisions more valuable.
