# In defense of AI mandates

Charity Majors defends the technology mandate as a legitimate management tool when used properly. She argues that a mandate is a funding mechanism that signals organizational commitment, allowing employees to prioritize learning and accept temporary slowdowns. Without a mandate, leaders effectively tell employees that AI skills are not a priority and provide no cover for deadlines or quality to slip, adding pressure and stress instead of clarity.

Majors acknowledges that mandates can be stupid and punitive, but they are necessary when time is short and change is existential. She emphasizes the need for consistency: if AI is existential, leaders must fund and support the transition; if it's a nice-to-have, they shouldn't later blame employees for not building expertise. She also warns that forcing through a change should be a last resort, and leaders must be right about the direction, as resentment can last a lifetime if they are wrong.

- A mandate is a funding mechanism that signals organizational priority and provides cover for learning tradeoffs.
- Without a mandate, employees are left to build skills on their own time, which communicates that AI is not a priority.
- Leaders must be consistent: if AI is existential, they must allocate resources and time; otherwise, don't blame employees later.
- Forcing a change should be the last resort, and leaders must be right or face lasting resentment.
