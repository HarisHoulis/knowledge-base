# How Kent Beck shapes the software engineering industry

In a wide-ranging interview on the Pragmatic Engineer podcast, Kent Beck reflects on his six-decade career and the evolution of software engineering. He argues that coding is only a small part of the craft; the rest involves building confidence, making connections, and understanding people. This human element, he says, is the 'biggest cosmic, practical joke' because he was promised that mastering computers would be enough, only to discover that understanding people is what truly gates impact (Orosz, 2026).

- Coding is a small part of software engineering; communication and understanding people are essential skills that are often overlooked.
- Kent Beck did not invent TDD so much as rediscover it from an old tape-to-tape programming book; writing tests first eliminated his programming anxiety.
- He created Extreme Programming and co-authored the Agile Manifesto, though he now calls 'agile' a mistake because the term is vague and easily co-opted.
- At Facebook, he discovered TDD was not used at all, prompting him to unlearn and relearn engineering in a company that scaled without heavy testing.
- Beck's '3X' model—explore, expand, extract—describes the three phases of building software products, each requiring different coding, hiring, and organization approaches.
