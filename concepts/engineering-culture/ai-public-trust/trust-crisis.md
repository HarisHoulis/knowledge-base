# Dario Amodei on AI Trust and Public Perception

In a quote [published on Simon Willison's blog](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Aug/16/dario-amodei/), Dario Amodei argues that the public's negative view of AI is fundamentally a crisis of trust, not a consequence of warnings from AI leaders. He states that people distrust companies, governments, and the tech industry, and that AI is just the latest iteration of this longstanding sentiment. Amodei believes this distrust cannot be fixed with a 'glitzy marketing campaign with a positive spin,' noting that claims like 'AI will cure cancer' are now seen as clichéd and deceptive.

Amodei contends that the only way to win back trust is to actually deliver on the big promises to benefit the world. He acknowledges that AI companies, including Anthropic, have not yet delivered on these promises, and he regards this as 'by far the most accurate criticism' of the industry. He encourages critics to focus on this lack of delivery rather than on messaging or marketing, emphasizing the need for tangible outcomes over rhetoric.

- Public negativity toward AI is driven by a broader crisis of trust in institutions, not primarily by AI leaders' warnings.
- Marketing campaigns with positive spins are ineffective; claims like 'AI will cure cancer' are clichéd and perceived as deceptive.
- Winning trust requires actually delivering on promised benefits to the world.
- Critics should focus on AI companies' failure to deliver on promises, not on messaging or marketing.
