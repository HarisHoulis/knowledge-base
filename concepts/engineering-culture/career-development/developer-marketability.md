# Increase Your Marketability as a Developer

In this post, Kent C. Dodds offers three ideas to increase developer marketability, especially for engineers struggling to find their first full-time job. He notes there is no shortcut to gaining experience, but recommends maximizing its impact by teaching as you learn: consume information, build something with the technology you want to learn, then teach what you learned to solidify it ([source](https://kentcdodds.com/blog/increase-your-marketability)).

He also advises making work public whenever possible, quoting Jeff Atwood's “When in Doubt, Make it Public” and Brad Abrams: “If no one knows you did X, did you really get all the benefits for doing X?” Examples include recording lunch teachings, publishing Slack answers as blog posts, tweeting screenshots, recording conversations with open-source creators, and livestreaming component work. He argues that opportunities come less from what you know or who you know than from “who knows what you can do,” and suggests diversifying where you publicize ([source](https://kentcdodds.com/blog/increase-your-marketability)).

Finally, he emphasizes being kind: nobody wants to work with someone unkind, and even a highly skilled developer may be passed over if they are unkind. He closes by saying that to get enjoyable projects, work, or jobs, you should be kind, make what you do public, and maximize the impact of time invested in experience. He also includes a disclaimer to check your employment contract before making things public ([source](https://kentcdodds.com/blog/increase-your-marketability)).

- Solidify learning by teaching after consuming information and building something.
- Make your work public to increase recognition and opportunities.
- Diversify where you publicize to reach a wider audience.
- Kindness matters for marketability and collaboration.
- Check your employment contract before making work public.
