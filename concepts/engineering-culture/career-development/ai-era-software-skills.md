# How I'd Get Into Software in 2026

Kent C. Dodds argues that although AI coding agents are increasingly capable—joking that "coding is over"—aspiring developers should not give up but instead focus on skills that remain valuable alongside agents. The first is systems thinking: the ability to understand a system's parts, their responsibilities, and how they interact. He suggests practicing by opening an AI agent and mapping out the application systems you already use (e.g., the DoorDash app), asking for visualizations and critically evaluating the agent's points, noting that even closed-source apps work fine since the goal is forming a systemic way of thinking.

- Develop systems thinking by decomposing existing apps into parts, responsibilities, and interactions, using an AI agent to help map and visualize them
- Learn to communicate intent—the 'what' and 'why' rather than the 'how'—including who it's for, the desired result, and constraints
- Encode intent as tests so agents have a mechanism to verify their work against your expectations
- If you don't communicate intent effectively, agents fill gaps with guesses, so notice this before they start guessing
