# Tarn Adams: It's Dwarf Behavior, Not Dwarf AI

In a recent quote, Tarn Adams, co-creator of Dwarf Fortress, humorously recounts how he has been compelled to abandon the term "AI" when discussing the game's dwarves. He explains that due to external pressure, he now refers to it as "dwarf behavior" instead of "dwarf AI," underscoring the semantic reshaping that occurs around contested terminology.

Adams further notes that this behavior is imperfect, admitting that the dwarves "misbehave sometimes." This acknowledgment highlights the emergent and often chaotic nature of the simulation, which, while not a formal AI system in the traditional sense, still produces complex, lifelike actions. The quote sheds light on how language choices in game design and AI research influence public perception and professional practice.

- Tarn Adams no longer uses the term 'AI' to describe dwarf behavior due to external constraints.
- The replacement term is 'dwarf behavior,' which acknowledges the game's emergent, imperfect simulations.
- The quote illustrates broader cultural debates about what qualifies as 'AI' and who controls that language.
- It reflects the intersection of game design, artificial intelligence, and terminology politics.
