# Fragments: July 6

Martin Fowler shares reflections from the second Future of Software Development Retreat in Europe. Unlike the previous event, attendees showed confidence that agentic development's value is proven; participants were shipping production work, not just discussing possibilities. Fowler notes the emergence of pattern libraries and the rapid evolution of terms like 'harness engineering.'

A key debate continues: whether architecture and design still matter. Fowler leans toward the view that The Genie (LLM) benefits from the same clear structures humans use, citing Laura Tacho's phrase that 'the Venn Diagram of Developer Experience and Agent Experience is a circle.' Good modularity and naming help both humans and AI.

The post also highlights growing concerns about token costs, with companies like Accenture throttling AI usage and a 404 Media report showing token bills exploding. Fowler discusses the ethics of AI use, referencing Charity Majors' argument that disengagement is less productive than working to improve systems. Finally, he reflects on Bret Devereaux's analysis of the Declaration of Independence, drawing a parallel to gaining perspective on current affairs.

- Agentic development has shifted from aspiration to production: attendees at the retreat are actively shipping with AI agents.
- Software architecture and design remain critical; AI tools understand codebases similarly to humans, so clarity helps both.
- Token costs are a rising operational concern, leading companies to restrict AI usage and even create cost-cutting plugins.
- Ethical engagement with AI is complex; pragmatic involvement may be more effective than renunciation.
- The article uses historical analysis to emphasize the importance of understanding context in technological and societal shifts.
