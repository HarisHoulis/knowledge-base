# The Pragmatic Engineer Essay Challenge: How AI Is Reshaping Software Engineering

Gergely Orosz presents winners and runners-up from an essay competition on how building software has changed since AI models became reliably good at coding. The winning grand-prize essay was Neil Kakkar's 'How software engineering changed: a tale of two eras', with category winners covering AI-written code, solo builders, and reflections on AI (source).

Anton Zaides, an EM at HoneyBook, describes a rapid shift from classic engineering to 'pure vibe coding' across three repos with differing review rigor. He notes that optional reviews eroded peer pressure to understand code, dropping from two reviewers to zero and producing a buggy, duplicated codebase; he argues EMs must stay hands-on to grasp the new flow, and now ships 20+ PRs a week versus one before (source).

Other essays offer framings: Paul D'Ambra says engineers are becoming 'software machinists' producing machine-made software in volume, predicting changes to CI and review; Kira Howe describes choosing to embrace AI tooling rather than resist; Marc-André Moreau recounts Devolutions going 'AI-native' and completing a three-year migration in one; Carlo Beschi lists 16 changes including tech-debt payoff, prototyping with Claude Design, and role blurring; Bruno Felix frames the shift from 'rowing' to 'steering' (source).

- Optional code review eroded understanding: HoneyBook went from two reviewers to zero, yielding a buggy, duplicated codebase
- Engineering managers are being pushed to stay hands-on to understand AI-driven workflows
- Framings include 'software machinists' (volume production with tooling and sampling) and 'rowing to steering' (managing parallel workstreams)
- Companies report large gains: a three-year migration done in one, more prototyping, and faster tech-debt payoff
- Role blurring: non-engineers and PMs shipping code, while some engineers mourn the loss of hand-crafted code
