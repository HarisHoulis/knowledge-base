# Educating an Architect: Architecture as a Socio-Technical Discipline

In this InfoQ podcast, Michael Stiefel speaks with Luca Mezzalira about software architecture as a socio-technical discipline that balances technological design with an organization's social and managerial context. The central argument is that architectural choices must be adapted to a company's unique culture and team structure rather than relying blindly on universal best practices.

Because organizations differ in culture and team structure, Mezzalira frames the architect's role as one of judgment and adaptation rather than rote application of industry-standard patterns. The discussion also touches on how architects are educated and developed, given that the discipline spans both technical and organizational concerns.

- Software architecture is a socio-technical discipline, not purely a technical one
- Architectural decisions must fit a company's culture and team structure
- Universal best practices should not be applied blindly across organizations
- Architect education must cover organizational and managerial context alongside technology
