# Got opinions on observability? I could use your help (once more, with feeling)

Charity Majors, in a follow-up to her earlier request for advice on buying software, asks the engineering community for their experiences and opinions on observability. She argues that choosing and managing observability tools is a high-leverage engineering activity, affecting every engineer and customer, and that deep technical expertise is essential for vendor engineering. The article enumerates a series of open questions covering topics such as migrating between observability vendors, controlling costs in three-pillar architectures, telemetry pipelines, team structures, build-vs-buy decisions, OpenTelemetry adoption, instrumentation overhead, tool consolidation, and the role of open source tools. She emphasizes that she is seeking input to overcome selection bias in her own network, and asks for email responses with permission to credit contributors by name (without disclosing employers).

- Vendor engineering is a subtle, high-leverage skill that enables staff+ engineers to develop executive-level strategy and communication.
- Observability decisions impact every engineer and customer, making them critical to an organization's success.
- Majors solicits community input on a wide range of observability topics, from migration and cost control to OpenTelemetry adoption and team structures.
- The goal is to inform the second edition of her book and to broaden perspectives beyond the 'three pillars' model.
- Contributors are asked to email answers within a week and may be credited by name without employer affiliation.
