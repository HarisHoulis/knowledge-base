# The Pragmatic Engineer AMA: Insights on AI, Hiring, and Media

In this Ask-Me-Anything episode, Gergely Orosz shares personal stories behind The Pragmatic Engineer. He reveals that the publication might not exist without the COVID-19 pandemic: Uber layoffs led him to leave, finish a book he was writing, and ultimately discover a passion for writing after the book took longer than expected. He also recounts a critical editorial decision: after drafting an exposé on working conditions at Bunq, he chose not to publish it after hearing from an engineer who credited Bunq for sponsoring his visa and enabling his career, which led Orosz to adopt a policy of writing about what works inside companies rather than what seems broken (Orosz, "The Pragmatic Engineer AMA"). 

Orosz also discusses his sole investigative piece on Pollen, which was triggered by the CEO dismissing him as a "nobody." His digging uncovered unpaid salaries, cancelled US health insurance, and a CTO intentionally triggering a $3.2M double charge without a postmortem. The findings were published despite bogus DMCA takedown attempts, and Orosz contributed to a BBC documentary. In the same AMA, he shares opinions on hiring and AI: he believes LeetCode-style interviews persist because they self-select candidates willing to tolerate corporate nonsense, observes that MCP became an industry standard partly because Anthropic was not yet a dominant threat at launch, and warns that AI tools can cause skill atrophy — he personally uses zero AI for writing to keep his skills sharp (Orosz, "The Pragmatic Engineer AMA").

- The Pragmatic Engineer's origin is tied to pandemic layoffs at Uber; Orosz turned to writing after a book project and discovered he liked it.
- Orosz adopted a 'write about what works' editorial policy after a Bunq engineer's counter-narrative prevented a critical exposé.
- An investigative piece on Pollen uncovered unpaid salaries, canceled health insurance, and a deliberate $3.2M double charge; the article remains despite fake DMCA takedowns.
- LeetCode-style interviews likely persist because they filter for candidates who tolerate meaningless work, a trait Big Tech values.
- MCP became a standard because Anthropic wasn't a feared competitor at launch; Google's similar protocol failed due to antitrust concerns.
- AI can cause skill atrophy; Orosz avoids AI in writing to preserve his writing ability.
