# Pushing software engineering limits with “napkin math”

The article profiles Simon Eskildsen, co-founder and CEO of turbopuffer, tracing his path from high-school algorithmic programming competitions to a nearly decade-long infrastructure engineering career at Shopify. It highlights how participating in the International Olympiad for Informatics taught him to write correct, fast, and memory-efficient programs, and to avoid getting stuck in unproductive approaches by starting over when necessary (Orosz, 2026).

- Napkin math uses memorized approximate costs and performance numbers for computer operations to quickly validate or challenge engineering design decisions (Orosz, 2026).
- Long tenure at one company can teach engineers to write software that ages well and to value simple, maintainable solutions over complex multi-team designs (Orosz, 2026).
- Tools like toxiproxy, built at Shopify, simulate network failures and enable robust testing of distributed systems without mocking low-level drivers (Orosz, 2026).
- VC funding is not always necessary; Eskildsen raised only $8M in seed funding and signed Cursor as a customer before building turbopuffer (Orosz, 2026).
