# The Death of Developer Advocates

Stephanie Jarmak argues that the traditional role of developer advocates is dying as AI agents fundamentally change what it means to be a developer. She traces the evolution from 1980s software evangelism, through the 2010s two-way feedback loop of developer advocacy, to 2026 where developers are no longer working alone but orchestrating fleets of agents. This shift demands a new kind of advocate—an 'agent advocate'—who relates not only to human developers but also to AI agents as users of developer tools. (Source: Jarmak, 2026)

Agents are unique in that they are both users and recommenders of tools: they read docs, call APIs, and encounter errors, but they do so differently than humans. Meanwhile, non-engineers with AI fluency are entering the developer ecosystem, as evidenced by Jarmak herself, a former astronomer turned open-source maintainer with 12,000 commits. The talk concludes that DevRel must embrace this broader audience and reposition advocacy to support both human and agent users, ensuring products are discoverable and usable by all. (Source: Jarmak, 2026)

- Developer advocacy has evolved from one-way software evangelism (1980s) to a two-way feedback loop (2010s), but now faces a new disruption from AI agents.
- In 2026, developers act as orchestrators of agent fleets, and non-engineers with AI tools can also become developers and maintainers.
- AI agents are both users and recommenders of developer tools, making them a new persona for DevRel to understand and support.
- The role of developer advocate must transform into 'agent advocate' to remain relevant in the AI-driven era.
