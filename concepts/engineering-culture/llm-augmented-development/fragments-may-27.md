# Fragments: May 27

Fowler also discusses the NHS' decision to close nearly all its open source repositories as a security measure against LLMs, which he considers ineffective, and cites the UK Government Digital Service's position that moving code private does not fix underlying weaknesses. Adam Tornhill's post on 'compressed cognition' is featured, describing the mental cost of agentic coding and the need to keep agent tasks small, avoid parallel human attention, and rely on verification over knowing every line of code. The fragments end with social media quotes, Karl Bode's analysis of Gen Z's anger at AI, Economist articles on jobs and graduates, criticism of stalled US AI regulation, and Fowler's endorsement of Beth Anders-Beck for Congress in MA-06.

- Characterization tests, static analysis, and proper architectural patterns are prerequisites for trusting AI agents to work autonomously; with them, a developer's role shifts from writer to curator.
- Agentic coding increases decision density and causes cognitive fatigue; it is better to keep tasks small and avoid parallelizing human attention across many agents.
- Closing open source repositories to protect against LLM-augmented attackers is an ineffective substitute for secure-by-design development and remediation.
- AI's effect on jobs is still uncertain, but early data suggests graduates in AI-exposed fields are seeing larger employment drops, and the full consequences may only appear during a recession.
- US frontier AI regulation has been delayed or canceled, leaving an 'opaque and essentially lawless' back-channel approach instead of stable rules.
