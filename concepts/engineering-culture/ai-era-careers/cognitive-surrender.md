# From Chrome DevTools to AI Engineering, with Addy Osmani

Addy Osmani, a 14-year Google veteran who worked on Chrome DevTools and Core Web Vitals, shared his journey from building a web browser at age 16 to becoming a director of engineering. His path to Google was unusual: he published free educational materials on frontend and JavaScript development, which caught Google's attention and led to a DevRel-and-builder role on the Chrome team. He discusses how Chrome DevTools evolved from a debugging tool into a quasi-IDE as web development grew more complex, and notes that memory management debugging remains a hard, underdeveloped problem (Orosz, 2026).

- Cognitive surrender is a key risk of AI-assisted development; mitigate by understanding every major decision the LLM makes.
- Aim for mutual amplification with AI agents: have agents log decisions and learnings while you review and internalize them.
- AI models cannot be accountable, so engineers remain accountable for code—similar to designated owners in Chromium.
- Engineering roles are unbundling; developing product sense, go-to-market awareness, and other non-engineering skills is critical.
- Osmani is bullish on software engineering: easier software creation expands the total addressable market for builders.
